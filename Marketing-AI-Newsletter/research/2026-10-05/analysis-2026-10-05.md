## What leads

**News lead:** the open web's price, set outside the courtroom. Wednesday, Judge Mehta dismissed Penske's and Chegg's AI Overviews suits: publishers alleged "an 'expectation'" of traffic, "But an expectation is not an agreement," and the court found it "more plausible" that they were "voluntarily acceding to cost-free crawling"; he pointed them to Congress and regulators. The same week, the parties who control access set their own prices. Google's own contribution pilot has admitted about 100 publishers, and several small and midsize ones told The Information the payments come to about a tenth of a percent of their ad revenue (Tuesday). Reddit is closing its free public API (Wednesday); in TechCrunch's reading, AI assistants "will need commercial deals with Reddit for its data." Cloudflare opened a closed beta that charges agents per request over HTTP 402 (Wednesday). Kint's line names the gap: "someone with authority has to address what is happening."

Stories 2–6: Google's ad tech in two courts (Castel sends Gannett, the Daily Mail and the AdX class toward a jury, trial set for April 5, 2027 per Law360; the Oct 2 joint proposed decree splits on 6 vs 12 months and on US-first timelines) · Safari in iOS 27 blocks The Trade Desk's ad domain, with a report of hundreds more to come (straddle: bug filed Sept 21, reported Sept 29; editor's note on the Oct 9 fix) · Agents get asked for ID at checkout (Shopify's WebMCP checkout with Web Bot Auth, Mastercard's agent-probability score with Skyfire's Know Your Agent, Resy locking out an agent; Muse past 5M with up to half of Meta's daily house-ad impressions) · The review bottleneck (Pantheon: manual validation is marketing's top barrier at 79%; agencies say they've stopped hiring graduates; PwC: access to learning down to 51%) · OpenAI's week (always-on dots, ChatGPT try-on, conversions in the Ads Insights API; GPT-6.1 Astra shelved and an apology to Australia).

## Common-thread candidates
1. **Free was a choice; now there's a price list.** The court read open crawling as the publisher's own choice. That same week Reddit said it will close its free public API, AI assistants included; Cloudflare put a 402 paywall in front of agents; and Google's pilot payouts surfaced (under $1,000 over several months for several small sites, more than $1M a year for one early publisher). The price of being read is being set by whoever runs the gate, one gate at a time.
2. **Agents must show ID; people's IDs get blocked.** Shopify asks agents to sign requests; Mastercard scores whether an agent started a payment; Skyfire sells Know Your Agent; Resy locks out unapproved agents; Reddit ends RSS on Nov 13, citing scraping. The same week, Safari blocked identity-resolution domains for people and caught The Trade Desk's ad server. Identity is moving from the shopper to the software acting for them.
3. **One system spends, models and reports.** Full-Funnel Campaigns pick the channels and model a 12-month return; WPP says DSP users are "less human and more machine"; OpenAI's dots keep working between conversations. Banks's line is the frame, and the advertiser's job becomes the counterfactual.
4. **Review is the bottleneck, and the reviewers weren't hired.** Google now says fact-check "all AI-generated content," titles and alt text included; Pantheon finds review and approval has passed creation as marketing's biggest blocker; agencies say they've stopped hiring graduates; PwC finds access to learning falling. The work AI pushed downstream lands where the junior jobs used to be.
