## Segment 1 — Cold open: the one idea of the week

## Segment 2 — The lead story: Uber's Agentic Pods (give this the most time)

Uber CTO **Praveen Neppalli Naga** published a detailed AI adoption report on July 7. The baseline numbers come from Uber itself — company-reported, with Uber as the only source: 99 percent of Uber engineers use AI tools; more than 70 percent of pull requests are attributed to local or cloud agents; engineers have built more than 2,500 agent skills.

The program to explain visually is **Agentic Pods**: roughly 30 AI-proficient engineers, each paired with a domain expert from a business function — finance, legal, operations, marketing, support, HR, procurement. Each pod gets exactly two weeks. Show the timeline as a graphic:

- Days 1–2: shadow the expert and document the workflow
- Day 3: prioritize opportunities
- Days 4–5: build a working agent beside the expert
- Days 6–9: validate with more people who do the same job
- Day 10: ship

Result: 16 pods across 16 business functions in two months. Show these before/after numbers on screen, one at a time:

- Capital allocation analysis across 150 cities: **15 hours → 30 minutes**
- Financial pacing reports: **two days → 10 minutes**
- Marketing website quality assurance: **two weeks → 50 minutes**
- **9,000** manually created support workflows moved to self-service automation

The key finding, in Naga's own words — show as an on-screen quote attributed to him by name:

> "The workflow becomes the unit of automation — not the individual task."

Narration point: the biggest gains came from redesigning whole workflows — handoffs, approvals, tools, cross-team steps. Speeding up single tasks was the smaller effect. The parallel is visible in AV production work, which crosses intake, scheduling, recording, editing, review, captioning, metadata, publishing, and archiving. State the parallel as an observation only.

## Segment 3 — The same shift in everyday tools: GPT-5.6 goes GA

Keep this segment tight; the story is availability plus one workflow-scale product.

On July 9, OpenAI moved **GPT-5.6** from limited preview to general availability across ChatGPT, Codex, and the API. On-screen: the three tiers — **Sol** (flagship), **Terra** (balanced), **Luna** (lower-cost). One narration line covers the orchestration features: Ultra mode coordinates multiple agents in parallel, Program Calling lets the model write and run coordinating programs, and a multi-agent API capability is entering beta — all aimed at coordinating work rather than answering single prompts.

The workflow angle that carries the segment: OpenAI also introduced **ChatGPT Work**, an agent that stays with a project for hours, works across connected applications and files, and produces finished documents, spreadsheets, slides, and web applications. As an observation, it echoes what Uber's pods hand-built — an agent owning a workflow rather than a task. Sober note: the operational questions are permissions, access, review controls, and records — beyond just benchmarks.

Quick beat: OpenAI also launched **GPT-Live** on July 8 — a full-duplex voice model that listens and speaks at the same time and handles interruptions. GPT-Live-1 is rolling out to Go, Plus, and Pro users; a smaller version becomes the Free default; API access is planned. At launch, GPT-Live is voice-only.

## Segment 4 — Media models become platforms: Runway Dev and Meta's Muse

**Runway Dev**, launched July 8: one enterprise API for Runway's own models alongside third-party models. Narration compresses the catalog to "Runway's own models plus third-party models from ByteDance, OpenAI, and ElevenLabs"; the full list appears on screen only — Runway Gen-4.5, Runway Aleph 2.0, Runway Act-Two, ByteDance Seedance, OpenAI GPT Image 2, ElevenLabs models. Runway's pitch to developers, delivered as reported speech: the company says switching between models takes a one-line code change.

Also worth one screen: the enterprise controls the platform competes on — contractual guarantees that customer data stays out of model training, zero-data-retention support, SOC 2 Type II compliance, IP indemnification, content moderation, and a 99.9 percent uptime commitment. These are vendor claims, straight from Runway's own materials — and they show what media infrastructure now competes on beyond output quality.

**Meta**, July 7: Meta Superintelligence Labs released **Muse Image** (available now) and previewed **Muse Video**. Muse Image follows detailed instructions, edits precisely, composes from multiple references, uses tools such as web search and code execution, and refines its own output before returning it. **Content Seal** is an invisible provenance signal designed to survive cropping, compression, resizing, and screenshots — detector in preview, video support planned.

Credit Meta's candor: Muse Video generates native audio, but Meta itself says audio-video synchronization and physically accurate fast motion still need work.

## Segment 5 — The plumbing layer: LucidLink and AVTensor

Frame: none of the above works without the data layer.

**LucidLink Connect for Frame.io**, announced July 7: presents Frame.io Camera-to-Cloud footage and an organization's other production storage inside one real-time filespace — without moving, migrating, or duplicating files. Original paths and permissions stay intact; editing works in Premiere, DaVinci Resolve, and Avid Media Composer; the system streams only the portions of a file needed for playback and editing. Enterprise accounts for now.

**AVTensor**, open-sourced by Runway on July 10: a Rust media decoder for training pipelines that returns audio and video tensors sharing the same time origin. Runway built it after finding an existing decoder silently misaligned audio and video in training data. Reported numbers for one screen: a 100-batch decoding benchmark went from **176 seconds to 122 seconds**, and model flops utilization improved **1.8 percentage points** in a production training run. Narration callback: synchronization problems in AI video can begin before the model is ever trained — a direct echo of the sync gap Meta disclosed.

## Segment 6 — Close: the pattern and the habit

Restate the through-line over a summary graphic: Uber redesigned workflows; OpenAI productized long-running work; Runway platformized model choice; Meta attached provenance to the pixels; LucidLink and AVTensor fixed the plumbing. **The model is becoming one component in a longer chain of work — and the chain is where the gains are.**

End with this week's habit from the Dispatch's "Back to Basics" series, Week 7, presented as information rather than instruction: an AI learns a desired output format faster from two representative examples than from a long description. The pattern works best when confidential details are removed from the examples first, and when the model is told explicitly to produce entirely new content that follows only the structure and formatting of the examples.
