## 1. Xero paid an influencer to tell small businesses to fire their accountants

**What happened:** Personal-branding strategist Amelia Sordell (77K Instagram followers) posted a Xero-sponsored story (#xero #xeropartner, discount link) saying she'd spent £120,000+ on accountants since 2020 and now connects Xero to Claude to produce the management accounts she'd paid an accountant £800 a month for. Accountants, Xero's actual sales channel, detonated on LinkedIn (Sept 3–5). Xero pulled the post; UK MD Kate Hayward apologized: the ad "does not reflect our values… and it should never have run."

**Primary receipts (LinkedIn):**
- Tom McNally, Sept 3: "Xero paid an influencer to tell small businesses they don't need an accountant. Accountants are furious. I get it, but nobody should be surprised. This is the innovator's dilemma playing out in public… they can see that the money is in the service, not the software. But they can't move into services without turning on the people who built them." https://www.linkedin.com/posts/tomdmcnally_xero-paid-an-influencer-to-tell-small-businesses-activity-7501263484764995585-1c9K
- Lucien Livsey, Sept 4: "Xero just ran an ad telling small businesses to fire their accountants. It didn't land well." https://www.linkedin.com/posts/lucien-livsey-31971a121_xero-just-ran-an-ad-telling-small-businesses-activity-7501583099533651968-sGV0
- Rumana Jeffreys, Sept 4: "Don't create value for one customer group by diminishing the perceived value of another — especially when they've spent years advocating for your brand… The £800 comparison reframes the accountant's fee as the cost of producing a spreadsheet, rather than the cost of judgement, interpretation and advice." https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rumana-jeffreys_xero-has-upset-a-lot-of-accountants-this-activity-7501671417730543616-NO9x

## 2. Suno pulled its Mary J. Blige ad: she never authorized it

**What happened:** An 85-second Suno spot circulated over the weekend (Sept 5–6): Blige in a studio while a producer builds "Pretty Flowers" with Suno's tools, Blige suggesting strings and a beat. Shorter cuts spread; fans read it as an endorsement of generative music. Suno then told Variety: "We entered into a business deal with someone who presented themselves as Ms. Blige's official representative. As soon as we learned this was not the case, and that Ms. Blige was uncomfortable, we terminated the advertising campaign." Blige participated in the filmed session; Suno hasn't named the intermediary or explained how it got to distribution. Suno faces separate copyright litigation from rights holders.

**Coverage:** Consequence (carrying the Variety statement): https://consequence.net/2026/09/suno-pulls-mary-j-blige-ad-says-she-never-authorized-campaign/

**Comment window:** open this week. Marketing lesson with legs: talent-rights chain of custody in AI-company campaigns (the one category where "who actually signed" is the whole story).

## 3. OpenAI's Astra launch film angered the community whose tool it used

**What happened:** OpenAI's ad for its new Astra model release stages a demo in the style of MIT's "Put That There" (1979) and shows a fashion designer alone with a screen, with Blender (the free, community-driven open-source 3D tool) on screen. Blender users objected to their community's software being used to sell the idea that they're superfluous; the artist behind the Blender 5.2 splash-screen Puma expressed "disgust" at her work appearing in the ad. A representative X post (Sept 3): "BLENDER to advertise ch*tgpt???? … Stay tf away from human-made art." Creative Bloq's read: the ad's vision of creative work is one person alone in a room.

**Coverage:** Creative Bloq (Sept 6): https://www.creativebloq.com/ai/even-just-the-advert-for-chatgpt-6-astra-is-creating-controversy — (details of the model release itself are outside our lane; the ad reaction is the item.)

**Comment window:** narrowing; the pattern is the durable part. Fourth AI-lab marketing misfire we've tracked (Meta's optimism ad, Anthropic's doom reel, OpenAI Summer Club, now Astra) plus Samsung's SSD spot and Suno's Blige ad in a fortnight: AI companies keep failing the "who is this for and how will they feel" brief.
