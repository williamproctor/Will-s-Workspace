## 1. Samsung sold a drive for filmmakers with an ad no filmmaker made

**What happened:** Samsung's launch film for the Portable SSD P9, a drive pitched at professional video workflows and "12K direct recording," carried an on-screen disclosure for its whole runtime: "This content is created using generative AI." The audience for the product (editors, cinematographers) is the audience most able to spot generated footage. Leaker Ben Geskin reposted it on X; r/videography piled on; Honor quote-posted behind-the-scenes photos of its Magic V6 shoot with "none of these images or the upcoming videos are AI generated" and a staged cough of "SAMSUNG" (Aug 28). Second Samsung AI-ad backlash of 2026 after the Galaxy S26 low-light clip in February.

**Primary receipt:** Tom Goodwin, LinkedIn, Aug 29: "That Samsung made an entirely AI ad is astonishing to me… It's either an incredibly poor decision, or it's deliberately designed to get noticed by making people angry." https://www.linkedin.com/posts/tomfgoodwin_that-samsung-made-an-entirely-ai-ad-is-astonishing-activity-7499387859070586880-ETJr — the thread under it is the sharper text: a former Samsung agency hand: "once an audience senses the work isn't real, the doubt doesn't stay with the ad. It transfers to the brand… Efficiency is a line item. Trust is the balance sheet." Another commenter: "Advertising has always carried a cost signal - spending real money on craft is part of the message. An AI ad quietly says we were not willing to spend it."

**Coverage:** CineD opinion (the irony piece: the ad selling 12K RAW storage contains no footage; Samsung's NAND business also profits from the AI datacenter demand that doubled SSD prices): https://www.cined.com/opinion-the-ai-ad-selling-storage-to-the-filmmakers-its-replacing-samsungs-portable-ssd-p9-campaign/ · Honor's mock via tech-ish (Aug 28): https://tech-ish.com/2026/08/28/honor-mocks-samsung-for-using-ai-in-its-ssd-ads/

**Comment window:** open. Unclaimed take: "the cost signal" — production spend was always part of the message; AI production removes the signal, and the audience most likely to notice is the exact audience for a pro-storage product.

## 2. "Holy moly SEOs": Google started auto-expanding AI Overviews

**What happened:** Chris Long (@chris_nectiv) posted Aug 27: "Holy moly SEOs: Google is now revealing the AI Mode prompt box BY DEFAULT for some queries. This is how Google continues to push AI Mode as the default search experience." Search Engine Roundtable replicated it Aug 28; Google confirmed to SER and The Verge (spokesperson Jennifer Kutz): "For some queries, AI Overviews may dynamically expand for topics where our systems determine it's most useful for people… If users have already begun scrolling… we cancel expansion." Robby Stein replied to Barry Schwartz on X with the same framing plus "We're always testing and tuning these experiences." The Show more button disappears; the full AI answer plus an open "Ask anything" box sits above the ten blue links.

**Receipts:** Long's post embedded in SEL (X status URL not surfaced; attribute via SEL): https://searchengineland.com/google-is-dynamically-expanding-ai-overviews-for-some-queries-486200 · SER: https://www.seroundtable.com/google-ai-overviews-push-ai-mode-responses-41974.html · The Verge (Kutz statement): https://www.theverge.com/tech/986364/google-search-ai-overviews-auto-expand · Search Engine Watch (Stein reply): https://searchenginewatch.com/google-drops-show-more-button-pushes-users-into-ai-mode/

**Why it's spreading:** SEOs have expected AI Mode to become the default; the speed and the "our systems determine it's most useful" trigger (unauditable) amplified it. Same week: AI Mode gained link carousels for developing topics; Google's own "SEO for AI responses" line from John Mueller: nothing special to do.

**Comment window:** open. The take with data attached: pair this with Brainlabs' 10.5% session decline across 54 clients (Digiday, Aug 25) — the click loss now has a mechanism.

## 3. Meta's placement exclusions vanished, and practitioners found out first

**What happened:** From Aug 25, advertisers reported the ad placements control gone from Ads Manager, replaced by value rules that cap bid reductions at −90% (range +1,000% to −90%). A placement can be made expensive to win but not impossible: exclusion became suppression. Bram Van der Hallen (Edge.be) surfaced it on LinkedIn (post URL not located; via PPC Land). It's the last step in a sequence: detailed-targeting exclusions removed Jan 21, 2025; default 5% budget to excluded placements Oct 16, 2025; unified Advantage+ banned campaign-level placement exclusions Feb 18, 2026; API v26 removed Explore Feed and Messenger Stories as selectable surfaces July 29, 2026.

**Context:** AdExchanger (Sarah Sluis) the same day documented Meta ads talent dispersing to OpenAI (roughly 10% of OpenAI staff list Meta experience: Dugan, Shomair; Fidji Simo), TikTok, Amazon, Roku. Via PPC Land weekly: https://ppc.land/the-week-ai-agents-got-the-ad-account-and-meta-got-a-two-hour-clock/

**Comment window:** open through Q4 planning. Practical posts win: what value rules can and can't do versus exclusions; the "preference, not prohibition" principle.
