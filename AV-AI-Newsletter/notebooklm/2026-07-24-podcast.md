## The through-line

The central idea is: **more modalities require more control.**

FLUX 3 proposes one learned backbone across image, video, audio, and action. The supporting stories show why architecture alone is not enough. Voice needs direction and language control. Generated effects need picture timing. Music needs owned references and recurring identities. Model selection needs cost, latency, quality, and provenance policies.

## Act One — FLUX 3: one backbone, narrow current access

FLUX 3 is the most important story and should receive the most time.

Black Forest Labs announced **FLUX 3** on July 23 as one flow-matching backbone trained jointly across image, video, and audio, with the same foundation extended toward action prediction.

The current release is narrower than the full vision:

- **FLUX 3 Video:** gated early access
- **FLUX 3 Action:** gated early access for selected research and commercial partners
- **FLUX 3 Image:** promised in the following weeks
- **Video/Image APIs and private weights:** planned later, without public dates
- **FLUX 3 Dev open weights:** planned later in 2026; no license published

Video is the most specified capability. It supports:

- Up to **20 seconds** in one generation
- Native audio
- Text-to-video
- Image-to-video from an opening frame or looser reference
- Video-to-video
- Keyframe transitions
- Video and audio continuation
- Multilingual dialogue
- Multiple styles and aspect ratios
- Typography and animated design
- Agentic chaining into longer multi-shot sequences

The claim is larger than “a new video model.” Black Forest Labs is presenting a media and action foundation intended to carry context among stills, motion, sound, and system behavior.

The evidence needs careful qualification. The preliminary vendor comparison used **early-checkpoint 10-second, 720p clips with audio**, not the complete 20-second format.

Reported preference results:

- Luma Ray 3.2: **93%**
- Runway Gen-4.5: **77%**
- Grok Imagine Video: **69%**
- Kling v3 Pro: **60%**
- Happy Horse v1: **59%**
- Happy Horse 1.1: **57%**
- Seedance 2.0: **52%**
- Gemini Omni Flash: **52%**

The developer did not publish the sample size, prompt set, rater details, confidence intervals, randomization method, inference settings, or separate audio/picture scoring. A 52% result may be a tie, and the tested checkpoint may differ from the gated system.

Major unknowns:

- Public pricing
- Public API access
- License
- Parameter count
- Hardware and VRAM requirements
- Production SLA
- Maximum resolution
- Frame rate and output formats
- Reference limits
- Independent hands-on evaluation

Land this act with the accurate tension: FLUX 3’s architectural ambition is broad; current access, specifications, and evidence are narrow.

## Act Two — audio becomes directed, picture-aware, and repeatable

### Qwen-Audio-3.0

Qwen-Audio-3.0-TTS launched July 21 in two hosted variants:

- **Flash:** lower-latency interaction
- **Plus:** quality-focused synthesis

The service supports **16 languages**, natural-language delivery direction, inline nonverbal tags, voice cloning from imperfect references, and up to **three minutes per pass**.

An independent leaderboard snapshot placed Plus at **1,234 Elo ±16 from 1,517 samples**, effectively tied with Simba 3.2 in that evaluation. That single benchmark does not establish equal quality across every language, delivery style, or production setting.

Weights were not released. Full language coverage and 48kHz support were still rolling out during the research window.

### Sonilo Sound Effects 1.0

Sonilo starts with the picture. The system can analyze up to **three minutes of video** and produce one synchronized sound-effects track around visible events and timing. An optional prompt adds creative direction.

The separate text mode generates **one to 180 seconds** of sound.

Output:

- AAC
- MP3
- WAV
- FLAC

Pricing:

- Video-conditioned: **$0.009 per second**
- Text-only: **$0.0018 per second**

The launch calls the model 1.0, while current endpoints use v1.1. There was no independent synchronization benchmark or public methodology.

### ElevenMusic References, Vocals, and Finetunes

References accepts **10 seconds to five minutes** of owned audio to guide style, instrumentation, and feel.

Vocals creates a reusable singing identity from uploader-supplied recordings or a service-library voice.

Finetunes creates a private style model from up to **50 tracks**, with each track between 10 seconds and 10 minutes and a maximum of 250 minutes total.

The vendor screens uploads for recorded matches. That is a safeguard, not proof of ownership, performer consent, or every required right.

The act’s takeaway: generated audio is moving closer to picture timing, delivery direction, recurring identity, and reference-based control.

## Act Three — model selection and visual structure move behind systems

### Runway Media Router

Runway released Media Router on July 23 through Runway Dev. A reusable configuration routes image, video, or audio requests according to one preference:

- Cost
- Latency
- Quality

Configurations can include allowlists, denylists, modality-specific credit ceilings, fixed IDs, version history, realized costs, and dry runs. Runway says there is no routing surcharge; the selected model’s normal rate applies, with one credit equal to **$0.01**.

The router’s quality scoring and selection logic are internal. Provenance and reproducibility still depend on recording:

- Which model was chosen
- Which router version acted
- Which policy was active
- What the realized cost was

The useful framing is “model routing as editorial policy.” A low-cost preview, fast review result, and quality-focused final can use different standing priorities.

### Qwen-Image-3.0

Qwen-Image-3.0 targets structured material as well as standalone images.

Vendor claims:

This is relevant to storyboards, title graphics, production diagrams, presentations, and interface/reference layouts.

The model is available in Qwen Chat, while API access remained limited or trial-based. No public weights, parameter count, license, model card, blind benchmark, latency profile, or stable API contract accompanied the examples.

## Common patterns to land

1. **Unified multimodal models are becoming production layers.** One architecture may carry context among stills, motion, sound, and actions, but quality can vary by mode.
2. **References and revision are replacing one-shot prompting.** Images, video, keyframes, owned audio, reusable vocals, finetunes, and defined transition controls are becoming normal inputs.
3. **Routing is moving behind the interface.** Models and hardware may change underneath a stable production tool.
4. **Audio is becoming picture-aware.** Video can provide timing for effects, while delivery and identity controls shape speech and music.
5. **Launch velocity is outrunning evidence.** Availability and evidence type belong in the story, not in footnotes.
6. **Accessibility does not settle rights or provenance.** Public files, screening systems, and paid plans do not establish every permission.

## Closing segment — Back to Basics, Week 9

The habit is: **turn recurring review criteria into a reusable checklist.**

Week 8 separated drafting from review. Week 9 makes that review consistent.

Checklist design:

1. Gather checks that recur across captions, edit notes, metadata, graphics, equipment briefs, and delivery records.
2. Write each item as a yes-or-no question.
3. Require evidence: source link, file path, timecode, or specification.
4. Label vendor, author, customer, and independent evidence.
5. Keep missing inputs, uncertain rights, mismatched durations, and absent approvals open.
6. Version the checklist and add project-specific items without removing the baseline.

Core checklist:

```text
Facts and sources
- Names, dates, numbers, timecodes, and links verified?
- Evidence type labeled correctly?
- Unknowns and unavailable features stated?

Preservation
- Required wording, timing, dimensions, channels, and file names unchanged?
- Source assets and approved versions identified?

Structure and delivery
- Every required section and file present?
- Format, destination, and deadline requirements met?

Audience and approval
- Language and examples fit the audience?
- Rights, consent, and provenance questions recorded?
- Human approval captured before release?
```

The checklist does not replace judgment. It makes repeated judgment visible and reviewable.
