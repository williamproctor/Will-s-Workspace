2026-04-24 Week of April 24, 2026
Full Edition

OpenAI shipped two step-change products in the same 48-hour window: GPT-5.5, a fully retrained agentic model that takes the top spot on most benchmarks, and ChatGPT Images 2.0, which opened a 241-point lead over Google's Nano Banana Pro — the largest margin in Image Arena history. DeepSeek V4 dropped nine hours after GPT-5.5 matching its benchmarks at 86% lower cost. Kling added native 4K direct output. And NAB 2026 in Las Vegas surfaced the clearest signal yet that the major NLEs — Avid, Adobe, and DaVinci Resolve — are now all AI-native: Avid partnered with Google Cloud to embed Gemini inside Media Composer, Adobe debuted the agentic Firefly AI Assistant and a new editor-first Color Mode for Premiere, and Blackmagic shipped DaVinci Resolve 21 with over a dozen new AI tools.

This Week in AI

OpenAI Ships GPT-5.5 — The Biggest Single-Release Jump Since GPT-4.5

OpenAI released GPT-5.5 on April 23, available to Plus, Pro, Business, and Enterprise subscribers in ChatGPT and Codex. The API rollout was announced as "coming soon." This is OpenAI's first fully retrained base model since GPT-4.5 — not an incremental fine-tune — and the benchmark deltas reflect that.

Headline numbers (all first-pass):

  • 82.7% on Terminal-Bench 2.0 (up from 75.1% on GPT-5.4), which measures complex command-line workflows requiring multi-step planning and tool recovery.
  • 78.7% on OSWorld-Verified (up from 75.0%), real desktop computer-use tasks.
  • 84.9% on GDPval, which tests performance across 44 economically valuable occupations.
  • 58.6% on SWE-Bench Pro, real GitHub issue resolution — higher than any prior GPT release, but still trailing Claude Opus 4.7 (64.3%) and the restricted Claude Mythos Preview (77.8%).
  • 922K token context window and the same per-token latency as GPT-5.4.

Pricing stepped up to $5 per million input tokens and $30 per million output tokens, exactly double GPT-5.4. OpenAI notes GPT-5.5 uses significantly fewer tokens to complete the same Codex task, which partially offsets the increase. A new GPT-5.5 Pro tier is available to Pro, Business, and Enterprise subscribers for harder reasoning tasks and long-horizon research, scoring 90.1% on BrowseComp and 39.6% on FrontierMath Tier 4.

Two data points from the pre-release testing group stand out. NVIDIA, which rolled Codex to more than 10,000 engineers internally, reported that debugging cycles compressed from days to hours and that multi-week experimentation now completes overnight. Separately, OpenAI says the model can run independently on a complex task for more than seven hours without drift — the kind of long-horizon autonomy that makes agents-in-production a real conversation rather than a demo.

Community reception has been split. On the positive side, developers are calling Codex + GPT-5.5 "completely cracked" — one-shotting app builds, acing frontend work, handling long contexts cleanly. On the negative side, heavy users burned through their weekly Plus and Pro session limits in 15 minutes of agentic use, and multiple reports flag intermittent "laziness" where the model pauses mid-task to ask whether to continue, even when instructed not to.

ChatGPT Images 2.0 Posts the Largest Lead in Image Arena History

Two days before GPT-5.5, OpenAI shipped ChatGPT Images 2.0 on April 21 — the successor to GPT-image-1.5, now a fully standalone model rather than a subsystem of the GPT-4o pipeline. It is available to every ChatGPT user (including free tier) in Instant mode, with a higher-quality Thinking mode restricted to Plus, Pro, Business, and Enterprise. Developers access it through the API as gpt-image-2.

The benchmark result has to be read twice to make sure you're not misreading it. On the Arena.ai text-to-image leaderboard, gpt-image-2 posted 1,512 at launch — 241 points above the second-place model (Google's Nano Banana Pro at 1,271). For context, the previous top-15 range was roughly 130 points total. This is the largest launch-day lead any image model has held in Arena's history.

What actually changed:

  • Text rendering accuracy jumped from ~90–95% (Images 1.5) to 99%+, across dozens of languages including Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Hindi, and Bengali. The long-standing weakness around menus, labels, infographics, and UI mockups is effectively solved.
  • Reasoning is native. The model plans composition, resolves spatial relationships, and interprets multi-part instructions before rendering. This is what "Thinking mode" unlocks: web search, verification, and multi-image batching (up to 8 consistent outputs from one prompt).
  • Aspect ratio range expanded to 3:1 through 1:3 — banners, posters, portraits, and mobile formats in a single pass.
  • Native resolution up to 2K, with 4K available through third-party hosting partners like fal.ai.
  • Pricing at 1024×1024: roughly $0.006 low quality, $0.053 medium, $0.211 high. Token-based billing for edits with reference images is $8 per million input tokens, $32 per million output.

The limitations matter. There is no transparent background output — workflows requiring alpha channel PNGs still need Images 1.5 or a Flux 2 variant. Brand logo fidelity is inconsistent (ZDNet's hands-on failed to reproduce the ZDNET wordmark accurately, and creators have flagged similar issues with proprietary typefaces). Generation is slower than Nano Banana 2 or Flux 2 — expect 15–60 seconds per image on ChatGPT Plus. And the artistic style range is narrower than Midjourney V8 for abstract or painterly work.

The designer reception has been unusual in its consistency. Poster and flyer designers are reporting that text-in-image is finally production-ready. Several said they were able to skip Photoshop for first-pass marketing assets because the composition sense is strong enough to handle layout decisions unassisted. The repeated skeptical reply — "here are some posters I made without AI" — became the week's recurring joke on X precisely because the outputs were hard to tell apart.

Kling Video 3.0 Adds Native 4K Direct Output

Kuaishou's Kling AI announced on April 23 that its Video 3.0 series now supports native 4K direct output — meaning the model generates at full 4K resolution rather than upscaling from a lower native resolution. According to the Chinese-language release, this makes Kling the first video generation model to deliver native 4K without post-processing, targeting film, advertising, and professional production customers.

This sits on top of the existing Kling 3.0 foundation, which launched in February 2026 with native 4K at 60fps in its broader release, up to 15-second durations, multi-shot sequencing, native audio across multiple languages and accents, and the unified multimodal pipeline (text-to-video, image-to-video, reference-to-video, and in-video editing in one model). The April 23 update makes 4K direct generation available in the main Video 3.0 product interface rather than requiring a separate workflow.

A preview of where the category is heading. Kuaishou published a 4K native-output reel on YouTube alongside the announcement. Worth watching less for the question of whether Kling specifically becomes the best video generation model, and more for what it signals about where all these tools are converging in terms of image quality, motion coherence, and camera control. If this is now the floor, it resets expectations for what Veo 4 (widely expected at Google I/O on May 19–20) and the next Sora-successor will need to clear.

Notably, Kling 3.0 and Kling 3.0 Omni were also added to Adobe Firefly's model roster during NAB week, joining a lineup that now includes Google's Nano Banana 2 and Veo 3.1, Runway's Gen-4.5, Luma's Ray3.14, Black Forest Labs' FLUX.2, ElevenLabs' Multilingual v2, and Topaz Astra. Firefly's pitch is increasingly that the model choice is the user's to make, not the platform's.

DaVinci Resolve 21 — Public Beta Ships With a Full AI Toolkit

Blackmagic Design announced DaVinci Resolve 21 on April 13 ahead of NAB, with the public beta available as a free download the same day. This is a major release — over 100 new features — and the AI additions specifically close most of the gap that Resolve had opened up against Premiere and Final Cut on AI tooling.

The new Photo page is the headline. It brings the full DaVinci color grading stack — primary correction, curves, qualifiers, power windows, node-based grades — to still photos, with tethered shooting support for Sony and Canon cameras, album organization, LightBox view, and batch processing across a whole album. Blackmagic is explicitly targeting photographers and colorists who want to use Hollywood color tools on stills.

The AI additions across the application:

  • AI IntelliSearch — natural-language clip search in the Media Pool by object, face, or dialogue keywords.
  • AI CineFocus — synthetic depth-of-field adjustment after the fact, with keyframeable rack focus, selectable aperture shapes, and optical bokeh effects.
  • AI Face Age Transformer — age or de-age a tracked subject across a clip. Positioned for flashback and flash-forward continuity.
  • AI Face Reshaper — change the shape and position of facial features on a moving subject (eyes, nose, mouth, eyebrows, overall face shape), with manual fine-tune controls.
  • AI Blemish Removal — skin retouching that preserves natural texture rather than smoothing everything flat.
  • AI Slate ID — automatic clapperboard metadata extraction, working on dark or out-of-focus frames.
  • AI UltraSharpen — sharpening tool for moving images, positioned to rescue upscaled footage or fix slight focus errors.
  • AI Motion Deblur — removes streaks and softness from motion, useful for slow-motion and freeze-frame work.
  • AI Speech Generator — voice cloning from as little as 10 seconds of source audio, with speed, pitch, and inflection control for generating multiple voiceover takes from text.

Beyond AI, the release adds Krokodove (70+ new compositing and graphics tools in Fusion), Fairlight folder tracks for audio management, native OGraf HTML and Lottie animation support (drag-and-drop .json and .lottie files), MultiMaster trim passes for generating multiple HDR and SDR deliverables from a single timeline, and Apple Immersive foveated rendering support.

Adobe's Premiere Color Mode won the NAB 2026 Product of the Year in the Graphics/VFX/Switchers category. Resolve's AI toolkit is the response.

NAB 2026: Avid and Google Cloud Partner to Embed Gemini Into Media Composer

Avid and Google Cloud announced a multi-year strategic partnership on April 16 to integrate Gemini models and Vertex AI directly into Avid Media Composer and the newly general-availability Avid Content Core. The partnership was demonstrated at NAB 2026 at both the Google Cloud booth and the Avid booth.

Three concrete pieces:

  • Gemini integration inside Media Composer. A multimodal extension lets editors query footage using natural language — by visual action, dialogue, or emotional cue — and generate B-roll, metadata, and automated logging without leaving the NLE.
  • Avid Content Core reaches general availability. Positioned as a unified data layer for global media assets, running on BigQuery, Vision Warehouse, and Vertex AI Search. It won the NAB 2026 Product of the Year award in the Media Supply Chain category.
  • Agentic search and discovery. Editors describe what they need; the system searches by context rather than filename or tag. What the press release calls "weeks of manual archive discovery compressed into seconds."

Google Cloud's Anil Jain framed this as a shift from automation to collaboration: "By embedding agentic AI directly into the tools video editors live in, we're moving beyond simple automation."

NAB 2026: Adobe Ships Firefly AI Assistant, Premiere Color Mode, Frame.io Drive

Adobe used NAB 2026 to push out the biggest bundle of updates to its Creative Cloud video stack in years.

  • Premiere Pro Color Mode (beta) — a new grading interface built for editors rather than dedicated colorists. Visual, layered, and scales from basic corrections to advanced looks. Won NAB 2026 Product of the Year in the Graphics, VFX, Switchers category.
  • After Effects AI Object Matte — automated subject detection and refinement for rotoscoping and masking, with manual edge tools for cleanup. Roto work that used to take hours collapses into minutes.
  • Frame.io Drive — lets teams mount cloud-based Frame.io projects as if they were local drives. Removes the manual-download and duplicate-storage friction that remote-collaboration workflows have been stuck with.
  • Firefly AI Assistant (public beta, coming weeks) — an agentic AI layer that takes natural-language intent and orchestrates work across Premiere, Photoshop, Lightroom, Express, Illustrator, and other Creative Cloud apps from a single conversational interface. Adobe confirmed it will extend to third-party models including Anthropic's Claude.
  • Firefly Video Editor picks up Enhance Speech (previously Premiere/Podcast only) plus noise, reverb, and level balancing across speech, music, and ambience.
  • Firefly model roster expands to include Kling 3.0 and Kling 3.0 Omni, joining Google's Nano Banana 2 and Veo 3.1, Runway Gen-4.5, Luma Ray3.14, FLUX.2, ElevenLabs Multilingual v2, and Topaz Astra.

NAB 2026: The Other AI Stories That Mattered

A short tour through the announcements that weren't the NLE giants but are worth tracking:

  • TwelveLabs Pegasus 1.5. A video intelligence model that discovers temporal boundaries in video and extracts structured metadata matching a customer-defined schema — outperforming Gemini 2.5 Pro by 30% on aggregate segmentation quality. Already in production with one major broadcast network. TwelveLabs also introduced Rodeo, an AI creative co-pilot for natural-language footage search and assembly, and announced a partnership with Autodesk on Flow Capture (their digital-dailies tool formerly known as PIX).
  • Amplify's KAI. AI story-building inside Adobe Premiere as an extension panel. Developed jointly with Gestmusic (a major European entertainment production company) with support from EIT Culture & Creativity. Editors search and navigate footage using AI-generated story structure — moments, topics, beats — generate story options via natural language, and send selects and story structure directly into the timeline.
  • Eluvio EVIE. Agentic orchestration for title libraries and live sports, built natively into a streaming media pipeline with "zero copy, zero file movement, zero re-transcoding." Inline frame-accurate video intelligence for VOD and live.
  • OBSBOT Tail 2 and Talent 2. AI-powered PTZR camera (4K/60fps with AI Tracking 2.0) and a new all-in-one production hub aimed at multi-camera podcasting and virtual production. Separately, OBSBOT ran a demo with 4DV.ai using roughly 60 synchronized Tail 2 cameras to drive a 4D Gaussian splatting volumetric capture installation, viewable in VR on the show floor.
  • MediaPET 2.1. Dr. Duane Varan's team launched an agentic video system on April 17 that replaces timeline-based editing with conversational direction — natural-language instructions executed across the entire project in real time, controllable from a phone.

DeepSeek V4 Matches GPT-5.5 at 86% Lower Cost — Nine Hours After the GPT-5.5 Launch

Roughly nine hours after OpenAI announced GPT-5.5, DeepSeek released V4 — a new open-source 1M-context mixture-of-experts model. Early independent benchmarks show it matching or exceeding GPT-5.5 on SWE-Verified and Codeforces while pricing approximately 86% below OpenAI's tokens. The model is fully open-source under an Apache-style license.

This is now the third time in eighteen months that DeepSeek has released a frontier-competitive open model within days of a flagship US launch. The pattern matters less as a pricing headline than as an ongoing signal about how long proprietary model advantages are holding. Answer so far: days, not months.

Voices This Week

Ethan Mollick on GPT-5.5 as a "Research Partner"

Wharton professor and long-time AI analyst Ethan Mollick, who had pre-release access through OpenAI's closed testing group, described GPT-5.5 as qualitatively different from its predecessors: "a research partner" that performs especially well when paired with contextual inputs from documents and plugins. His framing is worth noting because the benchmark language ("82.7% on Terminal-Bench 2.0") hides what the model actually feels like in use — closer to a collaborator that proposes next steps than an assistant that executes instructions.

Greg Brockman on What GPT-5.5 Enables

OpenAI President Greg Brockman during the press briefing: "What is really special about this model is how much more it can do with less guidance. It can look at an unclear problem and figure out just what needs to happen next. It really, to me, feels like it's setting the foundation for how we're going to use computers, how we're going to do computer work going forward." For a release briefing this is about as explicit a pitch as OpenAI has made that agents — not chat — are the product going forward.

Anil Jain on Avid × Google Cloud

Google Cloud global industry leader for media and entertainment Anil Jain: "By embedding agentic AI directly into the tools video editors live in, we're moving beyond simple automation. With Avid Media Composer and Google Cloud, an editor can now collaborate with an intelligent agent to create assets on the fly and handle the heavy lifting of matching styles and filling timelines." This is the partnership's central claim — that the model is no longer a standalone window the editor switches to, but a collaborator inside the NLE.

Dr. Duane Varan on Replacing the Timeline

MediaPET.ai CEO Dr. Duane Varan, on the launch of MediaPET 2.1: "We're not just adding voice to video editing — we're replacing the editing model itself. Creators no longer have to work clip by clip. They can simply express what they want, and the system carries it out across the whole project." Whether MediaPET's specific implementation wins or not, the thesis — that the timeline itself is a vestigial interface — is now being argued by multiple AV-tool companies at once, including Amplify with KAI and Adobe with the Firefly AI Assistant.

Common Threads

Agentic AI Is Now the Default Frame for New AV Tools

Every major launch this week — GPT-5.5, Firefly AI Assistant, Avid × Gemini, MediaPET 2.1, Amplify KAI, Eluvio EVIE, D-ID Agentic Videos — is being positioned with the word "agentic" in the first sentence. What "agentic" actually means varies: a model running seven-hour coding sessions, an assistant orchestrating Creative Cloud apps from one conversation, a search-and-assemble co-pilot inside an NLE, or an interactive video player that talks back to viewers. The marketing category is converging faster than the technology, which is worth tracking — the gap between "demo that runs agentically" and "agent that works reliably in production" is still the biggest risk in the space.

The Major NLEs Are Now All AI-Native

Within a single NAB week: Adobe Premiere got Color Mode, AI Object Matte, and the Firefly AI Assistant preview. Avid Media Composer became a Gemini-powered agentic workflow. DaVinci Resolve 21 shipped ten distinct AI tools including voice cloning, face reshaping, slate reading, and dialogue-keyword search. That's every major professional NLE adopting AI as a first-class feature in a thirty-day window. The era where AI was a plugin added to an NLE is effectively over; it is now part of the base product.

Proprietary Model Lead Times Are Measured in Days, Not Months

GPT-5.5 led the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index for roughly nine hours before DeepSeek V4 landed with comparable benchmarks and pricing 86% below OpenAI's. ChatGPT Images 2.0 opened a 241-point lead over Google's Nano Banana Pro on April 21; the assumption inside the industry is that Nano Banana Pro 2 and Flux 3 are weeks away, not months. The observation is simple — the cost of running frontier inference is dropping faster than any one company can defend, which is the quiet reason Firefly, Shutterstock, and Cliprise all shipped multi-model platforms this month rather than doubling down on a single partner.

Text-In-Image Was a Barrier; It's Now Solved

GPT Image 2's 99%+ text rendering accuracy — and the related Flux 2 and Nano Banana Pro scores in the 80s and 90s — collectively close a three-year-old weakness that had kept generative image tools out of production marketing, UI design, and packaging work. The practical consequence is that a designer asking an AI for a mocked-up poster, menu, or infographic gets something usable on the first pass, rather than an illustrative asset that has to be rebuilt in Photoshop or Figma with correct text composited on top.

Novel Ideas Worth Watching

Agentic Direction Replacing Clip-by-Clip Editing

MediaPET 2.1, Amplify's KAI, and Adobe's Firefly AI Assistant all argue the same thing from different angles: the timeline is a legacy interface, and "direct the project, don't edit clips" is the next mental model. None of the three has proven out at feature-length production scale yet, but the thesis itself is now being underwritten by three different companies in the same 30-day window.

Structured Metadata Schemas at Scale

TwelveLabs' Pegasus 1.5 is interesting less for the model and more for the UX: customers define their own metadata schema, and the model extracts against it. This flips the usual "tag what the model found" pattern into "find what the business needs tagged," and it's the approach most obviously compatible with broadcast and enterprise archive workflows where the taxonomy is fixed and well-documented.

Volumetric Capture via Off-the-Shelf PTZR Cameras

OBSBOT's 60-camera 4D Gaussian splatting demo with 4DV.ai is worth flagging because it suggests volumetric capture — historically a dedicated-rig, purpose-built workflow — is migrating toward arrays of commodity PTZR cameras. The installation was viewable in VR on the NAB floor. It's early, but it's the kind of demo that becomes a standard technique in three-to-five years.

Slate ID as a Solved Problem

DaVinci Resolve 21's AI Slate ID is a small feature that solves a genuinely annoying production workflow — manually logging take numbers, scene IDs, and timecodes from clapperboards that are sometimes dark or out of focus. It won't make headlines, but it's the type of AI utility that production teams quietly notice saving them hours per week.

Tool of the Week

DaVinci Resolve 21 (Public Beta)

What it does: Blackmagic's industry-standard editing, color, VFX, and audio-post suite, now with over a dozen new AI tools (voice cloning, face reshaping, slate reading, depth-of-field synthesis, object-based clip search, motion deblur) plus a new Photo page for stills color grading, the Krokodove Fusion toolkit, and native OGraf HTML / Lottie animation support. Over 100 new features total in a single release.

Who it's for: Colorists, editors, VFX artists, and photographers who already work in the Resolve ecosystem or who have been holding off on adopting Resolve specifically because the AI feature set lagged Premiere and Final Cut. The Photo page also brings the full DaVinci color stack to still photographers who weren't previously in the target market.

Pricing: The public beta of DaVinci Resolve 21 (and Fusion Studio 21) is free. DaVinci Resolve Studio 21, which adds advanced effects and collaboration features, is a one-time $299 purchase with free updates.

Worth trying? For anyone in the AV production stack, yes — the public beta is free, the release is production-ready enough that Blackmagic shipped it as a public beta rather than a private one, and the AI toolkit meaningfully closes the feature gap with Premiere. The final release typically lands around IBC in September if prior years hold.
