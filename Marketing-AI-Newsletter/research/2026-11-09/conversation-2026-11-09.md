## 2. "ChatGPT's ads don't pay": a working paper valued a ChatGPT click at $0.26 against $1.72 paid, OpenAI said users "do not seem to mind ads", and the bid cap is going away

**Timing note:** in window. Christian Schulze's LinkedIn post decodes to Mon Nov 2 09:49 UTC. The working paper (Maximilian Kaiser and Christian Schulze, "Can Advertising Fund Free AI?") is dated October 2026 and is under review. PPC Land wrote it up Nov 2, Frankfurt School's release ran Tue Nov 3, and SEJ, SEL and Digiday followed. OpenAI's Adam Fry spoke at an OpenAI-hosted dinner in New York on Monday night, Nov 2; Big Technology posted the transcript Tue Nov 3, and The Verge's story ran Nov 3 at 6:30 PM UTC. OpenAI emailed advertisers about the bidding change on Nov 5–6 (SER, Nov 6), effective Nov 9. ChatGPT's creative enhancements and wider card were 2026-11-02 stories; this item is new.

**What happened:** Schulze opened with "ChatGPT's ads don't pay, and fixing them could cost ChatGPT its users." The paper uses Grips Intelligence data on 1,898 online shops with about $16B in sales, about 10 billion sessions over 60 weeks (June 2025 to Aug 22, 2026), and the ad-free EU as the control.
- **Value per visit.** In US traffic, "a paid ChatGPT visit generates $0.89 in revenue per session against $2.45 for a paid Google Search visit, a 64% shortfall", with conversion at 0.37% against 1.17%. Of the 87 US advertisers buying both channels, 73 (84%) recorded no paid ChatGPT transaction, and one advertiser carries 66% of all paid ChatGPT purchases.
- **The parity price.** A ChatGPT click would have to cost $0.26 to return as much per dollar as a search click. The largest public account paid $1.72, "more than six times the parity price", and OpenAI recommends starting bids of $3 to $5.
- **Organic referrals.** With ads on, organic ChatGPT referrals to shops fell an estimated 5.9% (95% interval −12.6% to +1.2%; p = .103), which the authors give as "6 to 8%", and conversion on those visits didn't change.
- **The arithmetic.** Schulze on LinkedIn: OpenAI's projections assume "about $40 in ad revenue per free user by 2030", serving a free user costs "about $5 a year", and "Today the ads bring about $1".

The same Monday night, Fry said ChatGPT's "volume of search-like queries is now roughly one-fifth the volume of queries of Google" and, per The Verge, that "most people do not seem to mind ads in the product". Then OpenAI's weekly email said it is "evolving ChatGPT Ads to be more intelligent by default". From Nov 9, new campaigns get Target CPA instead of Max Bid and Target CPC instead of Max CPC, and the caps retire Jan 1, 2027. OpenAI's help page: "While Max CPC caps individual bids, Target CPC lets our system bid above or below your target in individual auctions". In Schulze's comments, Olivia Kory pushed back: "Last-click revenue isn't incremental revenue". We found no OpenAI reply to the paper; its own case study arrived Nov 9 (post-window note below).

## 3. Facebook's AI-written "Answers" pages: more than 6 million indexed, about 40 million visits a month from Google by one estimate, and closed to AI crawlers

**Timing note:** in window as news; the pages themselves predate it. Saul Westbrook's post on Peripheral Creative's site is dated Wed Nov 4 (uper.pl), and the post itself says "As of 3 November, Facebook have not spoken publicly". He says he first noticed the pages in June 2026, and his Semrush figures show the growth from July. SER wrote it up Thu Nov 5 at 8:01 am, embedding Glenn Gabe's X posts from that day; bediscoverable, uper.pl and somethinginc followed.

**What happened:** Westbrook found that Meta "quietly indexed millions of AI-generated Facebook pages under the url facebook.com/fb-answers/". The pages are Q&A-style answers with "no editorial oversight", including more than 180 English pages on padel tennis. By his Semrush estimates, the directory drew 40,590,906 visits worldwide in October (21,058,474 from the US and 2,936,009 from the UK), up from 36,388,769 in September and 12,167,857 in August. More than 39 million US pages rank, and ranking keywords went from 4 million to 53 million between July and August. He also found "LLMs are blocked from crawling these pages." Gabe called it "another example of massive "Scaled content abuse"", with "6M+ urls indexed" ranking "across surfaces (10-blue links, AIOs, and AI Mode)". He posted a second chart showing "visibility across AIOs, AI Mode, and Gemini". Per bediscoverable, Facebook's robots.txt lets Googlebot and Bingbot in and blocks GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and Google-Extended. Barry Schwartz said he'd heard rumors that Instagram is doing something similar. Neither Google nor Meta has commented. Scaled content abuse is one of Google's spam policies, and Google hasn't said whether these pages break it.
