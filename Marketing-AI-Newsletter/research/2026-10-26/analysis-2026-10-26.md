## What leads

**News lead:** Meta charged more per ad, and its agent went shopping. Meta's Q3 (Wed Oct 21, after the close) put advertising revenue at $63.06B, up 26%, on 10% more ad impressions and a 14% higher average price per ad. On the call, Susan Li tied the gains to Meta's AI systems: GEM is "driving a 1.7% increase in conversions for offsite conversion campaigns and a 9.6% increase in app ad conversions," Business Assistant adopters "drove almost 5% more conversions at the same cost per conversion and increased their advertising investment by 9% compared with similar non-adopters" (Meta's estimate), and "In Q3, nearly 10 million advertisers used at least one of these tools" (its generative-AI creative tools). Zuckerberg said more than 10 million people in the US and Canada set up Muse in a month and a half, that shopping is its most common use, "with more than a quarter of people who are using Muse engaging in this with an average purchase size over $200," and that Meta will "get paid a fee based on the transactions that we help facilitate. We expect this will be paid for by businesses." GAAP operating margin fell to 20% from 40% on a $9.89B charge for the agreement with 52 state attorneys general; Q4 revenue guidance is $73–76B. For marketers, two things are new: the average price per Meta ad rose faster than impressions, and Meta's shopping agent comes with a transaction fee that businesses are expected to pay.

## Common-thread candidates
1. **The platform edits the ad, and the contract hands you the risk.** OpenAI's enhancements "can run automatically without requiring you to review or approve each version," and its help page says it "does not provide a preview of every variation." Meta's clause makes customers "solely responsible" for AI output, and Adweek's advertisers pulled ads Meta's AI had altered. Just Eat put AI pictures on restaurants' menus and apologised. Meta says nearly 10 million advertisers used its generative-AI creative tools in Q3. The 2026-09-28 edition named default-on as the launch strategy and 10-19 dated the defaults; the new fact this week is liability, meaning who answers for output nobody reviewed.
2. **The AI channel is small where it can be measured, and the instruments are straining.** ChatGPT sends 0.62% of Google organic's traffic (Haynes) and 3.2 conversions for every 100 from organic (The SEO Works), and Indig can't find "hidden" AI traffic in Direct. Nozzle says 13.9% of SERPs "played dumb" for rank trackers, Ahrefs is cleaning tainted history, and Microsoft's Clarity docs say bot requests don't mean content was "cited, or surfaced". Semrush has AI Overviews on 88% of commercial keywords, which is Fishkin's caveat: the AI surface that matters most is Google's own.
3. **Identity moved behind the walls.** Apple blocks identity-resolution domains in Safari 27 but says "First party personalization is not tracking." The FTC makes Publicis keep LiveRamp open to rivals for five years. Salesforce pays about $5.66B for Braze's first-party engagement layer, Kevel's CEO says "Consolidation is going to happen.", and Zuckerberg says buying something will mean "talking to their agent." The cross-site route is closing, and the data that's left sits with whoever owns the login, the CRM or the agent.
4. **The date moved; the text didn't.** Meta pushed its terms to Nov 20 with "no other changes." OpenAI's opt-out date has read Oct 29, Oct 30 and Oct 31 across its own pages. Netflix cut its ad forecast because "some ad product capabilities" rolled out slower than expected. Google's Smart Bidding page says learning "never lasts more than 7 days" while another Google page still lists "Setting change." Read the current text and date-stamp it.
