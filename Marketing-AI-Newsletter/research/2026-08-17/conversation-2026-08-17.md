# Conversation Research — Week of August 10–16, 2026

Compiled 2026-08-18 (retroactive edition). Primary synthesis: PPC Land weekly (https://ppc.land/ad-tech-repriced-everything-this-week-except-the-thing-doing-the-counting/).

## 1. Perplexity blocks Time's agent ads, calls the format "deceptive" (Aug 11)

- Less than two weeks after Time started serving ads inside the markdown versions of its pages (aimed at AI agents; Mobian generates FAQ-formatted brand content from a brief; early buyers Ally Bank and Project Management Institute), Perplexity confirmed it blocked those ads from influencing its index and labeled the practice deceptive.
- Perplexity CCO Jesse Dwyer: company works continuously to protect users from deceptive practices "sponsored or not"; warned publishers deploying markdown ads risk a reputational downgrade in Perplexity's proprietary index, including a trust-score hit. Perplexity didn't answer how it defines deceptive practice or how it blocks the ads. Time didn't respond.
- Key detail: Time labeled the units as sponsored at the top of the markdown page; Perplexity's objection is to the practice, not the disclosure. The label doesn't survive retrieval — Robert Webster (TAU): "a promotional claim can end up cited as a neutral fact."
- Incentive reads: Steven Liss (OpenAds.AI) — the stance risks driving publishers to block Perplexity's crawlers; Shiv Gupta (U of Digital) — other model operators may follow, partly to keep control over how their own platforms are monetized. An answer engine blocking publisher-inserted ads is protecting inventory it hasn't sold yet.
- Webster's frame: the internet is splitting into two tracks (content/ads for humans; content/ads for agents), and platforms are ruling on the new inventory before any standard exists.
- Thread continuity: edition 1 — Time's "two audiences"; edition 08-03 — Time serving ads to agents; now the first platform veto.

## 2. NYT's Kopit Levien puts numbers on the content-economics argument (Odd Lots, Aug 10)

- NYT spent close to **$2B last year producing ~500,000 works of journalism** — a per-unit reference of roughly **$4,000** now public. Archive: 175 years.
- Licensing framework in three separated components: continuity (deal must run alongside a sustainable business), permission WITH control (governance of downstream use), then price. Publishers usually conflate permission and payment.
- Supply-side argument: Stack Overflow logged 1,442 questions in July, down 99% from the March 2014 peak (207,204/month) — the corpus that trained the models is being consumed by the products built on it. Buyer-side counter: a bipartisan amicus brief (Aug 4, D.C. Circuit) says three firms hold 88% of AI model API revenue.
- NYT position: suing OpenAI/Microsoft and Perplexity; licensing to Amazon. Q2 digital advertising +20.7% to $114M while USA TODAY Co. lost 22M monthly uniques in a quarter.

## 3. Agent hype met the receipts: Letaido's "40 hours to 60 minutes" vs KPMG's 49% cut rate

- Ahrefs launched Letaido (Aug 12), an agent-powered marketing workspace on its index (170T+ pages) plus AI-visibility data from 439M+ monthly prompts. Customer quote (Simmonds): keyword research and content audits "that used to take a full 40-hour week, now take just 60 minutes... the gap between teams that embrace AI agents and those that don't is going to widen, fast." No pricing, no availability dates, no independently verified performance data published.
- Same week, KPMG (covered Aug 8, n=2,145 leaders): **49% of organizations cut AI agent rollouts when costs outran value; established ROI at 7%** while planned AI spend held at $188M. Agencies split on token costs: Dept won't itemize them; Monks builds them into subscriptions; PMG caps staff at $50/day. WPP's Cindy Rose: outcome-based pay "years away."
- The discourse: vendor time-compression claims against enterprise cut rates. Both real; the gap is governance and verification.
