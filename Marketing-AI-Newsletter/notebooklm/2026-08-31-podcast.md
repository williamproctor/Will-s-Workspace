### Meta's placement exclusions disappeared, and buyers found out first

No blog post, no release note. From August 25, advertisers reported the ad placements control gone from Ads Manager, replaced by value rules that can adjust bids from +1,000% down to a floor of −90%. That floor is the whole story: a placement can be made expensive to win but never impossible. Exclusion became suppression. Bram Van der Hallen of Edge.be surfaced the change on LinkedIn, and the practitioner threads filled in the history, because this was the last step in a sequence: detailed-targeting exclusions removed in January 2025, a default 5% of budget flowing to excluded placements from October 2025, campaign-level placement exclusions banned in the unified Advantage+ structure in February, and Explore Feed and Messenger Stories removed as selectable surfaces in API v26 this July.

AdExchanger's Sarah Sluis published the companion story the same day: Meta's ad engineers are dispersing to OpenAI (roughly 10% of its staff list Meta experience), TikTok, Amazon, and Roku. Meta is retiring levers at the moment the people who understood why they existed are building the rival platforms.

**Comment window:** open through Q4 builds. Practical posts win: what value rules can and can't do versus true exclusions, and how to document the "preference, not prohibition" principle for clients.

## Act Two — Lead story

The most important hard-news story this week is **AI agents got the ad account**. Give it the most discussion time.

X launched X Ads MCP on August 24: a remote Model Context Protocol server inside its API gateway that exposes 23 advertising tools to any MCP-capable client, Grok and Claude Code included. Ten of the tools write to live accounts (create campaigns and line items, activate, pause, adjust budgets and creative), authenticated with the advertiser's own OAuth token. The structural safeguard is worth quoting because it's the only one: every campaign and line item is created paused, and nothing spends until a human explicitly activates it. No beta label, no rollout schedule; the endpoint and docs are live.

Three days later The Trade Desk shipped Zuma, framed modestly as the Q3 update to Kokai (a claimed 32% average CPA cut across 62 campaigns compares the new optimization model with the old one, not against a control). The decision that matters sits in one line: The Trade Desk is opening its AI layer to external tools, including Claude, so agencies can run the platform from interfaces they already use. For a company whose identity rests on independence from walled gardens, conceding that the interface is no longer where the relationship is won is consistent, and it's a tell about where buying is heading.

And the IAB confirmed on August 24 that it's building a framework to measure and attribute ads served to AI agents, targeting November 12. Agents can now spend the money; the standard for counting what they bought is eleven weeks out.

### Meta will pay up to $18 billion and cap teen use at two hours a day

Eight days into a federal trial in Oakland, with Instagram's Adam Mosseri already on the stand and Mark Zuckerberg scheduled, Meta settled with 47 states, DC, and three territories on August 26. The money: at least $12.1 billion guaranteed over ten years, up to roughly $5 billion more contingent on competitors adopting matching limits, plus $459 million resolving Cambridge Analytica claims; headline totals run from $17 billion (AP) to $18 billion (Reuters). Texas took a separate deal worth more than $1 billion; Florida and New Mexico stayed out. Meta admits no wrongdoing and will book a $10 billion legal charge this quarter that wasn't in guidance. Four of the states had been seeking penalties up to $1.4 trillion.

For marketers the product terms outrank the dollars: a daily two-hour limit on Instagram and Facebook for users 13 to 17, nighttime blocks, and expanded parental supervision, on a timeline California's AG describes as "within months." Capped teen time is capped teen inventory. Reach and frequency assumptions for Gen Z plans built on Meta will need rebuilding, and the contingency structure gives every other platform a financial reason to adopt the same clock.

### Google kept the transaction inside search and encrypted the way out

Three moves in one week. On August 27, AI Mode gained the ability to complete hotel bookings inside the product, paid through Google Pay, in US English with a partner list running from Booking.com and Expedia through Hilton, Marriott, and Wyndham; flight price tracking with target-price alerts went live in 180-plus countries. On August 28, Google confirmed AI Overviews now dynamically expand to full answers for some queries (see The Conversation). And on August 26, Search Engine Roundtable confirmed that Google is routing search clicks through an encoded google.com/goto passthrough redirect instead of linking directly, a mechanism in testing since July that Nozzle's Derek Perkins observed at near-total coverage across residential IPs. Google declined to comment.

The redirect is anti-scraping engineering, and its collateral effect is measurement: an encoded link can't be decoded without following it, which pushes rank-tracking tools into hundreds of requests per results page. Put the three together and the shape is clear. More of the journey completes inside Google's surfaces, the surfaces expand to fill the screen, and the exits get harder for anyone outside Google to observe. Marketing O'Clock devoted its August 31 episode to the /goto change.

### AI search got audited from three directions, and the audits agree

The advertiser view came from Brainlabs via Digiday on August 25: 54 clients across 19 sectors, January 2025 to April 2026, aggregate sessions down 10.5% (140.1 million to 125.4 million), with 46 of 54 losing traffic. The business view is harsher. A peer-reviewed study (arXiv 2608.07069, Norly Research) mapped 4,776 food venues in two Bali districts and ran 2,208 queries across ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Perplexity: at least 85.6% of venues were never recommended by any system in any run. Owning a website raised the odds of inclusion 1.92 times; review volume 1.64 times. Then the accuracy check: Empirank audited 182 businesses OpenAI systems had recommended and found 24 incorrect claims, roughly one in nine, with 479 more unconfirmed.

The commercial layer keeps growing on the same substrate. Adthena has retail taking 39% of ChatGPT advertising, with ads reaching 4.47% of US queries at one item per answer, which is the benchmark European buyers inherited when EEA ads launched on August 24 without personalization. Traffic down, most businesses invisible, one in nine recommendations wrong, and ad load rising: the distribution layer is being built faster than anyone is checking it.

The common thread: decentralized teams producing marketing at machine scale, with no function that owns whether it's licensed, labeled, or true. ### Microsoft turned on AI Max everywhere, and its ads liaison answered the Max CPC question

Microsoft Advertising's AI Max reached all accounts this week: keyword-free matching, real-time text customization, and final URL expansion with exclusions, arriving alongside the October 1 removal of Max CPC for new campaigns that we covered last week. Marketing O'Clock's August 24 episode led with it and raised two questions; Navah Hopkins of Microsoft answered both in the comments, which is where the useful detail lives. AI Max is a campaign-level setting, and ad-group-level settings (scheduling, location targeting, negatives, audiences) survive when it's on. And Max CPC isn't disappearing wholesale: it "will remain for eCPC, Target Impression Share, Portfolio bidding," because "not every brand can hit the conversion thresholds needed for conversion-based bidding to be effective."

That's a more nuanced position than Google's, which is why it belongs on the record. Microsoft is removing the manual ceiling where it overrides conversion goals and keeping it where accounts lack the conversion data to bid on. Bing also shipped an AI Max search-term landing page report with twelve default columns, and is testing renaming its Copilot Search answer box to "AI Overview," adopting the competitor's vocabulary because comprehension beats differentiation.
