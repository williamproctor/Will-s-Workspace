## What leads

**News lead:** the midterms set a political-ad record, ran more on CTV than on cable, and carried $187M of AI-made ads under a patchwork of state rules, and then the result turned on AI's power bills. AdImpact's final tally, reported by The Current on Wednesday Nov 4, was $11.87B, above 2024's $11.2B presidential-year total, with CTV at about 22% ($2.6B) and broadcast about $5.8B; Adweek had CTV ahead of cable for the first time in a midterm. On Magnite's call that evening, Michael Barrett said political contribution ex-TAC would be "over $40 million for 2026 versus the $15 million previously projected", which "is more than double the last presidential election and nearly 4x the last midterm cycle". Wesleyan Media Project counted 289 AI-generated ads with more than $187M behind them, about 90% of it Republican, and only 27% carried disclaimers through mid-October (USA Today). Erika Franklin Fowler: "There's no federal regulation of AI in advertising, so what we have instead is a patchwork of state laws that apply to mostly state candidates across the country." YouTube requires disclosure "when an election ad contains synthetic or digitally altered people or events" (CNBC), but Ken Paxton's AI video of James Talarico ran as organic posts on YouTube and X "with a three-second disclosure flashing in the middle". Then Democrats flipped seven Senate seats, at least 39 House seats and five governorships and vowed to rein in data-center power costs (Reuters, Nov 5). CNBC counted 41 data-center ballot measures in 10 states, and the Nasdaq fell 5.56% on Thursday. That day, OpenAI published a "National Community Compact": "We'll pay the full cost of the electricity we use and the infrastructure needed to serve us." Two things are new for marketers. Political money is now CTV money; that it leaves CTV after Nov 3, ahead of the holiday season, is our inference. And the companies building AI ad businesses now face voters over what AI costs.

## Angle candidates
1. "Set ChatGPT's Target CPC from your own numbers before the cap goes." (From Nov 9, new ChatGPT campaigns use Target CPA or Target CPC, which "lets our system bid above or below your target in individual auctions"; Max Bid and Max CPC retire Jan 1, 2027. Kaiser and Schulze's parity method gives the benchmark: your revenue per paid ChatGPT session against your revenue per paid-search dollar. Their sample's parity was $0.26 against a public $1.72 and OpenAI's $3–5 starting guidance. Keep one capped campaign running until Jan 1 for comparison, and judge on incrementality, not last click. Window: Nov 9 to Jan 1, 2027.)
2. "Find out where Google will stamp 'AI' on your ads." (Visible overlays now apply to designated assets in campaigns targeting the EU, India, New York, California, South Korea and Vietnam. Assets from Google's "fully-automated features" may be labeled "on your behalf", those labels "cannot be overwritten", and the setting "doesn't guarantee your compliance". Inventory which campaigns use auto-generated assets in those places, and decide whether to label your own AI-edited assets before Google does. Window: now, since the page gives no effective date; Pennsylvania's rule starts Dec 21. Not legal advice.)
3. "Fund your brand Search campaign: Performance Max may hand brand queries back." (Google's help page now says Performance Max "may also filter out navigational or brand-specific search queries", and tells advertisers to give brand Search campaigns "sufficient budget and high-quality, eligible ads" or to add Search Themes to Performance Max. There's no start date or definition of a brand query. Compare brand impressions in each campaign before and after early November. Window: now, before Black Friday (Nov 27).)
4. "Home-services marketers: AI Mode now books through Angi, Thumbtack, Housecall Pro and Jobber." (Stein: "It's available now in the U.S."; ServiceTitan is "coming soon". Jobber's and Housecall Pro's customers pay no lead fee, but non-partners can appear too (homepros), and Google hasn't said how providers are chosen. Check which platform your business is on, and what AI Mode shows for "[service] near me" in your area. Window: now, in the US.)
5. "Read what Facebook's AI pages say about your brand." (The fb-answers directory has 6M+ indexed pages and, by Semrush's estimate, about 40M monthly visits; the pages are AI-written with "no editorial oversight" (Westbrook), visible in AI Overviews and AI Mode, and blocked to OpenAI's, Anthropic's and Perplexity's crawlers per bediscoverable's reading of the robots.txt.
