# Industry track — week of October 5–11, 2026

## Agents at the door: a protocol for personal agents, and the sites that still block them

- **Personal Agent Protocol (Tue Oct 6; draft and design partners Fri Oct 9):** Sierra co-founders Bret Taylor and Clay Bavor announced "an open standard Meta and Sierra are developing along with industry partners at Genesys, Instinct, Rocket, Shopify, Stripe, and Walmart that defines how personal agents interact with businesses." How it works, per Sierra: "consumers decide what access to give their personal agents, and companies set parameters for what those agents can do." Sessions are built on OAuth; the customer decides "whether the agent has read-only or write access"; the company chooses the route (its website, its APIs "built on standards such as MCP and OpenAPI," or its own agent). Genesys CEO Tony Bates, in Sierra's post: "Personal AI is creating a new front door to the enterprise." Meta's announcement the same day names a different list (Genesys, NiCE, Decagon, Rocket, Shopify, Stripe, Walmart) and is aimed at businesses that block agents: "Turning away a personal agent means turning away the customer behind it. We'd rather work with businesses to address the underlying concerns than see them lose that customer," and "There is a path that is good for users and good for businesses. It starts with clear norms, gives businesses more predictable control, and evolves toward a more efficient way for agents to work together." (Meta's post via TechCrunch; Meta's own URL not surfaced.) CMSWire summarizes Meta's two conduct tests for Muse (a "one honest person" test, and whether the system would still work if every Muse user made the same request) and says sign-ins, reservations and purchases proceed only with explicit user approval. Friday Oct 9, Sierra published the draft ("Poppy") and 35 design partners that "will participate in the design process": Adyen, Atomic, Bank of America, BBVA, Chime, Cigna, Cloudflare, Comcast, DIRECTV, ElevenLabs, FOX, Gap Inc., GEICO, Hertz, Insurify, Klaviyo, Liberty Mutual, Mastercard, Nordstrom, Notion, Okta, OneSignal, OpenAI, PayPal, Plaid, SiriusXM, Synchrony, Target, United Airlines, Venmo, Visa, Wells Fargo, Zapier, Zendesk, 1Password. The spec: "This is draft version 0.1. Any part of it can change before a stable version, including in ways that aren't backward compatible." A company publishes `/.well-known/poppy.json` to tell agents how to start sessions and sign in, and which APIs and company agent it offers. Amazon and Google are on none of the lists. Origins: [Sierra, Oct 6](https://sierra.ai/blog/introducing-personal-agent-protocol) · [Sierra, Oct 9](https://sierra.ai/blog/poppy) · [spec (draft 0.1)](https://personalagentprotocol.org/docs/spec) · [TechCrunch (Meta's quotes), Oct 6](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/06/the-next-hurdle-for-ai-agents-getting-websites-to-let-them-in/) · [CMSWire, Oct 6](https://www.cmswire.com/contact-center/genesys-joins-sierra-meta-on-open-standard-for-personal-ai-agents-01/)
