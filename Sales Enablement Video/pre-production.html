02 — The Script

Polished Draft

Reworked from the initial brief. Cut the throat-clearing, reframed content as an infrastructure problem, and rewired the close from a CTA to a personal statement. ~195 words at 150 wpm gives Matthew about 75 seconds with room for the pauses that actually matter.

Act 1 — The Problem ~18 sec

The thing everyone knows but nobody says out loud.

VISUAL: Matthew on camera, clean environment — not a studio, more like a well-lit workspace. The framing says peer, not presenter. Cut to abstract data visualizations: content volume curves plateauing, headcount growing linearly while output stays flat.
Matthew You already know organic content is your highest-leverage growth channel. That's not the insight. The insight is that doing it well — really well, at the scale your business actually needs — is one of the hardest infrastructure problems in marketing. The talent is scarce. The systems are complex. And if you try to build it all yourself, you're looking at years before anything compounds.
Act 2 — The Engine ~35 sec

What it actually looks like when everything clicks.

VISUAL: Transition to Atlas interface — workflow configurations, content pipelines in motion, artifact generation. Data flowing through the system. Brief flashes of client growth curves. The visuals should feel alive, like watching an engine run.
Matthew I ran TechCrunch for ten years. I know what great content operations look like at scale — and I know what was always missing. Better tooling. Tighter data loops. More leverage per person. That's what we built.
VISUAL: Deeper into Atlas. Bespoke workflow being configured. Agentic pipeline processing data. Side-by-side of raw input vs. finished content output.
Matthew Atlas is our content engine. For every client, we build bespoke workflows — agentic pipelines that process your market data, your competitive landscape, your audience signals — and turn them into content that actually performs. But the technology is only half of it.
VISUAL: Quick montage — strategy sessions, editorial reviews, performance dashboards showing upward trajectories. The human layer on top of the machine.
Matthew We've done this with hundreds of companies. Every engagement teaches the system something new — which strategies actually drive growth, which formats convert, which audiences respond. That's not something you get from prompting ChatGPT. It's an engine built on real results, run by people who make it smarter every single week.
Act 3 — The Invitation ~12 sec

The hand-off.

VISUAL: Back to Matthew on camera. Warmer, more direct. A slight shift in energy — from explaining to inviting. He's no longer building a case; he's opening a door.
Matthew This is the thing I spent my whole career wanting to build. And now it exists. So — let's go deeper. Let me show you what this actually looks like for your business.

Script Notes — Changes from Draft

  • + "That's not the insight." The prospect sitting in this call already knows content matters. Spending 10 seconds telling them again is wasted time. This line skips past the obvious and gets to the actual problem — scale — in the first breath.
  • + "Infrastructure problem." Most content agencies position around creativity. We're positioning around engineering. "Hardest infrastructure problem in marketing" signals to VP-level buyers that we understand the actual constraint — it's a systems challenge, not a talent acquisition problem.
  • + "What was always missing." This is the move that makes Matthew's TechCrunch years work for the pitch instead of just next to it. He's not listing credentials — he's saying "I ran the thing and it still wasn't enough." That gap between TechCrunch and GrowthX is the product's origin story in six words.
  • + ChatGPT in one line. Every prospect is thinking it. Addressing it in a single declarative sentence — then immediately moving on — is the strongest possible move. Dwelling on it signals insecurity. Stating it and continuing signals that the answer is so obvious it doesn't warrant discussion.
  • + "My whole career." The original close was "That's why I'm here." Fine, but generic. "The thing I spent my whole career wanting to build" puts personal stakes on the table. It's a bet — Matthew is saying this is the culmination of everything he's done. That lands differently than a transition line.

05 — Music Direction

The Score

One texture, start to finish. Low resonant synth that sits underneath everything without ever calling attention to itself. The kind of sound that makes a room feel like it costs more than it does. If the prospect notices the music, it's too loud.

Musical Feel

  • One consistent texture from start to finish — no dramatic arc, no big build, no drop
  • Low, resonant synth pad as the foundation — warm but serious, like a hum underneath everything
  • Subtle sub-bass presence that you feel more than hear — chest resonance, not bass drop
  • Sparse, deliberate high-register touches — a single clean tone every few bars, almost like a signal
  • Gentle rhythmic pulse buried deep in the mix — not a beat, more like a heartbeat or a clock
  • The piece should feel like it was already playing before the video started and continues after it ends

Reference & Constraints

  • Think: The ambient bed under a high-end tech keynote — not the keynote music itself
  • Also: Brian Eno's "Music for Airports" meets modern brand film underscore
  • Mood board: Apple product reveal ambiance, Stripe brand film, Vercel conference opener
  • No melody that you could hum — this is texture, not tune
  • No crescendo, no climax, no emotional swell
  • Must sit completely under dialogue without competing — mix at 50% under voice
  • The energy is "the future is already here" — calm certainty, not excitement
  • Should feel expensive and considered, like every frequency was chosen on purpose

06 — Director's Notes

Tone & Execution

The Interrotron

Matthew looks directly into the lens the entire time. Not off-camera-to-an-interviewer. Not the "45-degree thought-leader gaze." Straight into the viewer's eyes, every shot, no exceptions. This is an interrotron setup — a teleprompter rig that lets him read or reference the interviewer's face while the camera captures direct eye contact. The effect: it feels like Matthew is sitting across from you personally, not performing for a camera crew. Every wide shot and every punch-in CU maintains the same axis. The variation comes from framing and energy, never from gaze.

Tone & Performance

The trap is performing authority. Matthew already has it — TechCrunch, a decade of editorial operations, the kind of resume that doesn't need a LinkedIn headline. So the direction is simple: talk quieter than you think you should. One person across a table. The more he sounds like he's explaining something to a colleague over coffee, the more the C-suite prospect leans in. Any trace of "sales video energy" and credibility evaporates.

The ChatGPT Line

Every prospect in the room is thinking it. The smart move is to say it once, plainly, and keep going. "That's not something you get from prompting ChatGPT" — stated as a fact, the way you'd say "water is wet." No emphasis. No dramatic pause after. The confidence is in not treating it like an objection. If we dwell on it, we signal that it's a real threat. If we mention it and immediately move to the next sentence, we signal that the answer is so obvious it barely warrants airtime.

Visual Language

Pull directly from the website. Warm cream, near-black, clean sans-serif type. The video should look like a page on growthx.com came to life, not like we hired a separate production company with a different mood board. When we cut to Atlas or data visualizations, keep the palette to three colors max. The moment you add a neon gradient or a floating 3D UI mockup, you're in "generic SaaS explainer" territory and the trust Matthew just built gets undercut by the visuals.

The Hand-Off

Matthew's final line is a stage direction for the sales rep. "Let me show you what this actually looks like for your business" — then the video ends and the rep is already sharing their screen. No end card. No logo hold. No 3-second fade to black. The transition should feel like Matthew just passed the conversation to his colleague sitting in the room. If there's a visible seam between the video ending and the demo starting, the whole thing is less effective.

Pacing

The math: 150 words/minute is about 185 words in 75 seconds. The script runs ~195 words, which gives Matthew about 10 words of breathing room — enough for two meaningful pauses. The first one matters most: the half-beat after "That's not the insight" in Act 1, where the prospect recalibrates from "I've heard this before" to "okay, this is different." The second is the shift into Act 3, where his energy changes from explaining to inviting. Those two micro-moments are the difference between a video that converts and one that gets politely watched.

What This Video Is Not

Marcel's videos already cover the origin story and the market thesis. This video assumes the prospect already understands why content matters — because the rep just spent 10 minutes establishing that. So we skip Act 1 of the broader sales narrative entirely. This video is Act 2: here's the system, here's why it's different, here's the person who built it. Act 3 is the live demo that follows immediately after. If this video tries to re-explain the problem from scratch, it's redundant and it wastes the rep's setup.
