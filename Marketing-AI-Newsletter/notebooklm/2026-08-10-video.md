### Exact match isn't exact anymore, and the migration just became mandatory

Two stories, one control problem. First the data: in a July 24 LinkedIn post, smec's Mike Ryan analyzed 383 million ecommerce Search impressions in accounts running AI Max and found more than 25% of exact-match keyword impressions were expanded by AI Max's search-term matching rather than matched literally. The expanded share rose from roughly zero in early 2025 to about 29% by July 2026, and the reporting surface can't separate expanded from literal traffic inside a single keyword row. Google reports a 15% average conversion lift for its AI campaigns; independent tests have repeatedly measured lower ROAS for AI Max traffic than for traditional match types. Both numbers can coexist because nobody outside Google can run the split.

Then the mandate: the August 5 email makes AI Max automatic from September 1 for campaigns using ACA or campaign-level broad match. Opting out means turning those settings off before the deadline. Exact match was the last precise control in keyword advertising; it's eroding from the inside in accounts that volunteered, and the September migration extends the mechanics to accounts that didn't.

### Agency holdcos found their AI business model: margin on tokens
