# Industry track — week of August 31–September 6, 2026

## FTC and 22 states sue Amazon over ad-auction pricing (Monday, Aug 31)

- Complaint (W.D. Wash.): since 2019 Amazon "secretly and systematically overcharged its approximately 1.2 million advertising customers by manipulating the 'auctions' that it uses to set the price of ads." Mechanism: a hidden **"soft reserve price"** implemented via what an internal document called an **"invented auction participant"** — a manufactured bid advertisers had to beat — turning a marketed second-price auction into a first-price one; Sponsored Products advertisers charged their full winning bid close to **80% of the time**. Surcharges rose on peak days (Prime Day, holidays). Alleged extraction: **$20B+**. Internal 2024 discussion (head of Amazon Ads and Chief Digital Economist) quoted: a "clever non-transparent way to charge first price" was "an incredibly effective way to drive revenue." Amazon's April 2026 auctions page mentions reserve pricing but, per FTC, fails to disclose "the nature, scope, and extent."
- States: Alaska, Arizona, California, Colorado, Florida, Idaho, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maryland, Nebraska, New Jersey, New York, North Carolina, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, Vermont, Washington (bipartisan).
- Amazon (blog post, Aug 31): suit is "misguided," "fundamentally misunderstands how advertisers operate"; average CPC flat 2019–2024 while sales per click rose; advertisers "properly" informed. Amazon ads revenue $68B+ last year.
- Origins: [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/08/ftc-states-sue-amazon-over-secret-ad-surcharge-scheme) · [complaint PDF](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc%5Fgov/pdf/AmazonAds-Complaint.pdf) · [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/ftc-file-lawsuit-alleging-amazon-deceived-advertisers-wsj-reports-2026-08-31/) · [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/09/ftc-alleges-amazon-illegally-made-20-billion-by-rigging-billions-of-ad-auctions/) · [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/31/ftc-accuses-amazon-of-running-a-secret-ad-surcharge-scheme-in-new-lawsuit/).

## Google escapes an ad-tech breakup (Wednesday, Sept 2)

- Judge Leonie Brinkema (E.D. Va., 1:23-cv-00108) **rejected** the DOJ's structural remedies — divestiture of AdX, open-sourcing DFP's final auction logic, contingent divestiture of "DFP Remainder" — and **accepted "most" of the parties' behavioral remedies "as modified by this Court."** Memorandum opinion sealed 14 days for redactions; parties to file a joint proposed Final Judgment within 30 days. Behavioral proposals on the table included real-time bid data for rival publisher ad servers, ending Unified Pricing Rules, no first-look/last-look, non-discrimination toward third-party exchanges/servers. Second time Google avoided a breakup (Chrome, search case, last year). April 2025 liability ruling: illegal monopolies in publisher ad server and ad exchange markets; publishers (The Atlantic, USA Today, Vox, Penske) have filed damages suits.
- Google's Lee-Anne Mulholland: "We're very pleased the court rejected the DOJ's proposal to break apart tools that help small businesses reach new customers and grow." DOJ's Matthew Huppert had argued Google would "test boundaries every step of the way" under behavioral remedies; Google's Karen Dunn: "no evidence there would be a buyer" for AdX.
- Origins: [Court order (CourtListener PDF)](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.vaed.533508/gov.uscourts.vaed.533508.1857.0_1.pdf) · [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/antitrust/google-avoids-ad-exchange-sale-as-judge-orders-tech-integration) · [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/media/google-wont-be-forced-to-break-up-adtech-biz-even-after-monopoly-ruling/) · [The Current](https://www.thecurrent.com/ad-tech/google-avoids-ad-tech-breakup-monopoly-case) · [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/google-avoids-adtech-breakup-in-federal-judge-ruling-2026-9).
