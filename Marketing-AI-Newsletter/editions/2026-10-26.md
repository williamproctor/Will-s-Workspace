## The Conversation

### Three traffic studies found little AI-assistant traffic, and "GEO is a distraction" drew a week of rebuttals

On Monday, October 19, Kevin Indig's Growth Memo checked 12 sites for AI visits hiding in Direct and concluded, "I could not yet detect 'hidden' AI traffic without self-reported attribution like Eric and his team at Ramp." On Wednesday, Marie Haynes put ChatGPT at 0.62% of Google organic's traffic across 100 sites ("That's not a typo. 0.62%."); on the 55 that track conversions, ChatGPT converted at 13.8% and Google organic at 3.02%, but Google drove 96.5% of conversions. The SEO Works, a UK agency, found across 95 sites that "For every 100 conversions organic search delivered, AI referrals delivered 3.2."

Haynes posted on LinkedIn the same day, "I think GEO is a distraction. Google is the surface that's likely sending the most visitors to your business." Rand Fishkin bundled the three studies in a #5minutewhiteboard that evening, and Search Engine Roundtable's Thursday headline said they "Suggest AI Visibility & Search Not Worth Investing In" (SER's framing). Mike King of iPullRank replied Thursday, "All three studies measure AI Search as a performance channel, find that it's a weak one, and conclude that it doesn't matter. That is the category error". SparkToro's write-up, crediting King, called AI "more like a brand channel, a digital billboard if you will". Each study is its author's own sample, all three authors sell search services or subscriptions, and Search Engine Land notes Haynes "didn't specify exact reporting dates, industry mix, or how conversions were defined."

**Comment window:** open through 2027 planning, while AI-visibility budgets get set. In Haynes's sample, 45 of 100 sites tracked no GA4 key events, so nearly half can't say what any channel converts. Instrument conversions, ask new signups how they heard about you (Whitespark's Darren Shaw says ChatGPT is the top answer in its signup flow), and compare AI referrals with paid search too (The SEO Works puts AI at 0.67x paid search and 2.48x direct).

### ChatGPT's ad rewrites switch on by default, and Meta makes advertisers "solely responsible" for its AI's output

On Tuesday, October 20, Search Engine Roundtable published OpenAI's email to ChatGPT Ads advertisers: "Starting Friday, October 30, 2026, we'll enable creative enhancements for your new and existing self-serve campaigns." It adds, "When enabled, these ads can run automatically without requiring you to review or approve each version." One toggle covers rewritten headlines and descriptions, image resizing, destination-URL optimization and translation, according to PPC Land, which found OpenAI's help page giving Thursday, October 29 when it checked on Wednesday. On LinkedIn, Joel H. listed the risks, including "3. Destination URL swapping breaks deterministic attribution."

On Monday, CIO.com and Computerworld reported a clause in Meta's revised terms: AI features "may make mistakes and may not always produce accurate, complete, or suitable results," and customers "are solely responsible for and assume any and all risks associated with such AI features and any resulting content." Meta's liability is capped at the greater of $100 or the amount paid in the prior 12 months. On Thursday, Adweek reported advertisers pulling Meta ads that had been given unwanted AI "glow-ups"; Ryan Adams of Exverus said "many of the options are tucked away in hidden or collapsed menus," and "If every one of those isn't manually unchecked, the enhancements still apply." Meta says advertisers can turn off individual Advantage+ features, and Friday's delay (below) left the clause in place.

**Comment window:** open until October 29 for ChatGPT and November 20 for Meta. Opt out before October 29, the earliest date OpenAI has given, and check each campaign, since "campaign-specific settings will take priority over account defaults." Keep dated screenshots of every AI setting you switch off on both platforms, because Meta's clause puts "any and all risks" on the customer.

### Just Eat apologized and paused its AI menu photos after one turned soda bread into a soda

Diners started posting Just Eat's AI menu images on Monday, September 21, when "AI vs Soda Bread" in r/JustEatUK showed a breakfast whose soda bread had become a soda. "It shows how grossly AI can misinterpret an item and falsely advertise it (which I'm pretty sure goes against the UK consumer act??)," the poster wrote; the thread had 658 points when fetched. Every image carried an "AI-generated" label. The Independent first reported Just Eat's apology on Monday, October 19: "We've been trialling AI-enhanced images and as with any new technology, it isn't always quite right the first time. We're sorry for any inaccurate images that have been posted. We're in the process of removing them and have paused rolling this out while we further develop the tool to ensure it best serves our partners and customers." The BBC followed on Wednesday. On LinkedIn, Elly Cattermole described "bacon and eggs in a pint of coke" and added, "AI is a tool, not a replacement for human judgement." Reddit commenters disagreed about whether restaurants had opted in, and Just Eat's statement doesn't say how they were enrolled.

**Comment window:** open this week, while the trial is paused. The images were labeled "AI-generated," and the Reddit poster still called one false advertising, so for creative a platform generates on your behalf (the item above), have a person review it before it goes live.

## This Week in Marketing AI

### Meta's Q3 ad revenue rose 26%, and Zuckerberg expects businesses to pay a fee on Muse purchases

Meta reported third-quarter results after the close on Wednesday, October 21. Advertising revenue was $63.06 billion, up 26% from $50.08 billion, on 10% more ad impressions and a 14% higher average price per ad. GAAP operating margin fell to 20% from 40% after a $9.89 billion charge for Meta's agreement with 52 state attorneys general, and fourth-quarter revenue guidance is $73–76 billion.

On the call, CFO Susan Li said the GEM ranking model is "driving a 1.7% increase in conversions for offsite conversion campaigns and a 9.6% increase in app ad conversions," and that by Meta's estimate advertisers who adopted Business Assistant's recommendations "drove almost 5% more conversions at the same cost per conversion and increased their advertising investment by 9% compared with similar non-adopters." Of Meta's generative-AI creative tools, she said, "In Q3, nearly 10 million advertisers used at least one of these tools." Partnership ads "surpassed $15 billion in annual revenue run-rate"; Meta had put them on course for a $10 billion run rate when we covered them September 28.

Zuckerberg said, "In just a month and a half, more than 10 million people in the US and Canada have set up Muse personal agents," and that shopping is the most common use, "with more than a quarter of people who are using Muse engaging in this with an average purchase size over $200." On the business model, he said, "For most people, the product should be free and we will build commerce tools so we get paid a fee based on the transactions that we help facilitate. We expect this will be paid for by businesses." Meta hasn't published a fee. Zuckerberg also said Muse "is coming to Meta Glasses starting next month". Quotes follow the Meta-format call transcript hosted by MediaNama, and the Muse and Business Assistant figures are Meta's own.

### Meta pushed its new business terms to November 20 after publisher letters and talks with the CMA

Meta's Business Help Center said on Friday, October 23, "From November 20, 2026, updated versions of the Meta Commercial Terms and the Self-Serve Ad Terms will take effect." They had been due October 30 (covered here October 19), and Reuters and Fast Company both have a claim to the first report. A Meta spokesperson told Nieman Lab the date moved "to allow more time for us to explain to our partners and stakeholders what's changing and what's not — including how Meta uses web crawlers and the fact that content owners will still be able to use robots.txt to signal preferences." Nieman Lab found that "As of October 23, Meta has made no other changes to the terms aside from the implementation date. No versions of the terms include language about respecting robots.txt preferences." Section 5's AI clause (see The Conversation) applies from November 20 as written.

On Thursday, the News/Media Alliance, which Digiday says represents more than 2,000 publishers, wrote to Zuckerberg, and WAN-IFRA sent its own letter with five asks. The CMA said it "has been in urgent discussions with Meta this week on this important issue" and "will continue to scrutinize these terms," and the European Commission said it is committed to holding Meta to EU law, "including Article 102 TFEU and the Digital Markets Act." Neither has opened a case. Matt Topic of Loevy & Loevy told Nieman Lab, "Publishers don't need more PR statements or explanations. They need Meta to keep its hands off their copyrighted material and respect publishers' terms of service."

### Apple says Safari 27 blocks identity-resolution companies at the domain level

On Monday evening, October 19, WebKit's blog said, "In our iOS, iPadOS, macOS, and visionOS 27 releases, Safari and WebKit block network access to identity resolution infrastructure to prevent cross-site tracking." "The blocking is done on the domain level, meaning they can't just change the structure of the network request to work around the protection," the post says, and of the companies, "They are not themselves login providers or newsletter services, they're just there to track you." It also says, "First party personalization is not tracking, and is not what our technologies seek to prevent." Apple names no vendors and publishes no list.

It's Apple's first formal statement of the policy. WebKit bug 324771 had shown iOS 27 blocking nine domains, among them uidapi.com, id5-sync.com and rlcdn.com (covered here October 5), and The Trade Desk's adsrvr.org came off the list in the iOS 27.2 beta (covered here October 12). AdExchanger's James Hercher wrote up the post on Tuesday. We found no response from LiveRamp, UID2 or ID5 during the week.

## Common Threads

### Platforms edit ads by default, and Meta's terms put the risk on advertisers

OpenAI's creative enhancements "can run automatically without requiring you to review or approve each version," Meta's terms make customers "solely responsible" for what its AI features produce, and Adweek's sources pulled Meta ads its AI had altered. Just Eat removed AI menu images that carried an "AI-generated" label, and Meta says nearly 10 million advertisers used its generative-AI creative tools in Q3.

### AI referrals are small where measured, and the measuring tools are strained

Haynes counted ChatGPT at 0.62% of Google organic's traffic, The SEO Works counted 3.2 AI conversions for every 100 from organic, and Indig couldn't find AI visits hiding in Direct. Nozzle says 13.9% of the SERPs it judged since September 16 ignored part of the query, and Semrush puts AI Overviews on 88% of commercial keywords, the Google surface Fishkin called "another matter."

### Safari blocks identity-resolution domains as customer-data companies change hands

Apple blocks identity-resolution domains in Safari 27 and says "First party personalization is not tracking," and the FTC's proposed order would keep LiveRamp open to Publicis's rivals for five years. Salesforce agreed to buy Braze's customer-engagement platform at an enterprise value of about $5.66 billion, and Zuckerberg says buying something will mean "talking to their agent."

### Dates slipped, and platforms' own pages disagreed

Meta's terms now start November 20, and during the week OpenAI's email and help page gave October 30 and October 29 for ChatGPT's creative enhancements. Netflix cut its ad forecast over a "slightly slower than expected rollout of some ad product capabilities," and Google's Smart Bidding page now says learning "never lasts more than 7 days" while another Google page still lists "Setting change" as a learning status.

A business that keeps using Meta's products after November 20 accepts the crawl grant and an AI clause that makes it "solely responsible" for AI output. The explainer walks through the Advantage+ creative toggles Adweek's sources found in collapsed menus, the case for dated screenshots of what you switched off, and why the robots.txt assurance sits in a spokesperson's statement and in no version of the terms; label it as not legal advice. Window: before November 20.

### Before you fund GEO, find out what your site can measure

Haynes found 45 of her 100 sites tracking no GA4 key events, and The SEO Works found AI referrals converting at 0.67x paid search and 2.48x direct. The post covers instrumenting conversions, adding a "how did you hear about us" field (Shaw's evidence for ChatGPT), and, if AI visibility gets funded as a brand channel as King argues, measuring it like one. Window: 2027 planning, now.

### Safari 27 blocks identity-resolution domains; inventory the ID vendors in your tags

Apple blocks the domains across its 27 releases, publishes no list, and says first-party personalization is out of scope. The audit post lists the UID2, ID5, RampID and similar calls in a tag manager, tests them in Safari 27, and maps what falls back to first-party data. Window: now, before holiday measurement reads.

### Patch PHP sites now, and serve a 503 if one has to go offline

PHP shipped the PHP2Shell fixes on October 21, and Google's John Mueller says to serve a 503 from outside PHP (a CDN, or DNS pointed elsewhere) while you patch, since Google's docs say URLs returning 5xx errors are "eventually dropped." The checklist covers version, patch, the 503 fallback, and the date each managed site came back. Window: now, until every site is patched.
