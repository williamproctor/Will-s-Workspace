## What leads

**News lead:** Google expanded ads in AI Mode to 10 more countries on the same day it told investors AI Mode monetizes lower. On Wednesday Oct 28, Shashi Thakur wrote, "Now we're scaling ads in AI Mode in Australia, Canada, India, Kenya, Malaysia, New Zealand, Nigeria, Pakistan, the Philippines, and Singapore," with Brazil, Mexico and the UK planned for Q4 and "multiturn ads in the U.S." for follow-up questions. After the close, Alphabet reported Search & other revenue of $63.35B, up 12% from $56.57B, YouTube ads of $11.21B (+9.2%) and Google Cloud revenue of $30.71B (+103%), and Pichai said "our year-over-year query growth rate is the highest it’s been in the last five years." On the call, Philipp Schindler named FX "the single largest driver of Search deceleration relative to Q2", then said "the strong growth in AI Mode queries had an impact because of the lower monetization we were seeing in AI Mode" and that "the majority of AI Mode queries are incremental, creating more monetization opportunities over time." Google didn't say how large the gap is (SEL). Its claim that "click-through rates are already similar between sponsored and organic product list items within AI Mode" rests on internal US data for Aug 20–Sept 3. The help page sets the terms: existing Search, Shopping and Performance Max campaigns are "automatically eligible", advertisers can't "opt out of showing ads in them separately from Search", the AI explainers next to ads can't be written, edited, approved or opted out of, and results are reported "in aggregate" in standard campaign reports. For marketers, two things are new: AI Mode is now ad inventory in 11 countries (the US plus the 10), and Google itself says AI Mode has "lower monetization", with no control beyond the negative keywords and brand exclusions Search already had.

## Common-thread candidates
1. **Fewer clicks, and nobody agrees what a click is worth.** Reddit lost "more than 1 million referrals on average per week" in October and says AI Mode "does not drive traffic". Google says AI Mode brings "lower monetization" but that most of its queries "are incremental". Indig's Similarweb panel has US organic visits down 13.2% over two years while paid search rose 21%, and publishers lost 41.5%. Adobe has AI clicks to US retail converting 90% better than non-AI traffic. Shepard puts AI impressions at an estimated "~0.9% CTR", yet removing them cost his site organic clicks too, and Findrix found AI Mode linking only to Google in 46.5% of product answers (vendor data; links, not clicks). Each counts something different (referrals, modeled visits, sessions, impressions, links), so set them side by side only with labels. The 2026-10-26 edition's thread was that the AI channel is small where it can be measured; this week the traffic losses showed up on the companies' own earnings calls.
2. **The default is on, and the disclosure is yours.** Meta's product browsing went on in existing ads on Oct 28 and ChatGPT's creative enhancements on Oct 30, and OpenAI's wider desktop card "uses existing campaigns and creative, so no advertiser action is needed" (its email, via SER). Ads in AI Mode are "automatically eligible", and advertisers can't edit the AI explainers beside them. TikTok's new guide, per write-ups, leaves advertisers responsible for disclosure and claims; Pennsylvania requires a disclosure on deceptive synthetic content from Dec 21; and the CMA's agentic-AI page says "businesses are responsible for how they engage with consumers, regardless of whether that is through people or AI systems." The counterexamples: Microsoft's messaging guidelines (open pilot) and Google's text guidelines (beta) let advertisers write rules for the AI, and Meta paused its terms after publishers' letters and Schiff's demand, with the Oslo order and the CMA case following within two days. Continuity: 09-28 named default-on as the launch strategy, 10-19 dated the defaults, 10-26 added liability; this week the switches flipped.
3. **Every opt-out now has a visibility price.** Bing says choosing `DisallowExtended` or `Disallow` for grounding means "reduced brand visibility in AI experiences". On Google, the shared token becomes a Google-Extended disallow, which also takes a site out of grounding in Gemini Apps. Shepard opted out of Search's AI features and lost organic clicks. Reddit's answer to scraping is a login wall, while its two licensing deals "are approaching the end of their respective current terms". MBL says of Meta's terms, "There is no option to refuse. The only alternative is to delete the account and lose contact with Norwegian Facebook and Instagram users.", and its third demand was "Et nei skal ikke gi trafikkstraff" (our gloss: saying no mustn't cost traffic). The CMA's concern is content used "without those businesses getting a choice on if or how their content is used". Price each switch before you flip it.
4. **Agents vs destinations.** Jassy says outside agents aren't meaningfully affecting Amazon Ads and that "Alexa for Shopping is going to be the primary shopping agent customers use to shop Amazon." Ready says "shopping is not a task to be automated". Schindler wants AI to take "the grunt work" and calls agentic experiences "clearly additive". Huffman wants Reddit to be "a direct destination". Lores has PayPal "collaborating with Meta and other AI companies to integrate PayPal into AI for personal agents", and OpenAI's dot "will also text you proactively". On the buy side, Mimica says the "majority of the work of a media buyer lives between the platforms." Each company is arguing that the agent should be its own or that its destination still matters, and the UK's regulators say they're coordinating "as the use of personal AI agents grows".

Window: before Dec 15 (Bing's temporary grounding preference) and Feb 1, 2027 (Microsoft starts honoring the preferences).)
4. "Plan for Reddit behind a login." (Reddit will test login requirements in "one or more international markets" starting this quarter, then "some form of logged-in restrictions globally, including in the United States", and its two licensing deals "are approaching the end of their respective current terms". Track Reddit-sourced citations in AI answers as their own line; Findrix's 35.6% of ChatGPT's US answers and Ahrefs' earlier 1 in 43 come from different samples, so don't present them as a trend. Window: this quarter, before the tests start.)
5. "Pennsylvania's AI-ad disclosure law starts Dec 21." (The Artificial Intelligence Deceptive Advertising Act covers synthetic content used "in a manner that creates a false consumer perception about the nature, origin or authenticity of the goods or services being advertised". The disclosure must state that the ad "contains content that was modified or generated using artificial intelligence", be "Displayed in the first instance", appear in the same medium, and be hard to remove where feasible. Content that is evidently fictional, parody or "limited to text" is exempt; the fine is up to $3,000, enforced by the Attorney General or district attorneys, with no private right of action. Inventory holiday creative; whether variations a platform generates count as the advertiser's is a question for counsel. Not legal advice. Window: before Dec 21.)
