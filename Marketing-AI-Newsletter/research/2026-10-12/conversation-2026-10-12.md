## 1. HubSpot cut nearly 660 jobs and said AI wasn't the reason

**What happened:** Tuesday Oct 6, CEO Yamini Rangan's memo to all employees, posted on HubSpot's site ("Updated: October 06, 2026"): "We have decided to reduce the size of our team by ~7% and will be saying goodbye to nearly 660 HubSpotters." The memo ties the cut to strategy: "Over the past year, we have shifted our strategy from building software that helps customers grow to delivering outcomes for them with AI. That shift is transforming product, pricing and how we serve our customers." Three changes: organize around customer outcomes ("Customers don’t think in Hubs and features"; teams for generate demand, win deals, delight customers, scale growth), a flatter organization with fewer layers, agile teams with clear ownership. Then a section headed "What is this not about": "This is not driven by AI-related efficiencies. We believe in a world where AI helps make us more productive and we will continue to invest to make that happen." And: "This is not simply a cost-cutting exercise." Severance: "20 weeks of base pay + 1 week per year of service, up to 30 weeks." HubSpot's 8-K: board approved the plan Oct 1; $65M–$75M in charges; substantially complete by the end of the first quarter of fiscal 2027, "subject to local law and consultation requirements." LinkedIn News (Lucia Maffei) framed the cut as targeting middle managers and recalled August's "largest single-day stock drop in over 12 years."

## 2. Rank trackers went haywire, and vendors said Google was feeding them fake results

**Timing note:** straddle. The tracking problems started in September: per Nozzle, requests began failing Sept 13 and the first poisoned pages appeared Sept 20; per Morningscore, truncated results began at 22:10 UTC on Sept 24, the day the September spam update started. The in-window part is the explanation and the argument: SISTRIX's post (Oct 7), Morningscore's account (Oct 8), practitioner posts (Oct 7–10), and Nozzle's study, dated Oct 10 with data through Oct 9. Search Engine Roundtable's Oct 12 report says Nozzle's Derek Perkins published it "Early this morning"; Nozzle's page is dated Oct 10. Use Oct 10 and state the conflict.

**What happened:** All week, SEOs posted that their tools disagreed with each other and with Google, while trying to read the spam update that finished Oct 8. Then the vendors explained. SISTRIX (Oct 7, Johannes Beus): since September 2026 Google returns results for some queries that "look like genuine search results but contain different websites"; on-demand updates are off; SISTRIX will focus on the top 10. Morningscore (Oct 8, Karsten Madsen): for almost six days its tracker recorded truncated results as real drops, and he apologized. Nozzle's "Poisoned Google SERPs" study (Oct 10) put a number on it: 8.0% of judged SERPs since Sept 13 came back "poisoned," against a 0.17% baseline; 24.9%, 26.7% and 27.0% on Sept 27–29; roughly 5% to 11% a day since Oct 1. On poisoned pages only 20.7% of organic sites carried over (88.5% normally), and the tracked site was missing from the top 10 on 58.6% of them (2.7% normally); the usual sites returned within three days on 77.9% of affected pages. Ads were kept 83.7% of the time vs 70.6% at baseline. Sample: 27.5M SERPs collected Sept 13–Oct 9, 17.8M judged, 49 countries. No Google statement found.

## 3. Anthropic wrote "don't seed AI answers" into Claude's rules

**What happened:** Thursday Oct 8, Anthropic published its 2026 Usage Policy update, effective Nov 12. The announcement describes a new section, "Do Not Engage in Deceptive Campaigns or Artificial Activity, which applies to deceptive activity of any kind (whether political or commercial)." The post doesn't mention search engines. The policy text does: Claude may not be used to "Manipulate the sources from which search engines or AI systems draw answers by seeding them with content that misrepresents its origin, authorship, or independence (e.g., networks of sites posing as unaffiliated sources corroborating the same claims)". The same section bars using Claude to "Distribute content through networks of fake or ostensibly independent outlets, websites, or accounts to hide that it comes from a common source," to "Build, improve, maintain, or contribute to tools, systems, or infrastructure designed to facilitate or enable deceptive campaigns...," and to "Market, sell, or solicit funding for any of the above as a product or service". Search Engine Journal's Matt G. Southern flagged the search-engine clause Friday Oct 9; the GEO corner of LinkedIn picked it up over the weekend.
