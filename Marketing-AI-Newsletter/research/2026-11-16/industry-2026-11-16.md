# Industry track — week of November 9–15, 2026

## Black Friday's AI forecast: Salesforce says AI will drive $75.8B of Cyber Week sales, while its own survey finds shoppers won't hand agents the card

**Timing note:** in window. Salesforce published "AI-first holiday" on Thu Nov 12. Its survey has no fieldwork dates. Adobe published nothing new in window; its holiday forecast is dated Sept 28 (before the window) and isn't used. TikTok Shop's Nov 11–30 campaign dates were seen only in secondary sources. Black Friday is Nov 27 and Cyber Monday Nov 30 (post-window).

- **The forecast (Salesforce, Nov 12):** "Salesforce proprietary research shows that AI is projected to drive $75.8 billion in Cyber Week spending, up 13% from 2025. AI-driven recommendations are expected to influence more than 1 in 5 holiday orders. Traffic flowing to ecommerce sites from AI tools has more than doubled, up 104% year over year." Shoppers arriving through AI referrals are "more than two times as likely to convert as typical ecommerce shoppers". Shoppers who use a brand's shopper agent have "an 8% higher average order value" and buy 23% more items, and "Brands that deploy AI shopping agents see seven times faster sales growth than brands without them." Caila Schwartz (Head of Agentic Commerce Shopper Insights): "AI is no longer a niche tool just for tech-savvy shoppers," And: "My advice before Cyber Week is simple: Be the brand that AI can find and humans can trust," and "Shoppers are starting with AI this season, so make sure your product data is accurate, your prices and inventory are current, and your own agent is ready to answer the hard questions." Origins: [Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/ai-first-holiday/)
- **The survey (same release; more than 2,500 shoppers in the US, UK, Canada and Australia):** almost 20% say AI is their first stop and 74% trust its recommendations; 30% of AI shoppers say the tools frequently introduce them to brands they didn't know, and 11% would skip a purchase if an assistant advised against it. 31% have tried a personal agent; Meta Muse is the most adopted (12% of respondents) and the most recognized, followed by Grok Bot. But only 19% of personal-agent users have used one at checkout, 9% give an agent unrestricted financial access and 36% set up a separate card. 73% aren't comfortable sharing payment information with an agent, and 49% aren't interested at all. Among agent users, 27% say an agent bought something too expensive, 22% had an agent blocked, and only 32% report no major errors. Origins: [Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/ai-first-holiday/)
- **Where the forecast meets this week's platform news:** OpenAI's Business Agents for US ecommerce businesses, with Shopify merchants integrated through Shopify Catalog (Nov 10; conversation item 1); ads while ChatGPT renders images (Nov 10) and ChatGPT's regional targeting for product feed campaigns (Nov 12; conversation item 3); Google's core update running to about Nov 24, so its usual post-update comparison week falls on Black Friday (SEJ; conversation item 2).

**Claims to hold precisely:** $75.8B is a Salesforce projection, and the release doesn't state its geographic scope or define "AI-driven"; don't call it a US figure. "AI traffic up 104%" is Salesforce's own measurement on the sites it sees. The survey figures are self-reported, from more than 2,500 shoppers in four countries, with no fieldwork dates. "Seven times faster sales growth" has no base in the release. The 8% higher order value is for shoppers who used a brand's shopper agent, measured against "the average order", not a brand-level result. Quartz's write-up of earlier Salesforce data compared 6.2% growth for brands with agents against 3.9% without ("59% higher"); that's a different claim from "seven times", so don't put the two side by side without both definitions. Meta Muse's 12% is adoption among respondents, not Meta data. Nothing here measures Black Friday itself.

## Courts, regulators, policy
- **Anthropic's usage policy bans seeding AI answers with disguised sources (effective Thu Nov 12; announced Oct 8)** — a follow-up line in analysis story 6; the 2026-10-12 edition covered the announcement and quoted the source-seeding clause. The PDF is headed "Usage Policy Effective Date: November 12, 2026". Under "Do Not Engage in Deceptive Campaigns or Artificial Activity", users may not: "Manipulate the sources from which search engines or AI systems draw answers by seeding them with content that misrepresents its origin, authorship, or independence (e.g., networks of sites posing as unaffiliated sources corroborating the same claims)"; "Distribute content through networks of fake or ostensibly independent outlets, websites, or accounts to hide that it comes from a common source"; "Build, improve, maintain, or contribute to tools, systems, or infrastructure designed to facilitate or enable deceptive campaigns..."; or "Market, sell, or solicit funding for any of the above as a product or service".
