## What leads

**News lead:** Meta put every business's website in its crawl terms. On Thursday, Nieman Lab reported that Meta's new Commercial Terms, effective Oct 30, authorize Meta to crawl sites "associated with" any business that uses its products and to use that content to "train" its products. The only alternative offered is to "stop all access or use of our Products for business or commercial purposes." Late Thursday, US time, Jason Kint started an X thread and wrote on Bluesky: "So every biz. Read that again." On Friday, Andy Stone called the reports "false" on X and said the update was about pulling "accurate information about products for sale on third-party websites", and the UK's News Media Association called it a "shameless landgrab attempt". That afternoon Meta removed "train" from the US-English text and limited the uses to "advertising and business products". The crawl grant, the test credentials and the "associated with" scope stayed, the contract still doesn't mention robots.txt, and the UK-English and French texts still said "train" when checked on Sunday. For marketers the news isn't the word. Advertisers had already granted crawl rights for "research and training" under the ad terms (SEJ). The new part is the trigger: from Oct 30 a business that only runs a Facebook Page is covered too. Editor's note: Relevant Audience found the UK-English text without "train" on Oct 20.

## Common-thread candidates
1. **The default moved, and this week brands paid for it.** TikTok's auto-generated assets ran under the National Trust's name until it paused everything. Meta adds product browsing to existing ads on Oct 28 unless you opt out. Meta's terms bind any business that keeps using its products after Oct 30. Google tells Search advertisers who use the Display Network to set it to "Low priority" with a 50% cap by February 2027 "to maintain your existing configuration." The 2026-09-28 edition named default-on as the launch strategy; the new facts are the cost (a charity's whole TikTok program paused) and the calendar (Oct 28, Oct 30, February).
2. **Attribution runs on a tag someone else controls.** For a week, ChatGPT's links lost the `utm_source` tag the channel is measured by, and the visits landed in Direct. Google added inline links but no click data. Universal Cart reports Buy clicks and transfers, not saves, and SEJ couldn't tell whether in-cart Shopify purchases fire analytics. Muse doesn't identify itself to the sites it shops. Amex wants intent logged before the agent buys. The platforms build the measurement layer for AI traffic, and this week they changed it in four places.
3. **Who gets to read the web, and on whose terms.** Meta wrote crawling into a contract that every business accepts by using its products. Google pays more than 400 publications under license for "enhanced content". DataDome counts Meta as half of all AI-agent requests on its network. The Commission is assessing Google's DMA changes and has said it will "seek the views of market operators and third parties". Access to content was set this week by contract, by license, by bot management and by regulators.
4. **The source beats the summary.** Ahrefs: "ChatGPT seems to be moving from sites that summarize information to the sources that produce it." 84.4% of factual answers cite an official source. Google is testing an "official places" block at the top of AI answers. Konitzny says GPT-6 "is becoming more critical of claims made by listicles and comparison pages." Semrush shows two supermarkets equally mentioned but 581 places apart on citations. And the LinkedIn collage was nine copies of one complaint. The first-hand page gets cited; the copy doesn't.
