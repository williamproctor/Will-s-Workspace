## The Conversation

### OpenAI's Business Hub shows ChatGPT mentions and clicks, and NoGood counted 1 Hub click against 1,020 GA4 sessions

OpenAI launched Businesses in ChatGPT in beta on Tuesday, November 10; a screenshot of its "Claim your business in ChatGPT" page had surfaced on Friday, November 6. A verified business sees Insights for the past 28 days, including "the number of mentions and website clicks a business receives," and the help page says, "For an online business, a mention counts when ChatGPT references the business's website. The brand name alone doesn't count..." Kevin Indig called the Hub "a sort of Search Console for ChatGPT," and SEL's Barry Schwartz wrote, "It is amazing OpenAI is giving us click data, a metric Google and Microsoft refuse to offer in its AI reporting tools."

Comparing the Hub with GA4, NoGood's Mostafa ElBermawy found 65 mentions and 1 click against 1,020 ChatGPT sessions (windows offset by a day) and called the product "a business profile and not a search console" (he discloses that his company, Goodie AI, supplied NoGood's crawler data). Previsible counted 1,834 mentions and 12 clicks against 32 GA4 sessions, and SEJ reported 353,356 mentions and 2,663 clicks for itself, warning that "A recorded click isn't a confirmed visit". Under Indig's post, Tim Lane wrote that "the more famous you are, the more Business Hub might undercount you," and Profound, which sells AI-visibility tracking, says brand names appear without a site reference "nearly 50% of the time" in its data.

**Comment window:** open now; the first full 28-day window since launch closes in early December. Log the ratio of Hub clicks to GA4 sessions each month as the instrument's calibration, and if you're a big brand, check whether ChatGPT cites your own pages.

### SEOs read the core update through split rank trackers, an under-logged AI report and a mid-rollout model change

On Wednesday, November 11, a day into the rollout, Glenn Gabe posted on X, "With Google poisoning the SERPs for AI scrapers and now SEO tools, it might be hard to gauge visibility drops/surges accurately." He "ran the numbers for ~1000 sites across a few verticals so far," posted Ahrefs and Semrush charts of the same sites ("Notice any differences there between ahrefs and semrush? lol.") and called Search Console data "the source of truth (for now)" (text as AEO Sherpa quotes it). The day before, Lily Ray had posted on LinkedIn, "For the past couple weeks, I've received more inquiries than any time I can remember from companies recently receiving manual actions," and wondered whether that was connected to the update. Google hasn't linked the two, or the update and Hydrogen.

After Hydrogen arrived on Thursday, Marie Haynes wrote that "there's less need for searchers to click through to websites if Search can now produce charts and images," a prediction, since no Hydrogen click data has been published. Lawrence Hitches advised, "My read: optimise more for the query fan-out." Under Google's LinkedIn announcement, Aristide D. wrote for advertisers that in AI Mode "those longer questions go to broad match, AI Max, Shopping and PMax... So if my CTR shifts after Nov 12, this is the first thing I'll check before touching the ads."

**Comment window:** open through the rollout (about November 24) and the December comparisons. The ranking system and the answer model changed in the same week the AI report under-logged, so read Search Console clicks against your own analytics before trusting any one tracker.

### ChatGPT Ads' target bidding went live November 9, and one advertiser said OpenAI "silently rejected" 3,930 pixel events

Since Monday, November 9, new ChatGPT conversion campaigns bid to a Target CPA and new click campaigns to a Target CPC, while existing campaigns keep Max Bid and Max CPC until January 1, 2027 (we covered the announcement last week). George Prodanov's LinkedIn summary that morning was "Your cap becomes an average", and OpenAI's help page says "Individual clicks may cost more or less than your target."

On Tuesday, Ian McGavin wrote, "When we launched ChatGPT Ads on our own account, OpenAI silently rejected 3,930 of our pixel events." That's one account's experience, and OpenAI hasn't commented, but its help page says target bidding uses "available conversion signals" and warns, "Setup alone does not establish that usable conversion signals are being received." Thursday's weekly email to advertisers (via SER) renamed Maximize Results "intelligent bidding," repeated that "Actual costs may be higher or lower than your target," and added regional targeting and multi-condition product filters for product campaigns. In the API, OpenAI recommends the new target fields from November 18, after which legacy fields used to create a campaign "will select Target CPA or Target CPC".

**Comment window:** open until January 1, 2027, when the caps retire for every campaign. Before moving a capped campaign to Target CPA, reconcile the pixel's accepted events against your own orders for the same days, since the system bids toward the conversions it receives.

## This Week in Marketing AI

### Google started a core update, put Gemini 4 Hydrogen in AI Mode and under-logged AI impressions for November 9–11

Google's Search Status Dashboard logged "the November 2026 core update, which applies globally and to all languages" on Tuesday, November 10, adding, "The rollout may take up to 2 weeks to complete." It's the third core update of 2026 (SEJ). On Thursday, Google brought Gemini 4 Hydrogen to Search, calling it "the biggest model upgrade to Search yet." Robby Stein, Google's VP of product for Search, posted, "It's now the new default model in AI Mode & being used for your hardest questions in AI Overviews"; the default and AI Overviews claims are his, not the Search post's, and Google hasn't defined the hardest questions. The post promises "a broader range of helpful links than before" and "more images, structured responses, comparison tables, and custom tools built for you," and SEJ noted that "Neither the post nor Stein's thread provide counts for links or sites."

The same Thursday, Search Console Help added, "A logging error caused a decrease in impressions on the Generative AI performance report in Search for data from November 9 - November 11, 2026. This issue affects data logging only." That's the Generative AI report, not the main Performance report, and Google hasn't said whether it will restore the data.

Google advises waiting at least a full week after a core update has rolled out before comparing, and SEJ, going by Google's two-week estimate, calculated that "the first full week after rollout would include U.S. Thanksgiving Day, Black Friday and Cyber Monday."

### Salesforce projects $75.8 billion in AI-driven Cyber Week spending and finds shoppers wary of agent checkout

Salesforce's holiday outlook on Thursday, November 12, says "AI is projected to drive $75.8 billion in Cyber Week spending, up 13% from 2025," and "AI-driven recommendations are expected to influence more than 1 in 5 holiday orders." The release doesn't say which markets the figure covers or define "AI-driven." By Salesforce's own measurement, traffic to ecommerce sites from AI tools is up 104% year over year, and shoppers arriving through AI referrals are "more than two times as likely to convert as typical ecommerce shoppers."

Its survey of more than 2,500 shoppers in the US, UK, Canada and Australia, with no fieldwork dates given, found that 31% have tried a personal agent, with Meta Muse the most adopted (12% of respondents). Of personal-agent users, 19% have used one at checkout, and 73% of shoppers "aren't comfortable giving a personal AI agent their payment information." Among agent users, 27% say an agent "bought something that was too expensive" and 22% had one blocked. Caila Schwartz, who leads agentic commerce shopper insights, advised, "Shoppers are starting with AI this season, so make sure your product data is accurate, your prices and inventory are current, and your own agent is ready to answer the hard questions." Salesforce sells commerce software, and its line that brands deploying AI shopping agents "see seven times faster sales growth" gives no base.

### The Trade Desk's revenue beat its own guide by 13%, and PubMatic, Zeta and Liftoff grew in the third quarter

The Trade Desk reported third-quarter revenue of $736 million on Monday, November 9, down 0.5% from a year earlier and 13% above its guide of "at least" $650 million, by our arithmetic. Its 10-Q says the quarter included "$15 million of incremental revenue earned from a strategic partner that we do not expect to recur." Net income fell to $38 million from $116 million, and it guided fourth-quarter revenue to at least $755 million. CEO Jeff Green said in the release that "we are still operating below the level of growth we expect from ourselves," and on the call, per Investing.com's transcript, that "there once was a monopoly that controlled all of search, and the LLMs have created competition."

PubMatic's revenue rose 17% to $79.6 million the same day, with CTV at about 21% of revenue and more than 120 "agentic campaigns" (its own category, up from 80-plus), including all five holding companies. Zeta's revenue rose 46% to $493 million, or 30% excluding Marigold and political candidate revenue, and it raised its full-year guide to $1,855 million at the midpoint. On Thursday, Liftoff reported revenue up 32% to $237.5 million and net income of $86.7 million; CEO Jeremy Bondy said the results "came in well above the high end of our previously published guidance."

### Yahoo Scout became the first licensee of a publishers' collective, and Google lost a second round against SerpApi

Yahoo took Scout out of beta on Tuesday, November 10, after "more than 3 billion answers" by its own count, to "underpin all of Yahoo Search" in the US, UK, Europe and Switzerland, adding Pivot Search and a Scout app. On Thursday, the nonprofit Internet Licensing Collective (formerly the RSL Collective), which says it represents "more than 3000 media brands, content owners and publishing platforms," signed Yahoo Scout as its first commercial customer. Digiday reports that the fee wasn't disclosed and that Yahoo declined an interview. The ILC's FAQ calls the license non-exclusive and projects expenses at or below 10%.

On Monday, Chief Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers of the Northern District of California granted SerpApi's motion to dismiss Google's amended complaint, with leave to amend; it's the second dismissal. SerpApi's blog and Law360 reported the ruling, and the copy of the order we found sits on a third-party host, not the court's docket. Dismissal with leave to amend isn't a ruling that scraping Google is lawful, and SerpApi's account is the winning party's. Glenn Gabe posted, "Serpapi wins again, but Google can still poison the SERPs..." (as AEO Sherpa quotes it).

### Google Ads says disclosed AI impersonation is still banned and shows advertisers their invalid-traffic credits

A Google Ads policy update posted Wednesday, November 11, says Google "updated its Unacceptable Business Practices policy on Public-figure impersonation to clarify that ads that impersonate public figures using AI are not allowed, even if they disclose that they were generated or edited using AI." Google says the clarification "does not change the enforcement of the policy."

On Thursday, Keerat Sharma, Google's VP and GM for Ads Privacy and Safety, introduced the Invalid Traffic Overview Card, rolling out gradually with two metrics, "Estimated savings" and "Credited cost." Estimated savings covers invalid clicks Google filtered out before billing, so it isn't money refunded. Sharma also wrote, "So far in 2026, we've reduced advertiser exposure to invalid leads by an average of 30% through continuous system upgrades," a Google figure with no stated baseline.

Anthropic's Usage Policy, covered at its October 8 announcement in our October 12 edition, took effect Thursday, November 12, barring use of Claude to "Manipulate the sources from which search engines or AI systems draw answers by seeding them with content that misrepresents its origin, authorship, or independence."

## Common Threads

### Platforms shipped their own meters, and each counts in its own unit

OpenAI's Hub counts a mention only when ChatGPT "references the business's website," SEJ cautions that "A recorded click isn't a confirmed visit," and Search Console's Generative AI report under-logged impressions for November 9–11.
