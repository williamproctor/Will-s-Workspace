## Act Two — transcription separates finished audio from live audio

OpenAI introduced two transcription paths:

- **GPT Transcribe:** completed or committed audio, **$0.0045 per minute**
- **GPT Live Transcribe:** continuous low-latency audio, **$0.017 per minute**

Both support context prompts, literal keyword hints, and expected-language arrays. GPT Transcribe can stream results while processing a completed file; the live model operates over an ongoing realtime connection.

Independent evidence measured:

- GPT Transcribe: **3.3% word error rate**
- GPT-4o Transcribe: **4.0%**

Lower is better, but aggregate WER does not predict every language, room, speaker, accent, or proper name.

Compatibility limits:

- JSON output only
- No native SRT/VTT
- No word-level timestamps
- No English translation
- No speaker diarization

Those jobs still require separate models or downstream processing.

## Act Four — provenance moves into audio and partial edits

OpenAI expanded its verifier and Content Provenance API to supported generated audio.

Supported formats:

- MP3
- Opus
- AAC
- FLAC
- WAV
- PCM

Limits:

- **50 MiB**
- **60-second decoded-duration limit**

A detected signal means supported OpenAI provenance was found. Non-detection does not prove human origin, absence of modification, ownership, or rights.

Supporting signals:

- **C2PA guidance** describes fully generated, partially modified, and later human-edited media through source types, actions, ingredients, and Regions of Interest. It records signed claims but does not prove every claim true.
- **Pangram Image** is a statistical detector preview, not signed provenance. Pangram reports 99.5% internal accuracy and a 0.16% false-positive rate on a specific pre-2022 set; those measurements are vendor-run.
- **Kimi K3 weights** shipped July 27 as approximately **1.56 TB** of MXFP4 files for a **2.8-trillion-parameter / 104-billion-active** model. The custom license and specialized multi-GPU deployment remain material.

## Act Five — supporting workflows and practitioner judgment

- **AutoCut Angles:** editable switching sequence from supplied camera tracks and one audio track inside Premiere or Resolve; single-speaker scope.
- **ElevenLabs Character Casting:** detects manuscript characters, suggests voices, previews dialogue, replaces a voice across the project, and prefills pronunciations. Vendor claims more than 90 languages and 10,000 voices; no independent workflow benchmark.
- **Grok Voice Think Fast 2.0:** $0.08 per audio minute; independent snapshot recorded 82.9% overall, 56.5% agentic, and 0.70 seconds to first audio.
- **Qwen mixed-scene audio:** 48kHz stereo research covering dialogue, ambience, and localized effects; paper only, with no weights, API, demo, price, license, or parameter count.

Practitioner context:

- **Victor Riparbelli:** video may become a two-way application that answers, demonstrates, draws, and checks comprehension.
- **Colin Smith:** easier tools do not create taste; editing knowledge still supports correction and provenance review.
- **Thais Castello Branco:** objective checks and contextual human preference solve different parts of creative evaluation.
- **Daniel Whitenack and Chris Benson:** agent containment depends on tools, credentials, network policy, uploads, monitoring, automated supervision, and human approval.
- **Akshay Nathan:** visible activity is not the same as meaningful progress.
- **Nathaniel Whittemore:** models occupy different roles in a stack rather than one winner serving every task.

1. Choose a completed caption file, edit, graphic package, equipment brief, metadata set, or delivery record.

6. Separate mechanical checks from criteria requiring qualified judgment.
7. Revise and version the checklist.


The goal is evidence that the checklist reflects finished production work—not a checklist that only appears complete on paper.
