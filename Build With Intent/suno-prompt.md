# Suno Generation Prompt — Build with Intent

## Prompt (paste into Suno Custom mode)

Sophisticated cinematic instrumental, 60 seconds. Opens with a solo muted jazz piano — a slow, deliberate three-note motif in a minor key. Sparse, elegant, slightly melancholic. Think Trent Reznor and Atticus Ross scoring a tech company that takes itself seriously. A soft analog synth pad swells underneath. At 11 seconds, the piano motif repeats but a plucked upright bass enters beneath it, adding weight. A single brushed snare hit punctuates the transition — understated tension. At 18 seconds, everything suspends. One held note. Two seconds of breath. At 22 seconds, a confident mid-tempo groove locks in — programmed drums (crisp, not bombastic), fingerstyle bass, and layered synth textures. Think Bonobo meets Jon Hopkins at 100 BPM. Controlled energy, not frantic. Each new layer enters precisely on the downbeat. Piano motif threads through as a melodic anchor. The build is architectural — each addition feels inevitable. Peaks at 38 seconds with full orchestration: strings swell (cellos, not violins — warmth, not sweetness), drums hit harder, bass deepens. At 42 seconds, the drums and bass drop out. Strings sustain alone for two bars — a moment of stillness at altitude. At 45 seconds, the resolution: the original piano motif returns, now played openly on a warm grand piano with sustain pedal. A nylon-string guitar doubles it an octave up. Felt, not performed. The room sounds real — you can hear the space around the instruments. Final chord at 57 seconds: a rich, open voicing that rings and decays naturally over 3 seconds into silence. No vocals. No irony in the music. The score treats the story with respect — the comedy lives in the picture, the music lives in the feeling.

## Iteration Notes

If the first generation leans too electronic or too busy, try emphasizing "sparse" and "space between notes" in a follow-up. The key quality is restraint — every note should feel earned. If it leans too somber, nudge toward "quietly confident" rather than "sad." The music should feel like the moment after you solve a hard problem, not the problem itself.
