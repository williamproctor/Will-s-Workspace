## 1. AI Overviews started showing up on most brand-name searches

**Timing note:** the ramp began before the window. DemandSphere's tracker has the share of branded keywords with an AI Overview at 26.12% on Sept 1, 69.21% on Sept 26 and 90.48% on Sept 27 (prior window). The posts and studies that made it a conversation all landed Sept 30–Oct 4. Straddle convention: state the ramp dates plainly.

**What happened:** Wednesday Sept 30, Chris Long (Nectiv) posted that 93 of 100 enterprise brands he checked that morning with SerpApi returned an AI Overview for their own name. Search Engine Roundtable wrote it up at 12:12pm: in Barry Schwartz's checks most of the AIOs sat mid-page, under the brand's own listing (Adobe's was at the top), and searches for Google itself and for news publishers were exceptions. DemandSphere (Ray Grieselhuber, Thu Oct 1) published daily data: 80.23% on Sept 28 and 82.06% on Sept 29, after a zigzag earlier in the month (57.43% on Sept 18, 31.15% on Sept 19). Method: one check per keyword per day across all markets and devices, counting an AIO whether or not the brand is cited. DemandSphere: "Google has not made any announcements on this change." Ahrefs (Fri Oct 2): 82.91% of US desktop branded keyword results had an AIO on Sept 29; by month, 61.3% (July), 65.1% (August), 73.3% (September), against 78.3% for non-branded keywords in September, from 232.8M crawls (92.5M branded). In Ahrefs' 100-brand panel, AIOs went from 5 of 98 brands on July 1 to 63 of 100 on Sept 30, and 48 of those 63 sat in position one (76.2%; average position 1.49). Ahrefs' most-cited external domains: Wikipedia (71.9%), YouTube (38.6%), LinkedIn (22.8%).

## 2. Google rewrote its AI-content guidance, and SEOs heard an update coming

**What happened:** Thursday Oct 1, Google's Search Central changelog: "Updated the using generative AI content guide with information from the Search Quality Raters guidelines." Why: "To get our documentation in sync with our presentations we use at our developer events." The new text in the guide: "Keep in mind that generative models don't retrieve facts, but predict a likely sequence of words based on their training data. Because of this, generative AI outputs may contain inaccuracies (also known as hallucinations). It is critical to manually factcheck and review all AI-generated content for accuracy and trustworthiness before publishing. This review also applies to metadata like <title> elements, meta description elements, structured data, and alternate texts for images, which can appear in Search results." Practitioners also flagged (Oct 2) a restructured "Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content" page, which has no changelog entry. It now defines main content ("Google Search defines "main content" as any part of the web page that directly helps the page achieve its purpose.") and describes it through effort, originality, talent or skill, and accuracy: "using generative AI to produce large amounts of text without manual oversight or curation represents little to no effort"; YMYL content "must be highly accurate and consistent with established expert consensus"; and "Fabricating creator profiles (such as by using AI-generated headshots, made-up names, or false credentials to make content appear as if it was written by human experts) is a form of deception. Any form of deception makes a page untrustworthy to both users and our automated quality systems, and is a signal of a low-quality page." Sunday Oct 4, Lily Ray published a prediction that the next big update is close.

## 3. Amazon's unBoxed pitch: "Amazon's AI handles the rest"

**What happened:** Tuesday Sept 29 at unBoxed in San Francisco, Amazon renamed its unified ad platform: "Today at unBoxed 2026, we announced Amazon Ads Agent, the new name of the unified platform, now powered by agentic AI." Three campaign types: sponsored ads; Display, Video and Audio (DVA, the new name for Amazon DSP); and Full-Funnel Campaigns, which put sponsored ads, display, video and streaming TV in one campaign: "Advertisers provide their budget, products and creative, and Amazon's AI handles the rest on their behalf, planning, executing and continuously optimising campaigns." Amazon: "Full-Funnel Campaigns is now available to all advertisers in the United States." Amit Bhattacharyya (Amazon's title: Vice President, Agentic Intelligence and Models): "One campaign ties every format from streaming TV to sponsored ads, shares signals in real time and tunes itself toward long-term growth. Advertisers set their goals and campaign settings, and Full-Funnel Campaigns connects discovery to purchase." Product page: "A minimum daily budget applies"; "no upfront fees or additional charges for orchestration or AI-powered creative generation." Amazon's takeaways page (Sept 30): "Beta testers saw 67% higher long-term return on ad spend and 29% lower cost to acquire new-to-brand customers" (Amazon-reported). Digiday: "Advertisers approve creative, for example, but cannot select individual channels or specific audiences." Bhattacharyya to Digiday: "We are figuring out how to give them more transparency on the audience side."
