### OpenAI's Business Hub shows ChatGPT mentions and clicks, and NoGood counted 1 Hub click against 1,020 GA4 sessions

OpenAI launched Businesses in ChatGPT in beta on Tuesday, November 10; a screenshot of its "Claim your business in ChatGPT" page had surfaced on Friday, November 6. A verified business sees Insights for the past 28 days, including "the number of mentions and website clicks a business receives," and the help page says, "For an online business, a mention counts when ChatGPT references the business's website. The brand name alone doesn't count..." Kevin Indig called the Hub "a sort of Search Console for ChatGPT," and SEL's Barry Schwartz wrote, "It is amazing OpenAI is giving us click data, a metric Google and Microsoft refuse to offer in its AI reporting tools."

Comparing the Hub with GA4, NoGood's Mostafa ElBermawy found 65 mentions and 1 click against 1,020 ChatGPT sessions (windows offset by a day) and called the product "a business profile and not a search console" (he discloses that his company, Goodie AI, supplied NoGood's crawler data). Previsible counted 1,834 mentions and 12 clicks against 32 GA4 sessions, and SEJ reported 353,356 mentions and 2,663 clicks for itself, warning that "A recorded click isn't a confirmed visit". Under Indig's post, Tim Lane wrote that "the more famous you are, the more Business Hub might undercount you," and Profound, which sells AI-visibility tracking, says brand names appear without a site reference "nearly 50% of the time" in its data.

**Comment window:** open now; the first full 28-day window since launch closes in early December. Log the ratio of Hub clicks to GA4 sessions each month as the instrument's calibration, and if you're a big brand, check whether ChatGPT cites your own pages.

### ChatGPT Ads' target bidding went live November 9, and one advertiser said OpenAI "silently rejected" 3,930 pixel events

Since Monday, November 9, new ChatGPT conversion campaigns bid to a Target CPA and new click campaigns to a Target CPC, while existing campaigns keep Max Bid and Max CPC until January 1, 2027 (we covered the announcement last week). George Prodanov's LinkedIn summary that morning was "Your cap becomes an average", and OpenAI's help page says "Individual clicks may cost more or less than your target."

On Tuesday, Ian McGavin wrote, "When we launched ChatGPT Ads on our own account, OpenAI silently rejected 3,930 of our pixel events." That's one account's experience, and OpenAI hasn't commented, but its help page says target bidding uses "available conversion signals" and warns, "Setup alone does not establish that usable conversion signals are being received." Thursday's weekly email to advertisers (via SER) renamed Maximize Results "intelligent bidding," repeated that "Actual costs may be higher or lower than your target," and added regional targeting and multi-condition product filters for product campaigns. In the API, OpenAI recommends the new target fields from November 18, after which legacy fields used to create a campaign "will select Target CPA or Target CPC".

**Comment window:** open until January 1, 2027, when the caps retire for every campaign. Before moving a capped campaign to Target CPA, reconcile the pixel's accepted events against your own orders for the same days, since the system bids toward the conversions it receives.

### Salesforce projects $75.8 billion in AI-driven Cyber Week spending and finds shoppers wary of agent checkout

Salesforce's holiday outlook on Thursday, November 12, says "AI is projected to drive $75.8 billion in Cyber Week spending, up 13% from 2025," and "AI-driven recommendations are expected to influence more than 1 in 5 holiday orders." The release doesn't say which markets the figure covers or define "AI-driven." By Salesforce's own measurement, traffic to ecommerce sites from AI tools is up 104% year over year, and shoppers arriving through AI referrals are "more than two times as likely to convert as typical ecommerce shoppers."

Its survey of more than 2,500 shoppers in the US, UK, Canada and Australia, with no fieldwork dates given, found that 31% have tried a personal agent, with Meta Muse the most adopted (12% of respondents). Of personal-agent users, 19% have used one at checkout, and 73% of shoppers "aren't comfortable giving a personal AI agent their payment information." Among agent users, 27% say an agent "bought something that was too expensive" and 22% had one blocked. Caila Schwartz, who leads agentic commerce shopper insights, advised, "Shoppers are starting with AI this season, so make sure your product data is accurate, your prices and inventory are current, and your own agent is ready to answer the hard questions." Salesforce sells commerce software, and its line that brands deploying AI shopping agents "see seven times faster sales growth" gives no base.

### Yahoo Scout became the first licensee of a publishers' collective, and Google lost a second round against SerpApi

SerpApi's blog and Law360 reported the ruling, and the copy of the order we found sits on a third-party host, not the court's docket. ### Google Ads says disclosed AI impersonation is still banned and shows advertisers their invalid-traffic credits

A Google Ads policy update posted Wednesday, November 11, says Google "updated its Unacceptable Business Practices policy on Public-figure impersonation to clarify that ads that impersonate public figures using AI are not allowed, even if they disclose that they were generated or edited using AI." Google says the clarification "does not change the enforcement of the policy."

On Thursday, Keerat Sharma, Google's VP and GM for Ads Privacy and Safety, introduced the Invalid Traffic Overview Card, rolling out gradually with two metrics, "Estimated savings" and "Credited cost."
