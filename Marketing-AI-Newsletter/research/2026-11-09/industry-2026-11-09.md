# Industry track — week of November 2–8, 2026

## The midterms: a record $11.87B in political ads, CTV ahead of cable, $187M of AI ads mostly without labels, and a vote that turned on AI's power bills

**Timing note:** in window. Election Day was Tue Nov 3. AdImpact's final tally of $11.87B was reported by The Current on Wed Nov 4; AdImpact's own release wasn't found. Earlier in-progress figures ran before and on Election Day: $11.21B (TheWrap, Oct 27), $11.4B (Adweek), "A record $11.6 billion" (Axios, Nov 3). Wesleyan Media Project's AI-ads count is from its Oct 29 release (before the window); USA Today covered it Oct 30, and Erika Franklin Fowler updated it on WBUR's On Point on Fri Nov 6. CNBC's piece on platforms ran Nov 3 (updated Nov 4). Magnite raised its political outlook on its Nov 4 call. Reuters' results story and OpenAI's compact ran Thu Nov 5, the day the Nasdaq fell.

- **Spend (AdImpact, via The Current, Wed Nov 4):** $11.87B, above 2024's $11.2B presidential-year total. CTV took about 22% ($2.6B) and broadcast about $5.8B. Campaigns & Elections, on the same data: broadcast $5.84B, CTV $2.61B (about 22%) and cable $1.27B (about 11%), and it asked whether campaigns "bet too big on broadcast". MediaPost (Nov 5): Republicans $5.4B against Democrats' $5.0B, and total spend up 33% on 2022. Adweek reported CTV overtaking cable for the first time in a midterm, with CTV up 122%. The Current also cites PubMatic: CTV was 86% of political spend on PubMatic's platform Aug 31–Oct 12. - **The sell side (Magnite Q3 call, Wed Nov 4; Michael Barrett; Motley Fool transcript):** "As I mentioned earlier, political spend significantly exceeded our expectations. - **AI ads (Wesleyan Media Project, Oct 29; USA Today, Oct 30; On Point, Nov 6):** 289 unique AI-generated ads with more than $187M behind them, about 90% (about $169M) of it Republican; 82% of the sponsors were Republican and 13% Democratic; spending on such ads was under $50,000 in 2024; and only 27% carried disclaimers through mid-October (USA Today). Franz (Wesleyan Media Project), to USA Today: "It's kind of gone from zero to 100 in just that two year term". Maya Hutchinson (Battleground AI), same episode: "I think labeling everything as AI then doesn't tell you anything about the content or the context of the advertising itself." - **Platforms and the law (CNBC, Nov 3, updated Nov 4):** about 30 states have laws on AI in political ads and there's no federal one; the Protect Elections from Deceptive AI Act and the AI Ads Act haven't left committee.
