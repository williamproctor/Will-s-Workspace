## What leads

**News lead:** Shopping agents spread in the last week before Black Friday, and retailers and platforms chose which ones to let in. On Thursday Nov 19, Salesforce forecast that Cyber Week (Tue Nov 24 through Mon Nov 30) "will drive a record $357 billion in global online sales, and $75.8 billion of that figure will be influenced by AI and agents", or "21% of all global sales". It added: "Twenty percent of holiday shopping traffic will be nonhuman". The agents arrived the same week. Meta's Muse launched in Australia on Tuesday, with ShopBack, Kogan, MECCA and Officeworks among the early connectors. "With the festive season coming up, the timing couldn't be better," said Will Easton, Meta's managing director for Australia and New Zealand. On Wednesday Loblaw joined in Canada, where shoppers can "plan meals, build their basket and check out" inside Muse. GeekWire reported that Muse's shopping skill now says "Do not search Amazon", after Amazon blocked Muse (Kaziukėnas, Nov 13). Asked about outside agents at a Semafor event, Andy Jassy said "it has to be a good customer experience, which it hasn't been to date, and it has to be a fair exchange of value." The same Tuesday, Amazon.com.au joined YouTube Shopping's new Australian affiliate program as a partner (Google Australia). Klarna introduced an assistant that "handles shopping, returns, bookings, and everyday admin" over WhatsApp and iMessage, "Currently live with a small group of early users". Google's Demand Gen ads can now end in "checkout links or Universal Commerce Protocol-enabled checkout" (Nov 18). What's new for marketers is that the agent decides which stores it searches and the retailer decides which agents get in, so being inside an agent is a distribution decision as much as a ranking one. Salesforce's $75.8B is the number it published Nov 12 (2026-11-16 story 3), now scoped as global and "influenced by" rather than "driven by". Black Friday is Nov 27.
