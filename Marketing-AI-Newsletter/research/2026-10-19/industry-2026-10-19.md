# Industry track — week of October 12–18, 2026

## Meta's crawl clause: the terms, the publishers, and the Friday rewrite

**Timing note:** straddle. Meta notified business users of updated Commercial Terms "in early October" (Nieman Lab: a blog post and a Meta Business Suite notice). The new terms take effect Friday Oct 30 and replace the version effective March 6, 2024. In window: Nieman Lab's report (Thu Oct 15), Meta's denials and Jason Kint's thread (Fri Oct 16), Meta's rewrite (Friday afternoon, Oct 16; reported Sat Oct 17), the publisher trade bodies' statements (Oct 16–17), and the split between the US-English and UK-English texts (Oct 17–18). The edition states the notice date plainly.

- **Nieman Lab breaks it (Thu Oct 15, 12:59 p.m.; Andrew Deck and Hanaa' Tameez):** the new clause in full: "You authorize us (or any entity acting on our behalf) to use automated software (such as web scrapers, bots, robots, spiders, crawlers, and user agents) and create test credentials to access, index, cache, analyze, collect, retrieve, or crawl the domains, web pages, or URLs (and any content available through them) that are associated with your products or services, or that are made available through your or users’ use of Meta Company Products and services, and to periodically pull updates from such domains, web pages, or URLs. Meta may use any content from such domains, web pages, or URLs to provide, develop, improve, research, train, and maintain the integrity of, the Meta Company Products, services, and features provided under these Commercial Terms and any other applicable supplemental terms." There's no opt-out; the notice reads: "If you do not agree to the updated terms, please stop all access or use of our Products for business or commercial purposes." Matt Topic (Loevy & Loevy): "This is a remarkable provision to bury in [the] terms of service, given that this would give Meta access to material that would likely cost many millions of dollars in a licensing market," and "It’s not clear whether a court would enforce this, but publishers should assume that it would be enforceable." James Grimmelmann (Cornell Law School): "For news publishers, this clearly represents permission to Meta to train AI on any content from their sites and possibly to deploy some of that content, even in competition with the publishers themselves," and "publishers either have to trust that Meta doesn’t mean the full extent of what these terms say, or they have to stop [using business accounts]," he said. Grimmelmann also said test credentials could technically let Meta "sign up as a fake user" to get past registration walls; Meta declined to say whether they would be used to bypass paywalls. Benjamin Sobel (University of Wisconsin), on tying: "If you want to use the Meta products that require acceptance of the commercial terms, you have to also acquiesce to the scraping and crawling permissions exacted in those terms." Meta's public-affairs spokesperson, by email: crawling language was previously scattered across several terms and is now consolidated, and "content owners can still manage how their content is accessed by using robots.txt." The tip came from Stephen Beale (The Steampunk Explorer), who saw the notice in his Business Suite dashboard: "I know my site has been scraped to death" and "But it just seems like such an arrogant, in-your-face move on [Meta’s] part… It just makes me angry and I don’t want them to get away with it." [Nieman Lab, Oct 15](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/10/metas-new-commercial-terms-will-allow-the-scraping-of-news-sites-legal-experts-say/)
- **What is new, and what isn't (SEJ, Fri Oct 16; Matt G. Southern):** "The current Commercial Terms, dated March 6, 2024, have no crawling clause. Meta’s ad terms do." The current Self-Serve Ad Terms (Aug 22, 2025) already let Meta crawl an advertiser's domains once it places an ad order, for uses including "research and training". The Ad Creative Generative AI Terms (May 6, 2024) cover URLs advertisers give Meta's generative ad tools. What changes is the trigger and the reach. The grant now attaches to any business use, including a business that only manages a Facebook Page and never buys ads, and it reaches any site "associated with" the business's products. Neither the new Commercial Terms nor the new Self-Serve Ad Terms mention robots.txt. SEJ's reading of Meta's crawler documentation: Meta-ExternalAgent crawls for uses such as training foundation models or indexing content; a separate Meta-WebIndexer token serves Meta AI search; Meta-ExternalFetcher and FacebookExternalHit may bypass robots.txt. SEJ adds that the European Publishers Council is concerned Meta could claim permission to use an article whenever a user shares a link to it. ## Agentic commerce: Google's cart, Meta's in-app browsing, and who vouches for the agent

- **Google's Universal Cart launches in the US (Thu Oct 15; Suresh Ganapathy, Senior Director, Product Management, Consumer Shopping):** "Today, Universal Cart is rolling out in the U.S. across Google Search and in the Gemini app." It saves products from multiple retailers wherever you shop on Google and is built on "the 60+ billion continually refreshed product listings in Google’s Shopping Graph." It sends price-drop and back-in-stock alerts, draws on Google Wallet to suggest which card to use, and shows promo codes. "You can securely check out with Google Pay in just a few taps with many of your favorite stores right in the cart, or you can transfer your items directly to the merchant’s cart to finish buying on their site." Named merchants: "Nike, Sephora, Target, Ulta Beauty, Walmart, Wayfair, and many Shopify merchants." Merchants "will remain the seller of record." Google's framing: "our focus spans two fronts: unlocking agentic capabilities that act on your behalf, and designing practical tools that give shoppers confidence and control. Google’s Universal Cart is built for that second track". SEJ (Matt G. Southern): "This first release is one-way and covers cart creation and transfer only. The guide says updating, deleting, or syncing cart contents between Google and the merchant’s system isn’t supported. Native checkout on Google is a separate API and isn’t required for cart transfer." The UCP integration hub in Merchant Center is rolling out gradually in the US, with Australia and Canada next year. The UCP Analytics dashboard tracks Buy-button clicks through purchases and cancellations, and the Merchant API (in alpha) adds cart-transfer metrics; "None of these pages list a metric for products saved to a cart." Missing from the launch post, per SEJ: YouTube and Gmail (which Google's I/O post in May said would come later), price history, compatibility warnings and loyalty information. On Shopify stores, where Direct Checkout is on by default and Google Analytics and custom pixels don't fire (2026-09-28 edition), SEJ "could not determine if in-cart purchases are handled with the same checkout process as purchases completed through Direct Checkout." - **American Express Intent Intelligence Framework (Tue Oct 13):** captures and authenticates a customer's purchase intent before an agent transacts. A Create Intent API records the instructions and constraints (merchant, maximum budget, product preferences), and the customer authenticates and signs off. An Intent ID and a Proof of Intent Token then obtain a tokenized credential that the agent uses at checkout in place of the card number. Hannah Bozian, VP of Agentic Partnerships and Strategy: "These new transactions will have a consumer, a merchant, and an agent," she told TechCrunch. And: "Our approach differs from the implementations of others in that it pulls intent earlier into the transaction flow. And this is important because it helps mitigate some risk before the risk occurs," she said. Her example: a customer asks for white Nike sneakers in size 9, the agent tries to buy Adidas, and Amex can stop the transaction. Amex says it hasn't yet seen disputes rise from agentic commerce. Company survey (Amex Trendex): 66% of surveyed US consumers say they're likely to use AI agents to research the best price over the next 12 months, and 61% for recommendations. It builds on the ACE developer toolkit launched in April. Digital Commerce 360 (Oct 16) reports no live integrations yet. Luke Gebb, head of global innovation: "Providing authorized purchase intent before an agent transacts can give issuers greater confidence that an agent is acting as a customer intended, while also helping issuers proactively manage risk and reduce the chance of costly disputes across the payments ecosystem," he told Digital Commerce 360. ## Measurement studies and data releases

- **Ahrefs: how ChatGPT fans out (Mon Oct 12; Louise Linehan; vendor study):** 66.6 million free, logged-out GPT-5.6 Luna responses and their 15 million fan-out queries from August and September. 14.4% of responses contained fan-out queries, about 1.6 each on average. 6.8% of fan-outs used the `site:` operator, across ~116K domains, and 75.9% of the most common `site:` searches pointed at a brand site. Retailers (Walmart, Costco, Amazon) made up 11.9%, and Reddit and Facebook 1.9% between them. "Official" appeared in 13.1% of English fan-outs in September, up from 10.1% in August; "price" went from 6.04% to 8.88%, a 47% relative rise. In a separate test of 20,000 US prompts, 84.4% of factual questions cited at least one official source, against 68.3% of comparison questions.
