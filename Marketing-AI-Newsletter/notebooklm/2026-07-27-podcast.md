That's a Google-measured number, and independent tests published last November found the opposite on cost per conversion, a discrepancy nobody has resolved publicly. Dynamic Search Ads retire in September with automatic upgrades into AI Max, which makes the AI format the default rather than the option. Sundar Pichai repeated that Google sends "billions of clicks to websites every week" through AI features. Then the kicker: SE Ranking found AI Mode showed a text ad on 29% of tested commercial keywords, but the advertiser's domain appeared among the answer's cited sources only 11% of the time. Paid placement, citations, and rankings are behaving like three separate surfaces. Buying one does not buy the others.

### August 2: AI-content labels become enforceable in the EU, with fines up to 3% of turnover

The European Commission published its Article 50 guidelines and finalized the Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content on July 20. The EU AI Act's transparency obligations become applicable **August 2**, with fines up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide turnover, whichever is higher. Google announced July 24 that it's signing the Code. Its own documentation still keeps liability with the advertiser.

Two details matter most for marketing teams. First, the artistic carve-out does not cover advertising: Commission guidance explicitly lists a synthetic spokesperson selling a product as content needing the full label. Assistive editing (retouching, color work, cropping) stays exempt. Second, the EU is one layer of a stack. New York's synthetic-performer disclosure has been live since June 9 at $1,000 to $5,000 per violation, India's rules since February, and California's SB 942/AB 853 are synced to the same August 2 date. Google spent the month shipping the plumbing: an AI label setting across five ads products, attestation fields in the Google Ads and DV360 APIs. All of it records declarations that no system validates and that remain the advertiser's legal problem.

### ChatGPT Ads ships conversion bidding on top of an auction nobody can see into

OpenAI rolled out the toolkit performance marketers were waiting for: conversion-optimized (oCPC) campaigns, geo exclusions, AppsFlyer and Adjust integrations, hashed-data advanced matching, a bulk API, and product cards showing prices and ratings. Daily budgets move to seven-day rolling averages from July 27. The platform that started with $200,000 minimums now has none.

Demand is arriving faster than the reporting. Adthena tracks 7,378 advertisers in ChatGPT, up 97-fold in a quarter from an admittedly small base, led by Booking.com. But the native Ads Manager shows advertisers only their own numbers. No impression share, no auction insights. UK advertisers have watched costs swing 39% to 278% with no visible cause, and you're bidding on loose conversational prompt categories rather than keywords you can enumerate. This auction sits roughly where paid search sat two decades ago, except the spend is arriving at modern scale before the reporting exists.

### Similarweb: ChatGPT falls to half of gen-AI traffic, and citations point at different pages than clicks

Similarweb's 2026 Generative AI Landscape report landed this week: 9.5 billion monthly web visits to gen-AI platforms, up 70% year over year. ChatGPT's share of standalone gen-AI web visits fell from above 80% in late 2024 to just above 50% by May. June snapshots of the same panel put Gemini around 27% and Claude around 9%. For anyone doing AI-visibility work, four findings stand out:

- ChatGPT's citation rate jumped from roughly 1.3% to 6.8% of answers in a year, led by travel (22.6%) and retail (13.5%). Reviews and user-generated content supply 28.9% of top citations; news another 26.0%.
- Citations and referrals diverge: 65% of cited URLs sit two or more folders deep in a site, while 58.8% of referral traffic lands on homepages.
- AI referral traffic converts at 7.1%, second only to paid search at 7.8%.
- Ads appeared in 26% of US desktop ChatGPT chats in June, up from 14% in May, at a 0.50% click-through rate. Which means roughly three-quarters of even the most ad-saturated assistant surface is still earned exposure.

Caveats before anyone builds a deck on this: these are panel estimates, and the vendors disagree with each other. Similarweb puts AI Overviews in 43% of US searches where Adthena's pulse says 18%, and their UK ChatGPT ad numbers are flatly incompatible. There is no standard measurement yet.

### WARC: 88% more creative, 45% better. The brief is the bottleneck.

WARC's "The New Creative Advantage" (produced with TikTok and LIONS Advisory; 400 marketers across four markets; covered July 22) found 88% of marketers producing more creative volume since adopting AI. Only 45% report significantly better quality. The study locates that 43-point gap in the brief: 67% feed generative models demographic data as the primary input, while 59% of the same population says demographic segmentation no longer works. Just 17% consistently add community or audience insight. TikTok's Andy Yang, in the foreword, calls it "not a technology gap, it is an intelligence gap." (Disclosure: TikTok commissioned research that concludes marketers need the cultural signal TikTok sells. The data stands; the alignment belongs on the record.)

The mechanism is the useful part. AI compresses production, which removes the human layers that used to compensate for thin briefs. The brief now travels almost directly to the finished asset, so input quality matters more than ever, at the exact moment most teams left it unchanged.
