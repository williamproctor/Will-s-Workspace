# Conversation track — week of August 17–23, 2026

Edition date: 2026-08-24. What marketing/AI X and LinkedIn actually argued about this week, with primary receipts where located.

## 1. OpenAI's influencer "Summer Camp" backlash (the week's main character)

**What happened:** OpenAI hosted ~30 content creators at an all-expenses-paid retreat dubbed "OpenAI Summer Camp" at Wildflower Farms, a luxury resort in Gardiner, NY (Hudson Valley) where cabins run about $2,000/night. Attendee vlogs ("girlboss-style business circles" per The Verge) broke containment over the weekend of Aug 15–16 and backlash cascaded across X, TikTok, Reddit, Instagram, and LinkedIn through the week. Critics called it dystopian and a transparent image play — an AI company with data-center water/energy scrutiny courting mostly young female lifestyle creators at a nature resort.

**Key receipts:**
- The Verge analysis (the definitive piece on the marketing mechanics): creators absorb the backlash, brands rent their trust; explicit parallel to the 2023 Shein brand trip. https://www.theverge.com/tech/975173/openai-influencers-brand-trip-ai-backlash-marketing
- Attendee Grace McCarrick (145K TikTok followers) responded on LinkedIn: she "genuinely had no idea that people were this anti-AI," and said the response exposed "the shocking AI psychosis running rampant online" — quoted by The Verge. (Direct LinkedIn post URL not located; attribute via The Verge.)
- OpenAI statement (to Times Union/BI): "Creators are an important part of our community… We welcome healthy debate as AI becomes more prevalent, and we value creators who choose to engage with us." Also told Business Insider the event was education-focused, tied to the ChatGPT Work launch.
- Times Union (local origin reporting, Gardiner NY): https://www.timesunion.com/hudsonvalley/news/article/open-ai-wildflower-farms-tech-creator-retreat-22395319.php
- Business Insider: https://www.businessinsider.com/openai-creators-retreat-upstate-new-york-backlash-2026-8 — notes some attendee posts were deleted by Friday.

**Why it's spreading:** it sits on three live wires at once — anti-AI sentiment (jobs, water, energy), influencer-marketing fatigue, and the irony of an AI company using the most human marketing channel there is. Creator-side defense videos are feeding a second wave.

**Comment window:** open and hot. The uncontested marketing take: OpenAI ran the standard consumer brand-trip playbook and discovered AI is not a standard consumer brand — the trust it needed to rent is exactly what the audience thinks AI is eroding. Risk-transfer to creators (who ate the backlash) is the mechanism worth naming.

## 2. LinkedIn's "seems like AI slop" button hits 1M clicks; flagged content −40% views

**What happened:** LinkedIn CPO Hari Srinivasan posted (Thursday, Aug 20) that over one million members have clicked "seems like AI slop" since the button launched July 30, that members now see 40% fewer views on content LinkedIn classifies as AI slop, and that creators whose posts get enough community flags will start seeing a notice in Post Analytics ("Some members told us this post seems like AI").

**Primary receipt (verbatim, from his LinkedIn post):**
- "Over a million people have now clicked 'seems like AI slop,' since we launched it two weeks ago...helping us better understand how our community experiences low-quality content."
- "I hesitate to give numbers, as everyone has a different network and feed, but overall members are now experiencing 40% less views on what we classify as AI slop from just a few weeks ago."
- "I know I'm increasingly conscious on how to not sound like AI & the goal is to provide helpful feedback."
- Post URL: https://www.linkedin.com/posts/hsrinivasan1_one-more-follow-up-on-ai-slop-first-activity-7495960472341409792-U4ZA (171 comments when captured)

**Context/coverage:** The Register (Aug 24): LinkedIn's automated detection was already catching hundreds of thousands of slop comments daily and "billions of other automation attempts" in recent months; LinkedIn also removed its own AI "enhance your post" feature in July, replacing it with a proofreader. https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/08/24/users-mash-linkedins-ai-slop-button-1m-times-in-3-weeks/5291802 · Engadget: https://www.engadget.com/2241857/linkedin-says-its-ai-slop-button-is-working/ — notes prior algorithm moves against "it's not X, it's Y" phrasing.

**Why it's spreading:** every marketer publishing on LinkedIn now has readers holding a slop button, and the platform that sold AI writing assistance is now penalizing AI-sounding writing. Top comment pushback (visible on the post): LinkedIn spent years nudging members to write with AI and now hands members a button to punish it.

**Comment window:** open; the Register piece is dated today (Aug 24). Smart contribution: the −40% is a distribution penalty on a *classifier's* judgment, not a human's — the practical question is what false positives do to legitimate posts that merely sound polished.
