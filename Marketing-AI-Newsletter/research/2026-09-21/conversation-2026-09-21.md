## 1. Novig's Sydney Sweeney ad: did the outrage play work?

**Timing note:** the "Just Sports" spot launched Wednesday, September 9 (prior window). The backlash crested September 12–14, and the week of Sept 14–20 carried the argument over whether it worked, plus the first measurement. The edition covers that in-window aftermath and says the launch date plainly.

**What happened:** Novig, a federally regulated US sports prediction market (Athletic description), ran its first national campaign with an otherwise-nude Sydney Sweeney covered by sports equipment, pitching "just sports" against rivals that list contracts on politics, wars, and death. Sweeney is an equity holder and, per the CEO, the creative lead. It was one of three celebrity-led prediction-market spots that week (Polymarket with LeBron James, Eli Manning, Derek Jeter and others; Betr with Jeremy Piven and Adrian Grenier), per [Front Office Sports' newsletter](https://frontofficesports.com/newsletter/asset-class-sydney-sweeney-effect/). Female athletes answered with a "this is what sport looks like" wave.

**Receipts (in window):**

## 3. The first read on August 17: budget-limited CPCs up 15.8%

**What happened:** Google's help page now states the Aug 17 target-based bidding rollout completed globally Aug 27 (spotted by Hana Kobzová, PPC News Feed). Mike Ryan (Smarter Ecommerce) posted median CPC data on X Sept 14: budget-limited tROAS campaigns' median CPC +15.8% after Aug 17 (≈€0.38 → €0.44 per TechWyse's reading), never-budget-limited campaigns' CPCs −13%. Verbatim: "Two natural consequences of ending bid suppression are: 1) CPCs are rising for budgeted-limited campaigns, and 2) a sizable pool of cheap clicks have been liquidated and are now available to non-limited campaigns." And: "Google lowered bids on budget-limited campaigns to prevent exceeding budget. Ironically, this meant that most campaigns were *rank-limited* by CPC. Now you can see that situation reversing itself." On the PPC Live podcast (SEL, Sept 15) he added that advertisers raised tROAS around the update even on campaigns that weren't budget-limited, and that a full read needs one to two conversion cycles (30–60 days). Origins: [SER data post](https://www.seroundtable.com/data-google-ads-aug17-bidding-update-42086.html) · [SER completion date](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-august-17-bidding-update-finished-august-27-42090.html) · [SEL podcast writeup](https://searchengineland.com/google-ads-ai-needs-guardrails-not-blind-trust-ft-mike-ryan-488756) · [TechWyse](https://www.techwyse.com/news/platform-updates/google-smart-bidding-august-2026-update-cpc-bid-suppression). Ryan's X status URL not surfaced; attribute via SER.

**Continuity:** our Aug 17 lead ("September is unreadable by design") and Aug 24 lead (manual price controls narrowed). This is the first outside data.

**Comment window:** open until the mid-October read. Practical post: for budget-limited target campaigns, the lever is now budget, not target.
