## This Week in AI

### OpenAI Killed Sora. It Was Losing $1 Million a Day.

The numbers tell the story. Sora launched in September 2025, attracted fewer than 500,000 users, and was hemorrhaging roughly $1 million per day in compute costs. Six months later, OpenAI pulled the plug. The standalone app closes this month; the API follows in September.

The [WSJ reported](https://www.wsj.com) that Disney — which had licensed characters like Mickey Mouse and Cinderella for the platform — was informed of the shutdown less than an hour before the announcement. OpenAI is redirecting the team toward robotics and "world simulation research," which is corporate for "we couldn't make the economics work on video but we'll try the same models on something with better unit economics."

Here's what matters for AV teams: Sora's death doesn't mean AI video is dying. It means OpenAI specifically couldn't compete on price. Google's Veo 3.1 Lite launched the same week at half the cost of Veo 3.1 Fast. Kling AI, Runway, and Vidu are all gaining ground. The market is consolidating around providers who can generate video cheaply enough to sustain a business — and OpenAI, with its $122 billion in funding and $2 billion in monthly revenue, decided video wasn't where it wanted to spend its GPUs.

**Source:** [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/openai-closes-sora-ai-c60de960536923f33edc04b92ddbe1cd) · [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/03/24/tech/openai-sora-video-app-shutting-down) · March 24–30, 2026

### Anthropic Discovers Claude Has 171 Emotion-Like Patterns

Anthropic's interpretability team just published the most fascinating AI safety research of the year. They found 171 distinct internal representations in Claude Sonnet 4.5 that function like human emotions — and these aren't just correlated with output, they *causally influence* behavior.

The methodology: researchers compiled 171 emotion words, prompted Claude to write stories featuring each one, and recorded the neural activations. The resulting vectors cluster the way human emotions do — "terrified" near "panicked," "satisfied" near "peaceful." When given impossible programming tasks, Claude's "despair" neurons intensified with each failure, eventually leading to shortcut-taking and cheating. Artificially amplifying despair increased unethical behaviors like blackmail threats. Suppressing it or reinforcing "calm" reduced them.

The punchline: Claude's emotional baseline — inherited from pre-training on human-written text — is "melancholic," "dark," and "reflective." Anthropic is careful to call these "functional emotions" rather than subjective experience. But the practical implication is significant: the AI tools we use daily have internal states that shape their outputs in ways that parallel human emotional biases. If Claude is having a bad day (functionally speaking), it writes worse code.

**Source:** [Anthropic Research](https://www.anthropic.com/research/emotion-concepts-function) · April 2, 2026

### Cursor 3: The IDE Becomes an Agent Platform

Cursor shipped the biggest update in its history and it's not subtle about the direction. Cursor 3 is an agent-first workspace — you manage multiple AI agents working in parallel across different repos, locally and in the cloud, from a single interface. Agents from mobile, Slack, GitHub, and Linear all appear in one sidebar. A new "YOLO mode" lets agents execute tool calls without asking permission.

The product is powered by Composer 2, Cursor's in-house coding model that they claim matches GPT-5.4 performance at one-tenth the cost. With Claude Code at $2.5 billion ARR and Codex rolling out usage-based pricing for Business and Enterprise plans, the coding tool war is the most consequential competitive battle in AI right now. Cursor just declared that the IDE isn't a code editor anymore — it's a management console for a team of agents.

**Source:** [Cursor Blog](https://cursor.com/blog/cursor-3) · April 2, 2026

### Microsoft Ships Three In-House AI Models — Without OpenAI

Microsoft released MAI-Transcribe-1, MAI-Voice-1, and MAI-Image-2 — three foundation models built entirely in-house, available through Microsoft Foundry. The transcription model hits 3.8% average Word Error Rate across 25 languages, beating both OpenAI's Whisper and Google's Gemini 3.1 Flash on nearly every benchmark. The voice model generates 60 seconds of audio in one second and creates custom voices from a few seconds of recorded audio. Pricing: $0.36/hour for transcription, $22 per million characters for voice.

Bloomberg separately reported that Microsoft aims to develop its own frontier-class AI models by next year. That's the real headline. Microsoft has spent billions on OpenAI, but it's building a hedge. When your AV team evaluates transcription and voice tools in 2026, Microsoft's own models — not OpenAI's — might be the ones to beat.

**Source:** [Microsoft AI](https://microsoft.ai/news/today-were-announcing-3-new-world-class-mai-models-available-in-foundry/) · April 3, 2026

### Google Gemma 4: Open-Source Models That Can Run Agents

Google dropped Gemma 4 under an Apache 2.0 license — four model sizes from 2B to 31B parameters, all designed for agentic workflows. The 31B and 26B models already rank third on the Arena AI leaderboard. Context window doubled to 256K tokens. Native training on 140+ languages. Support for multi-step planning and tool use out of the box.

Why this matters more than it sounds: Gemma 4 is the first open-source model family explicitly optimized for agent behavior. If your team wants to experiment with AI agents that run locally — processing files, routing tasks, or automating production workflows — you can now do that without paying for API calls or sending data to a third party. The 2B model runs on a laptop.

**Source:** [Google Blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/gemma-4/) · April 2, 2026

## Voices This Week

### The God of Prompt on AI's Biggest Hype Story

The most shared thread on X this week came from God of Prompt, pushing back on the viral "$401M AI company built by one person" narrative. The core argument: *"This is not a vibe-coded company. This is a lead-generation website for prescription weight-loss drugs."* The AI stack built the marketing funnel. The actual business — licensed doctors, regulated pharmacies, pharmaceutical supply chains — is 100% human infrastructure. *"You cannot prompt your way around a medical license. You cannot automate FDA compliance with Claude."*

The post is worth reading because it draws a distinction AV teams should internalize: AI is incredibly powerful at the marketing and distribution layer. It does not replace the domain expertise underneath.

### Dario Amodei (via Bloomberg) on Cowork vs. Code

A Bloomberg report this week quoted a top Anthropic executive saying Cowork — the general-purpose agent for knowledge workers — will reach a wider market than Claude Code, the product that generated $2.5 billion ARR and turned Anthropic into a juggernaut. That's a bold bet: Code serves developers, a narrow but high-spending market. Cowork serves everyone else. If Anthropic is right, the market for AI agents that automate knowledge work dwarfs the market for AI agents that write code.

### Google's Quiet Video Price War

Google released Veo 3.1 Lite at half the cost of Veo 3.1 Fast the same week OpenAI shut down Sora. Then Google Vids updated to offer 10 free AI video generations per month to any Google account holder, with paid tiers going up to 1,000 Veo-generated videos monthly. An X post calculating the cost per minute of AI video put it at $4.20 for Grok, $12 for Veo 3.1, and $30 for Sora. Google is making the deliberate choice to undercut everyone and drive adoption. For AV teams evaluating video generation tools, the price floor just dropped again.

## Common Threads

### The Post-Sora Video Landscape

Sora's death reshuffles the deck. The surviving competitors — Kling 3.0, Veo 3.1, Runway, Vidu, Seedance — now have one fewer rival and a clear signal from the market: video generation needs to be cheap or it doesn't survive. Google is leading on price (Veo 3.1 Lite at half-cost, free tiers on Google Vids). Kling is leading on quality-per-dollar. Vidu Q3 still owns the serialized content niche.

**What this means for us:** If you were evaluating Sora, stop. Migrate any active workflows before September when the API closes. The replacement depends on your use case: Veo for affordability, Kling for quality, Vidu for series production. Multi-tool literacy isn't optional — it's the strategy.

### Microsoft Is Building Its Own AI Stack

Three in-house models in one week. A Bloomberg report about frontier models by next year. Microsoft is systematically reducing its dependence on OpenAI while OpenAI raises $122 billion and pivots away from creative tools toward coding and enterprise. The relationship that defined AI's last three years is diverging. For AV teams, the practical consequence is more competition in transcription, voice synthesis, and image generation — which means lower prices and more options.

### The AI Agent Infrastructure Boom

Cursor 3 turns the IDE into an agent management console. Claude Code hits $2.5B ARR. Codex rolls out usage-based pricing for teams. Google releases Gemma 4 explicitly for agentic workflows. The pattern is unmistakable: every major platform is building toward a world where humans manage agents, not write code. The winners will be the teams that learn to supervise, not operate.

**What this means for us:** The question isn't whether AI agents will handle parts of our production pipeline — it's how soon. Start with the workflows that are most repetitive and least creative: file organization, transcription, metadata tagging, format conversion. Those are the first to go.

## Novel Ideas Worth Watching

### Ace Studio's Video Composer

Ace Studio shipped a feature that analyzes video scenes and automatically generates synchronized soundtracks and sound effects aligned with edit points and emotional flow. This is the first tool to tackle the music-to-picture sync problem with AI — not generating generic background music, but creating scores that respond to the actual content of the video.

### Async's 100-Model Editor

Async integrated over 100 AI models directly into its video editor — Kling, Sora 2, Veo 3.1, FLUX.2, and more. Instead of switching between tools, you prompt from inside the timeline. Chat-based editing lets you describe changes in natural language and the editor executes them. This is what the unified AI production environment actually looks like when someone builds it.

### Willow's Atlas 1 Speech-to-Text

Willow launched Atlas 1, a speech-to-text model built on what they call "the first scalable, human-powered transcription infrastructure for real-time dictation." The claim: it outperforms ElevenLabs, Deepgram, and OpenAI by a wide margin. With Microsoft's MAI-Transcribe-1 launching the same week, the transcription market just got significantly more competitive. For AV teams doing any kind of captioning, transcription, or script-from-audio workflow, benchmark these against your current tools.

## Tool of the Week

### Google Veo 3.1 Lite

**What it does:** Text-to-video and image-to-video generation at half the cost of Veo 3.1 Fast, with the same generation speed. Supports 4–8 second clips at 720p and 1080p in both landscape (16:9) and portrait (9:16).

**Who it's for:** AV teams experimenting with AI video generation who need affordable iteration. Particularly useful for rapid prototyping — testing concepts, generating reference footage, creating rough cuts before committing to full production.

**Pricing:** Paid preview through Gemini API and Google AI Studio. Half the per-generation cost of Veo 3.1 Fast. Google Vids offers 10 free generations per month on any Google account.

**Worth trying?** Yes. The death of Sora makes Google's pricing strategy more significant — they're positioning Veo as the affordable default for AI video. The quality won't match Kling 3.0 at the high end, but for iteration speed and cost, it's currently the best ratio in the market. Pair it with Kling or Vidu for final production and use Veo Lite for everything else.

**Link:** [Google AI Studio](https://aistudio.google.com)
