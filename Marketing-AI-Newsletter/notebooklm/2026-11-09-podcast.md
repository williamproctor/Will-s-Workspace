### A working paper says "ChatGPT's ads don't pay," and OpenAI swaps bid caps for targets on November 9

On Monday, November 2, Christian Schulze of Frankfurt School opened a LinkedIn post with "ChatGPT's ads don't pay, and fixing them could cost ChatGPT its users." His working paper with Maximilian Kaiser (October, under review) uses Grips Intelligence data on 1,898 online shops, with the ad-free EU as the control; Kaiser advises Grips, as the paper discloses. In US traffic, "a paid ChatGPT visit generates $0.89 in revenue per session against $2.45 for a paid Google Search visit, a 64% shortfall." To return as much per dollar as a search click, a ChatGPT click would have to cost $0.26; "the largest publicly known account" paid $1.72, and OpenAI recommends starting bids of $3 to $5. Of 87 US advertisers buying both channels, 73 recorded no paid ChatGPT sale, and one advertiser accounts for 66% of paid ChatGPT purchases. Organic ChatGPT referrals fell an estimated 6 to 8% with ads on, a result that isn't statistically settled (p = .103).

That night OpenAI's Adam Fry told a press dinner that ChatGPT's "volume of search-like queries is now roughly one-fifth the volume of queries of Google" (Big Technology), counting every prompt that triggers a web search, and, per The Verge, that "most people do not seem to mind ads in the product." Ahrefs' tracker puts ChatGPT at 0.36% of referral traffic (via SEJ). By Friday, OpenAI had emailed advertisers (via SER) that new campaigns get Target CPA and Target CPC from November 9 in place of Max Bid and Max CPC, which retire January 1, 2027. Its help page says, "While Max CPC caps individual bids, Target CPC lets our system bid above or below your target in individual auctions". We found no OpenAI reply to the paper.

**Comment window:** open now, with existing caps retiring January 1. Set the target from your own revenue per paid ChatGPT session against your search revenue per click (the paper's parity method), keep one Max CPC campaign as the comparison, and judge it on incrementality; as Olivia Kory commented on Schulze's post, "Last-click revenue isn't incremental revenue..."
