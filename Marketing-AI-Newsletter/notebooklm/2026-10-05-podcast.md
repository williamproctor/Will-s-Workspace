## Act Two — Lead story

The most important hard-news story this week is **Penske Media and Chegg lost their AI Overviews suits; Reddit and Cloudflare moved to charge AI for access**. Give it the most discussion time.

Judge Amit Mehta of the federal court in Washington, D.C., dismissed Chegg's and Penske Media's antitrust suits over AI Overviews on Wednesday, September 30, in one opinion covering both cases. Their reciprocal-dealing theory needed an agreement trading content for traffic. Mehta found that the plaintiffs had pleaded only that they "have an 'expectation' that Google will send them search traffic if they make their content available for free," and wrote, "But an expectation is not an agreement. It is simply how a general search engine works." On price, he found it more plausible that the publishers were "voluntarily acceding to cost-free crawling" than that the terms came from "a meeting of the minds with Google," and said their theory would mean Google "would have agreements with billions of web content publishers." Neither company sued for copyright infringement, per The Ruling Desk. The opinion says the court "does not treat Plaintiffs' alleged harms lightly" and points elsewhere with a line from a 2009 decision: "that is an issue for Congress [or regulators] to consider."

Outside the courtroom, The Information reported on Tuesday that Google's AI contribution pilot (our September 21 story) has admitted about 100 publishers, and several small and midsize ones said the payments come to about a tenth of a percent of their ad revenue. One early publisher is on track for more than $1 million a year, and several small sites got under $1,000 over several months, per Ars Technica's summary; Google hasn't published rates. On Wednesday, Reddit said on r/modnews that it will stop supporting RSS feeds on November 13, calling RSS "a common surface for large-scale scraping and automated abuse," and TechRepublic's reading of the update has Reddit taking no new requests for public API access from October 31 and ending remaining public Data API access in March 2027. In TechCrunch's reading, AI assistants "will need commercial deals with Reddit for its data."

Also on Wednesday, Cloudflare opened a closed beta of its Monetization Gateway, which "allows domain owners to charge agents for access to their website, APIs, MCP tools, or datasets" by answering requests with HTTP 402 Payment Required; payments settle in USDC on the Base blockchain through Coinbase's x402 Facilitator. Buyers and sellers must be in the US for now, and product manager Rohin Lohe told Fortune the plan is to be "widely available by early 2027." For pages, Cloudflare's post points to Pay Per Use, where verified buyers report each use and pay for it, because "a page might be crawled once and used a thousand times."

### Publishers' ad tech claims against Google head to trial; Google and the DOJ split on the decree's deadlines

In New York on Wednesday, September 30, Judge P. Kevin Castel denied most of Google's summary judgment motions in the publisher cases of the ad tech multidistrict litigation. Gannett (now USA TODAY Co.) and the Daily Mail lost only their New York General Business Law claims, the AdX publisher class (about 5,000 publishers, per Bloomberg Law) survived, and Google won against The Progressive and Inform. Castel also refused to exclude the plaintiffs' damages experts, whose estimates (not awards) put the Daily Mail's damages at $599,955,198, Gannett's at $900,771,489, and the class's at $1.72 billion through March 31, 2024. On Friday, Law360 reported that Castel had penciled in April 5, 2027, for the first trial, with the Daily Mail and Gannett.

In Virginia the same Friday, the Justice Department, Virginia and 16 other states, and Google filed one proposed Final Judgment, as Judge Leonie Brinkema had ordered on September 2, with each side's language labeled where they couldn't agree. The agreed text runs six years, bars Google from reimplementing First Look or Last Look in DFP or AdX, gives DFP publishers header bidding trafficking within six months, and says "Nothing in this Final Judgment applies to AdSense, AdMob, DV360, or any other current Google advertising technology tool besides Google Ad Manager or Google Ads." The brackets cover the deadline for the AdX–Prebid adapter and for AdX interoperability with rival publisher ad servers ("No later than [Plaintiffs: 6] [Google: 12] months from the Effective Date"), which side proposes the three monitor candidates, and a Google-only subsection that would limit the timelines to the US, with the rest of the world "by a time not to exceed 24 months after the Effective Date."

The judgment isn't entered, and Brinkema decides the bracketed terms. For advertisers, the agreed scope covers Google Ad Manager and Google Ads and leaves DV360 out; for publishers, the gap between 6 and 12 months decides how soon AdX can bid through Prebid and into rival ad servers.
