# Monday Signal — Week of September 7, 2026

> **Two ad auctions had their integrity adjudicated in one week, with opposite momentum: on Monday the FTC and 22 states accused Amazon of rigging seven years of ad auctions with an invented bidder that extracted $20 billion or more, and on Wednesday a federal judge let Google keep its ad-tech stack with behavioral remedies only.** In between, Google's AI Max migration went live as a month-long window rather than a switch, Gemini 3.8 Flash shipped into AI Mode and stopped citing sources for two days, and Search Console's AI reports finally reached every site on earth without a click column. On the feed, Xero paid an influencer to tell small businesses to replace their accountants with Claude, and Xero's own sales channel revolted.

## The Conversation

### Xero paid an influencer to tell small businesses to fire their accountants

Personal-branding strategist Amelia Sordell posted a Xero-sponsored Instagram story (tagged #xeropartner, discount link attached) saying she'd spent more than £120,000 on accountants since 2020 and now connects Xero to Claude to produce the management accounts she used to pay £800 a month for. Xero sells through accountants and bookkeepers; they're the channel that recommends it to the 4.9 million small businesses on the platform. LinkedIn did the rest between September 3 and 5. Xero pulled the post, and UK managing director Kate Hayward apologized for an ad that "does not reflect our values… and it should never have run."

The practitioner reads were sharper than the trade coverage. Tom McNally: "This is the innovator's dilemma playing out in public… they can see that the money is in the service, not the software. But they can't move into services without turning on the people who built them." Rumana Jeffreys on the mechanism: "The £800 comparison reframes the accountant's fee as the cost of producing a spreadsheet, rather than the cost of judgement, interpretation and advice." Lucien Livsey's summary headline did numbers on its own: "Xero just ran an ad telling small businesses to fire their accountants. It didn't land well."

**Comment window:** open and squarely in GrowthX's lane: B2B software, influencer programs, AI positioning. The unclaimed angle is the brief. The creator said the quiet part out loud because nobody in the approval chain owned the channel relationship, which makes influencer briefs the place where channel strategy leaks.

### Suno pulled a Mary J. Blige ad after saying she never authorized it

An 85-second Suno spot circulated over the weekend: Blige in a studio while a producer builds a track called "Pretty Flowers" with the company's generative tools, Blige suggesting strings and a beat and reacting warmly to the result. Shorter cuts spread fast and fans read it as the Queen of Hip-Hop Soul endorsing AI music. Then Suno told Variety: "We entered into a business deal with someone who presented themselves as Ms. Blige's official representative. As soon as we learned this was not the case, and that Ms. Blige was uncomfortable, we terminated the advertising campaign." Blige clearly sat for the session; the campaign built from it apparently had no valid sign-off. Suno hasn't named the intermediary or explained how the deal reached distribution, and it faces separate copyright litigation from rights holders.

**Comment window:** open this week. The transferable lesson is chain of custody for talent rights in AI-company campaigns, the one category where "who actually signed" is the entire story.

### OpenAI's launch film angered the community whose tool it borrowed

OpenAI's ad for its new Astra model stages a demo in the style of MIT's 1979 "Put That There" project and shows a fashion designer working alone with a screen, with Blender, the free, community-built 3D tool, doing the work on it. Blender's users objected to seeing software that exists to teach people the craft used to sell the idea that the craft is optional; the artist behind the current Blender splash screen said she was disgusted to find her work in the ad, and one widely shared post put it less politely: "Stay tf away from human-made art." Creative Bloq's read on the spot's worldview: the future of creative work as one person in a room talking to a screen.

That makes four AI-lab marketing misfires we've tracked since August (Meta's optimism ad, Anthropic's doom reel, OpenAI's Summer Club, now Astra), plus Samsung's SSD spot and Suno's unauthorized campaign in a single fortnight. The failure is the same each time: the audience with the most to lose was the audience the campaign reached first.

**Comment window:** narrowing on the ad, durable on the pattern.

## This Week in Marketing AI

### The FTC says Amazon rigged its ad auction with an invented bidder for seven years

The complaint, filed Monday in the Western District of Washington with a bipartisan group of 22 states, alleges that since 2019 Amazon "secretly and systematically overcharged its approximately 1.2 million advertising customers by manipulating the 'auctions' that it uses to set the price of ads." The mechanism is a hidden "soft reserve price" implemented through what an internal document called an "invented auction participant": a manufactured bid advertisers had to beat. The FTC says the effect turned a marketed second-price auction into a first-price one, with Sponsored Products advertisers charged their full winning bid close to 80% of the time, and surcharges rising on Prime Day and holiday peaks. Alleged extraction: $20 billion or more. Internal notes from a 2024 discussion between the head of Amazon Ads and the company's chief digital economist called the "clever non-transparent way to charge first price" an "incredibly effective way to drive revenue."

Amazon called the suit "misguided" in a blog post, said it "fundamentally misunderstands how advertisers operate," and noted average CPC was flat from 2019 to 2024 while sales per click rose. That defense is about outcomes; the complaint is about disclosure. Amazon's auctions page has mentioned reserve pricing since an April update, which the FTC says still fails to disclose "the nature, scope, and extent" of the system. For anyone bidding on a $68 billion ad platform, the practical question is immediate: your bid strategy assumed a second-price auction, and the government says you were in a first-price one.

### Google keeps AdX and DFP; the remedies are behavioral and mostly sealed

Two days after the Amazon filing, Judge Leonie Brinkema issued a two-page order in the Justice Department's ad-tech case: the structural remedies (divesting AdX, open-sourcing DFP's final auction logic, a contingent divestiture of the rest of DFP) are rejected, and "most of the parties' proposed behavioral remedies, as modified by this Court," are accepted. The memorandum opinion explaining which ones is sealed for fourteen days while the parties propose redactions; a joint proposed Final Judgment is due within thirty. Google's Lee-Anne Mulholland was "very pleased." It's the second time in a year Google has avoided a breakup after being found a monopolist, following the Chrome outcome in the search case.

What's on the table matters for buyers even before the opinion unseals. The behavioral proposals included real-time bid data for rival publisher ad servers, an end to Unified Pricing Rules, no first-look or last-look advantages, and non-discrimination toward third-party exchanges and servers. The DOJ's Matthew Huppert argued Google would "test boundaries every step of the way" under behavioral terms; Google's Karen Dunn argued there was "no evidence there would be a buyer" for AdX. Publishers including The Atlantic, USA Today, Vox Media, and Penske have damages suits pending on the April 2025 liability finding, so the money question moves to those courtrooms.

### The AI Max migration is live, and it's a window, not a switch

September 1 arrived and Google began converting Search campaigns that still used campaign-level broad match or standalone Automatically Created Assets to AI Max, in place, progressively through September 30. Campaign IDs survive because AI Max is a setting, not a campaign type. The broad-match cohort gets search-term matching switched on; the ACA cohort also gets text customization, which means Google rewriting headlines and descriptions from your site content unless you turn it off after the fact. Brand inclusions and exclusions carry over. There's no opting out of the conversion; there is changing the settings afterward. Google Ads API v25.1 exposes per-campaign timestamps (`campaign.aca_migration_date_time` and `campaign.broad_match_migration_date_time`; join them with OR, not AND), and `campaign.ai_max_setting.enable_ai_max` turns "which of ours got upgraded" into a query.

The independent evidence points both ways, which is why the reporting discipline matters. Smarter Ecommerce measured a median 13% increase in conversion value with a median ROAS difference of zero across more than 250 retail campaigns; Lunio measured invalid traffic diverging (AI Max campaigns rising to 5.28% while standard search ended at 3.07%); Alphabet's filings show paid clicks growing 13% in each of the last two quarters while CPC growth slowed to 3%. Improvado's reporting note is the one to pin: don't sum the classic search-term report with the new AI Max search-term view, and check expanded final URLs, because the landing page that served may not be the one you configured.

### Gemini 3.8 Flash landed in AI Mode and forgot to cite anyone

Google put Gemini 3.8 Flash into AI Mode for Pro and Ultra subscribers on September 2, two weeks after 3.7 Flash, with Robby Stein announcing it on X. By the next morning Gagan Ghotra had posted a run of top-of-funnel queries in which the new model returned answers with no links and no citations at all, and Glenn Gabe replicated the difference side by side against the default model. Barry Schwartz reported it on Search Engine Roundtable; Stein replied within hours: "This isn't working as intended, and we'll roll out a fix soon." Links were reappearing by September 4.

A two-day bug that only hit paying subscribers sounds minor, and the lesson isn't. Whatever visibility a brand has inside AI answers is downstream of model defaults it doesn't control, and a routine model swap switched citations off without anyone at Google noticing until practitioners posted screenshots. Google's fix arrived because two SEOs tagged the right VP on X. Build the visibility plan on that assumption.

### Search Console's AI reports and opt-out reached every site, still without clicks

Google updated three help documents with the same line: "As of August 31, 2026, we've rolled out this control to all websites worldwide." Barry Schwartz's headline captured the history: "For real this time." The generative AI performance reports (impressions in AI Overviews, AI Mode, and Discover's AI features by page, country, date, and device) began with a handful of UK sites in June under a CMA conduct requirement, spread through July and August, and are now global, subject to a minimum-impressions threshold that keeps small sites from seeing one. The Search generative AI control went global the same day: a toggle that removes a site from AI features as a link or as grounding, which Google says is not a ranking signal for core search. Opt out and you get no AI traffic or impressions; stay in and you can count impressions but not clicks, queries, or positions.

The timing is the story for measurement teams. The reports reached everyone on August 31; the AI Max migration started converting campaigns on September 1; the Gemini 3.8 citation bug ran September 2 to 4. Every AI-visibility question a client can ask this month now has a dashboard that answers half of it.

## Common Threads

### The auction is the product, and the product is under review

Amazon's alleged invented bidder, Google's court-modified behavioral remedies, and Google's own auto-migration to AI Max all ask buyers to trust auction mechanics they can't inspect. In the same week, regulators said one of those auctions was rigged and a judge declined to break up another that was already ruled a monopoly.

### Marketing to the people your product displaces

Xero to accountants, Samsung to filmmakers, OpenAI to Blender artists, Suno to musicians. Four campaigns in two weeks reached the audience with the most to lose before anyone else, and each company apologized or pulled the work. The brief never asked who would see it first.

### Citations are a feature flag now

A model swap made AI Mode stop linking for two days, and the fix arrived because two SEOs tagged a VP on X. Visibility inside AI answers rests on defaults nobody outside the platform controls, which is the strongest argument this month for owning first-party demand.

### Measurement arrives after the migration

Search Console's AI reports went global the day before AI Max started converting campaigns, and they still don't count clicks. The migration question and the click question stay open at the same time, on the same dashboards.

## Angles for the Week

### An invented bidder: what a first-price auction does to your Amazon strategy

The complaint's core allegation is that Sponsored Products advertisers paid their full bid about 80% of the time. Whether or not it holds up, the practical post is what changes in bid strategy when you assume first-price mechanics, and how to read Amazon's reserve-pricing disclosure. Window: open now; reopens with every filing.

### Google kept the stack. Here's what behavioral remedies could change for buyers

Real-time bid data for rival ad servers, no first look, no Unified Pricing Rules: each proposed remedy has a buyer-side consequence, and the opinion unseals around September 16. A plain-English preview now, updated when the text lands, owns the topic twice. Window: two weeks, then again at the Final Judgment.

### The brief was the problem

Xero's influencer didn't invent the "replace your accountant" message; she was briefed to sell savings to business owners by a company whose revenue arrives through accountants. The post to write is the channel-aware influencer brief: who sees this first, what they lose, what the approval chain is for. Window: open; GrowthX-native.

### Your citations are someone else's default

Two days without links in AI Mode, fixed by a tweet. The argument for first-party demand (email, community, direct) writes itself when the alternative depends on a model flag. Window: open, durable.

### It's a window, not a switch

Through September 30, campaigns are converting on Google's schedule, not yours. The checklist post: query the migration timestamps, check `enable_ai_max` daily, separate AI Max search terms from classic ones in reporting, and verify expanded final URLs against the landing pages legal approved. Window: through September 30.
