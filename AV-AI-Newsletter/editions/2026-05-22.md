## This Week in AI

### Gemini Omni Flash — Google's New Multimodal Video Model Ships to Every Surface

Google introduced **Gemini Omni** at the I/O 2026 keynote on May 19, the first model in a new "Omni" family that — in Google's framing — can "create anything from any input." The first shipped model is **Gemini Omni Flash**, which targets video as its initial output modality. Image and audio outputs are on the roadmap; nothing in the Omni family has a developer API yet, though one is "coming in the weeks following launch."

**What's new versus Veo and Nano Banana.** Google positions Omni Flash as a step up from the existing Veo 3.1 + Nano Banana stack on three specific dimensions:

- **Multimodal input in a single prompt** — combine text, images, video clips, and audio references in one request and the model reasons across all of them
- **Conversational editing** — "swap the background, swap the wardrobe, add a cinematic zoom, change the character's voice" are now prompts, not multi-step workflows
- **Real-world physics + scene continuity** — Google's stated focus area on the new model is movement, object interaction, and shot-to-shot continuity that earlier video models have struggled with

**Character consistency** is the feature that lands hardest for AV teams. Google says Omni Flash preserves identity, lens look, and voice across scenes automatically, without requiring the user to specify it in each prompt. The same camera lens look is applied across every shot in a project; characters retain their identity across cuts; the model maintains a stylistic baseline across an entire session.

**Availability and pricing**:

- **Gemini app + Google Flow** — globally available today to Google AI Plus ($7.99/mo), Pro ($19.99/mo), and Ultra ($249.99/mo) subscribers
- **YouTube Shorts Remix + YouTube Create app** — free to creators 18+, with watermarking, identifying metadata, a link back to the source video, and opt-out for original creators
- **Maximum clip length** — 10 seconds per clip. Product director Nicole Brichtova was explicit that this is a "product decision, not a technical limitation" — the cap will rise as Google calibrates safety policies for longer formats
- **Enterprise/developer API** — Gemini API and Agent Platform API, "coming in the weeks following launch," with SynthID watermarking and C2PA on all API outputs and reserved-throughput SLAs available
- A **Gemini Omni Pro** variant is on the roadmap; no release date

**The honest assessment.** Early creator coverage notes the raw video quality of Omni Flash still trails Seedance 2.0 and Kling for cinematic output. The bet Google is making isn't "best-looking 10-second clip." It's "best conversational editing surface on top of a frontier-level generative video model that sits inside the platform you already use to do post." For AV teams whose work needs YouTube Shorts assets or rapid client variations, the no-cost YouTube Shorts/Create access is the practical entry point this week. For higher-end pre-vis and concept work, Flow + Omni Flash is the surface to evaluate. For production-pipeline integration, the API arriving in the coming weeks is the inflection point to plan around.

### Gemini 3.5 Flash — Frontier-Class Model, 4× the Speed, Less Than Half the Cost

Gemini 3.5 Flash is Google's headline I/O model launch — the first member of a "Gemini 3.5" family that Google has positioned as "frontier intelligence with action," meaning frontier-class capability plus agentic execution as the primary use case. Gemini 3.5 Pro is coming next month; Google is using it internally now.

**The pitch in numbers** (per Google's internal evaluations):

- **4× faster output tokens/second** versus comparable frontier models
- **Less than half the cost** of comparable frontier models on equivalent workloads
- **Beats Claude Sonnet 4.6, Claude Opus 4.7, and GPT-5.5** on:
  - **MCP Atlas + Toolathlon** — agentic tool-use benchmarks
  - **Finance agent v2** — multi-step financial analysis
  - **MMMU Pro** — multimodal understanding
  - **MRCR v2 (1M pointwise)** — long-context information processing

**The marquee demo**: Antigravity, Google's agent-first development platform, used Gemini 3.5 Flash to build **a fully functional operating system in 12 hours**, running **93 agents in parallel**, with total API costs **under $1,000**. The number to anchor on isn't the OS — it's the orchestration. Ninety-three concurrent agents reasoning, planning, and executing tool calls against a shared codebase, finishing in twelve hours of wall-clock time, on a budget that any AV pro could expense for a single project.

**Internal traction**: Google reports Gemini 3.5 Flash is currently driving **3 trillion-plus tokens per day** of internal usage across its AI developer tools, up from 500 billion tokens per day in March. Google's internal usage is the leading indicator most worth tracking: the company has the most aggressive deployment surface and the most honest cost discipline of any frontier-model provider.

**Where it's available today**:

- Gemini app and AI Mode in Search (default model globally)
- Gemini API in Google AI Studio and Android Studio (for developers)
- Antigravity, the agent-first dev platform (for agentic workloads)
- Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform + Gemini Enterprise (for orgs)

**For AV teams**, the immediate question is whether Gemini 3.5 Flash is now the best agentic model for production AV workflows. The honest answer is "probably worth A/B testing against your current stack this week." Anthropic and OpenAI will both ship counter-positioning within the next four to six weeks; this isn't a settled state. But the cost-versus-capability point on Google's curve has moved enough that anyone whose AV agents currently run on Claude Sonnet 4.6 or GPT-5.5 should at minimum sanity-check what their token economics look like on Gemini 3.5 Flash.

### Google Flow Becomes a Creative Copilot

Flow is the most AV-relevant product update in Google's I/O package, full stop. Google introduced Flow at I/O 2025 as a single-prompt video generation tool. The 2026 update is the platform release: Flow is now an end-to-end creative production environment with an agent, a programmable tools layer, and mobile clients on both iOS and Android.

**Flow Agent** — multi-step planning and project-spanning execution. The agent handles brainstorming, storyboard development, scene generation, dialogue writing, character art, and multi-variation generation in a single conversational thread. The most useful affordance is **batch editing**: a change applied at the project level (a different lighting setup, a wardrobe shift, a tonal recolor) propagates across every shot in the project without re-prompting each one individually. Flow Agent is rolling out to all Flow users globally.

**Flow Tools** — vibe-code your own utilities. Users can now build custom Flow tools — image editors, video resizers, custom shaders, hand-drawn animation passes, text-layering utilities — in natural language, no coding required. Built tools can be shared with other Flow users, who can remix them. All Flow users globally can use existing Flow Tools; Google AI subscribers can create and remix. This is the first major creative app to ship a user-built-tool-sharing surface analogous to what Cursor has for IDE rules, or what Apple's Shortcuts has for OS automations.

**Flow Music** — section-by-section song editing, full-track covers, music video creation. The Gemini Omni integration in Flow Music means a user can generate a song with structural awareness (intros, verses, choruses, bridges via Lyria 3 Pro), then generate a synchronized music video for it via Omni Flash — all in one project.

**Mobile**: Flow and Flow Music both shipped native iOS and Android apps this week. Web remains the primary platform for full feature access; mobile is positioned for on-the-go creation, capture-and-iterate flows, and the rough-cut work editors typically can't do in front of a workstation.

**The numbers**: Flow has shipped to **140+ countries**, has generated **1.5 billion images and videos** since its 2025 launch, and Google reports **50 billion+ images created with Nano Banana** since Banana's summer 2025 release. VP for Google Labs Roman Yusuf framed the platform's repositioning: *"Flow is evolving from this prompt-in, content-out tool to an agent that's a copilot at every step of the creative process."*

**For AV teams**, Flow is now in a different category than it was at launch. The combination of Flow Agent (project-spanning), Flow Tools (custom utility creation), and Flow Music (audio + music video) plus Omni Flash inside the same surface puts Flow in the same conversation as Runway, Pika, and Adobe Firefly Video as production-pipeline candidates. The honest assessment: it's still maturing, and for high-end work the existing Resolve/Premiere/After Effects pipelines remain canonical. But for rapid prototyping, social-first content, pre-vis, mood-board work, and music-video work for branded content, Flow now reads as "credible production tool" rather than "interesting consumer demo."

### Lyria 3 + Lyria 3 Pro Ship for Developers

Google's music-generation play landed with less fanfare than Omni or 3.5 Flash but is the more meaningful release for audio-side AV workflows. **Lyria 3 Clip** and **Lyria 3 Pro** are both available in public preview through the Gemini API and Vertex AI as of this week.

**The two models**:

- **Lyria 3 Clip** (`lyria-3-clip-preview`) — 30-second high-quality clips, 44.1 kHz stereo, MP3 output. Optimized for speed and high-volume request workloads. Use cases: background loops, social-media stings, branded short-form audio
- **Lyria 3 Pro** (`lyria-3-pro-preview`) — full songs up to **~3 minutes** with prompt-controllable duration, full structural awareness (intros, verses, choruses, bridges), MP3 or WAV output, 44.1 kHz stereo

**Capabilities that distinguish Lyria 3 from the previous Lyria 2 series**:

- **Vocals + timed lyrics** — generate vocals with timestamps, or pass in your own lyrics for the model to perform
- **Multimodal input** — text prompt + up to 10 reference images that guide style, mood, and visual-to-audio coherence
- **Eight languages** for vocal generation — English, German, Spanish, French, Hindi, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese
- **SynthID watermarking + C2PA** on all outputs

**Pricing on the Lyria 2 reference is $0.06 per 30 seconds of generated audio** — Lyria 3 specific per-token pricing isn't fully published yet, but the Lyria 2 anchor sets expectations for the Pro tier somewhere in the $0.10-0.20/minute range. Generations are capped at 10 regional online prediction requests per minute per base model in the preview period.

**Workspace integration**: Google Workspace customers and Google AI subscribers can use Lyria 3 inside **Google Vids** to add a custom track that matches a brand's style — meaning the music generation surface is wired into the corporate-video creation flow that AV teams already use for internal client work.

**For AV teams**, Lyria 3 sits next to **ElevenLabs Expressive Mode** (covered May 8) and the **OpenAI Realtime audio stack** (covered May 15) as the third credible foundation-model surface for audio-side workflows in active production. The three models target different problems — Lyria for music, ElevenLabs for character voice, OpenAI Realtime for live conversation — but the combined effect is that every audio surface an AV team touches now has a strong AI-native foundation option. Pair Lyria 3 Pro with Omni Flash inside Flow and the pre-vis-with-temp-score workflow is a single integrated session.

### Karpathy Joins Anthropic, Plus Anthropic's Other Big Week

**Andrej Karpathy joined Anthropic on May 19** — one of the most consequential individual hires in AI this year. The signaling, even more than the personnel.

**The role**: Karpathy reports to pre-training team lead **Nick Joseph** and is building a brand-new team focused on **using Claude to accelerate Claude pre-training research itself**. Pre-training is the most compute-intensive and most expensive phase of building a frontier model. The bet Anthropic is making with this hire isn't "more compute" — it's "use AI-assisted research to make the pre-training process itself more efficient and more effective." If the bet works, the structural economics of building frontier models shift in Anthropic's favor.

**Why Karpathy specifically**: He co-founded OpenAI as one of the original 11 in 2015, led AI at Tesla (FSD, Autopilot), returned to OpenAI for a year, then ran Eureka Labs (his AI-education startup) before joining Anthropic this week. He's one of the few people in the industry who can credibly bridge LLM theory (his nanoGPT and the "neural networks: zero to hero" YouTube series), large-scale practical training (Tesla scale), and modern frontier model deployment.

**The framing on X**: *"I've joined Anthropic. I think the next few years at the frontier of LLMs will be especially formative. I am very excited to join the team here and get back to R&D."*

**Anthropic also brought on Chris Rohlf** to the frontier red team — a 20-year cybersecurity veteran (Yahoo, Meta) — formalizing Anthropic's continued investment in the adversarial-testing layer that distinguishes its safety positioning.

**The product-side context**: Anthropic shipped meaningfully more than just hires this week:

- **May 18** — Web search tool returns richer SEC filing data, easier grounding for financial-research agents
- **May 19** — **MCP tunnels in research preview** (connect Claude Managed Agents to MCP servers in your private network); **self-hosted sandboxes for Managed Agents** (run tool execution outside Anthropic infrastructure); live updates to MCP servers and tool configs during active sessions; large outputs (>100K tokens) automatically spill to a sandbox file with a truncated preview the model can read from
- **May 21** — **Claude Compliance API integrations** with security and compliance tools for governance across the platform
- Claude Code shipped four releases (v2.1.143 → v2.1.146) through the week with MCP pagination and reliability fixes

**For AV teams**, the MCP tunnels release is the one to track. Production AV networks often have private infrastructure (Frame.io enterprise tiers, on-prem asset libraries, internal review platforms) that can't be exposed to Anthropic's cloud infrastructure for security reasons. MCP tunnels mean a Claude Managed Agent can now reach those private MCP servers without exposing them publicly. The self-hosted sandboxes release closes a parallel gap — tool execution stays inside the customer's network. Both are enterprise-grade releases that move Claude Managed Agents from "interesting prototype" to "deployable inside a production AV environment with security review."

The broader story: Anthropic is making two parallel bets — research velocity (Karpathy) and enterprise deployment surface (MCP tunnels, sandboxes, Compliance API) — that together set up the company for the **late-2026 IPO that's reportedly the explicit target** at a ~$800B valuation. Whether the bets land is a 2027 question; this week was the setup.

### OpenAI's Reasoning Model Disproves an 80-Year-Old Math Conjecture

On May 20, OpenAI announced that an internal general-purpose reasoning model had disproved the **planar unit distance conjecture**, an 80-year-old open problem in discrete geometry first posed by Paul Erdős in 1946. The proof has been externally verified by **Fields Medalist Tim Gowers** and by mathematicians including Noga Alon, Melanie Wood, Thomas Bloom, Daniel Litt, Will Sawin, Arul Shankar, Jacob Tsimerman, and Victor Wang.

**Why this matters even though it's not a video model**:

For 80 years, mathematicians believed the best constructions for the unit distance problem were essentially square-grid arrangements. OpenAI's model produced **a fundamentally new family of constructions** — yielding approximately n^(1+0.014) unit distances among n points (where the previous best was essentially linear in n with only logarithmic improvement). The exponent 0.014 looks small. It isn't. It's the first **polynomial improvement** on the problem in 80 years.

**The construction draws on algebraic number theory** — specifically, infinite class field towers — to build the new configurations. This is a cross-field connection no human mathematician had previously made. OpenAI's framing on the model: *"AI systems are now more capable of holding together long, difficult chains of reasoning and connecting ideas across fields in ways researchers may not have previously explored."*

OpenAI calls this "the first time AI has autonomously solved a prominent open problem central to a field of mathematics." The earlier false claim from a different research group earlier this spring (briefly viral, retracted within days) gave this announcement higher scrutiny than usual — Tim Gowers's validation is what makes the May 20 announcement credible.

**Why this matters for AV teams indirectly**: The same capability — *"hold together long, difficult chains of reasoning, connect ideas across fields"* — is the underlying primitive for the agentic workflow tools AV teams are increasingly relying on. A model that can autonomously solve an 80-year-old open problem is the same model class that can autonomously hold together a 30-step production pipeline. The Erdős proof isn't a video model. It's a credibility marker for the entire reasoning-model category that the production-agent surface depends on. If reasoning models can disprove decades-old conjectures, the question of whether they can autonomously handle a multi-step asset-versioning workflow gets a different answer than it had three weeks ago.

The strategic context: OpenAI's reported IPO target is September 2026. A verified mathematical breakthrough sharpens the company's narrative that its reasoning models are approaching or exceeding human-expert capabilities in cognitively demanding fields.

### Cannes 2026 Wrap: "AI Came Out of the Closet"

Cannes 2026 closes tomorrow (May 23). The festival press has converged on a single framing for the wrap: **AI "came out of the closet"** this year. The phrase comes from Variety's May 19 takeaways and has propagated through the Hollywood Reporter, Stephen Follows's Marché du Film analysis, and the secondary press cycle since.

**The numbers**: 40,000 professionals at the festival, 16,000 registered at the Marché du Film from 140 countries. The Marché hosted **250 events** including conferences and panels, of which **45+ were AI-focused**. The "AI on the Lot" satellite conference (Todd Terrazas) has grown from 600 attendees in 2024 → 1,200 in 2025 → an expected **2,000 in June 2026** at the Amazon lot in Culver City. The World AI Film Festival ran alongside the main festival, drawing **5,500 submissions from 80 countries** in its second year. Cannes formalized an "AI for Talent Summit" inside the Marché — invite-only, focused on how AI shifts the agent/talent business model.

**The defining quote** came from Demi Moore at the jury press conference (May 12). Asked by Variety about AI in the industry and whether it should be more regulated: *"AI is here. And so to fight it is to fight something that is a battle that we will lose. So to find ways in which we can work with it I think is a more valuable path to take."* When asked whether the industry is doing enough to protect itself: *"I don't know the answer to that. My inclination would be to say probably not."* Moore added that AI *"can never replace what true art comes from, which is not the physical — it comes from the soul."* The quote landed in part because of who said it (jury member, not random spokesperson) and in part because it was the first major Hollywood voice to say out loud what the rest of the room was already operating on: AI's role is settled; the open questions are procedural.

**Stephen Follows**, in his Marché takeaways, framed the shift more precisely: *"AI is regarded as an analyst, not an author."* Producers, distributors, and sales companies at the Marché openly discussed using AI for project analysis, audience simulation, focus-group testing, storyboarding, and marketing-copy generation. Discussion of AI as a creative author — generating performances, scripts, or final-cut sequences — remained contested, with **Steven Soderbergh's John Lennon documentary** (10% AI-generated footage, covered last week) as the most prominent test case.

**Hollywood, notably, stayed home.** Christopher Nolan and Steven Spielberg both declined invitations. Studios cited budget constraints and "fear of negative French critical reception damaging box office." The withdrawal created a gap, and independent AI-assisted productions filled it. Doug Liman's Bitcoin: Killing Satoshi (covered last week), Soderbergh's Lennon doc, Stephen Kay's Answr, the Storyverse slate, the Critterz follow-through, and the World AI Film Festival's 5,500 submissions all converged at the Marché. The official competition continued its formal ban on AI-generated work — making the Marché's openness the visible counterpoint.

**The Gossip Goblin story is the leading indicator worth tracking.** Zack London's eight-person AI film outfit, run out of a Stockholm apartment with off-the-shelf AI tools, has hit **500 million-plus views** on satirical AI shorts. LA talent agents, producers, screenwriters, and A-list actors flew to Stockholm this spring to meet him. The pattern — kitchen-table AI studios producing audience-validated work without geographic, capital, or studio gatekeeping — is the structural shift Cannes 2026 acknowledged but didn't quite know how to absorb. Expect 10–20 more visible Gossip Goblin-equivalents by year-end as the cohort that watched this Cannes from the outside decides the timing is right.

**The Meta partnership** with the festival (Meta House at the Majestic Hotel; Ray-Ban Meta smart glasses on a brigade of influencers; the Soderbergh documentary as a direct product of the partnership) drew a high-profile public backlash but didn't slow the underlying acceptance trend. The festival signed a multi-year deal with Meta. The deal will run regardless of the criticism it draws this week.

**The wrap matters because the underlying questions are now settled.** Cannes 2024 and 2025 were "should AI be in films?" Cannes 2026 was "what are the new disclosure norms, what are the new credit conventions, what are the new business models?" The Human Provenance disclosure standard (covered last week) ships into this context as the procedural answer to the question Demi Moore left open. SAG-AFTRA's next negotiating cycle is the next watch-item. Some streamer will require disclosure to a recognized AI-use standard within 18 months; the watch is which streamer, when, and which standard.

## Tip of the Week

### Build Your Memory Layer This Weekend — Start a Decisions Log

Two weeks ago we built a one-page personal context file (who you are, what your work is). Last week we built a reusable AI skill (one recurring task, captured as a reusable instruction set). This week's tip is the next layer: **start a decisions log.**

**The problem it solves.** Even with a strong context file and a library of skills, the AI you work with still doesn't remember *what you've already decided*. You and the model worked through the right way to handle a tricky vendor handoff three weeks ago. You documented the choice nowhere. Now this week's prompt re-asks the same question, the model re-reasons from scratch, and you re-argue your way back to the same answer. Multiply that by every recurring decision you make and the lost time compounds.

**What goes in a decisions log.** One row per decision. Five fields, that's it:

1. **Date** — when you made the decision
2. **Decision** — one sentence: what you decided
3. **Context** — the situation that prompted it (two to three sentences)
4. **Reasoning** — why you chose this option over the alternatives
5. **Status** — *active* / *superseded* (link to the replacement) / *abandoned*

**The five categories every AV professional should be logging from week one**:

- **Tool choices** — "we use Frame.io because the SSO integration with our Okta tenant is cleaner than DaVinci Resolve's"
- **Workflow norms** — "we always cut at 24p for client review even when delivering 30p — the review feedback is more honest at 24p for some reason"
- **Client and stakeholder preferences** — "Director X hates yellow color casts" / "Producer Y wants captions burned in by default for first cuts" / "Department head Z prefers Slack DMs over email for time-sensitive approvals"
- **Format and deliverable specs** — "standard SDR master is BT.1886 + 16-235; standard HDR master is HDR10 P3-D65; archive master is ProRes 4444 12-bit"
- **Vendor relationships** — "Vendor X delivers reliably but is slow; Vendor Y is faster but quality is inconsistent above 4K"

**Where to drop it.** Same places as the context file and the skills — a `DECISIONS.md` next to your `AGENTS.md`, a Claude Project file, a Notion database (if you want filtering), or a Google Doc paste-able into prompts. The format doesn't matter; the discipline of writing it down does. Reference it from prompts when continuing work: *"check my decisions log for our standard SDR master format before suggesting changes."*

**Why this builds compound leverage.** Skills (last week) tell AI *how* to do recurring work. Memory tells AI *what's already been decided*. Without memory, the model re-litigates settled questions every session. With memory, the model picks up where you and a previous-you left off — and future-you can audit your own past reasoning when a decision goes sideways. The compound effect over six months is that the AI you work with starts to *agree with you* about how your work is supposed to run, because it has the receipts.

This is Step 3 of the Agent OS framework. Week 1 (May 8) was **Identity + Context**. Week 2 (May 15) was **Skills**. Week 3 (this week) is **Memory**. Week 4 (next week, May 29) covers **Connections** — read-only access patterns for email, calendar, Slack, and your asset library, so your agent stack starts to *see* your actual work in addition to remembering what you've decided about it.
