## The Conversation

### John Lewis's Christmas ad drew scorn on X and 5.6 stars from System1, and Dior had to explain its "all hand-drawn" film

John Lewis premiered "Gloria and Pickles," its 20th Christmas ad, on Tuesday, November 3. The same day Sarah Vine called its children "Spoilt, entitled, rude and overindulged," and Piers Morgan called it "possibly the worst advert I've ever seen for anything" (on X, as quoted by the Mirror; we didn't locate the posts). Carma's social listening put sentiment at 77% negative (DecisionMarketing). System1, which tests UK TV ads on a nationally representative sample, gave it 5.6 stars, John Lewis's best since 2019 (LBB), and on Wednesday System1's Andrew Tindall posted that "10% reacted negatively but only 6% felt real contempt. But 69% loved it."

Dior launched "Christian Dior's Night in Color," a six-minute film by Sylvain Chomet, on Wednesday with Jonathan Anderson's statement that it "is all hand-drawn using techniques that go back to the 1940s" (WWD). By Thursday, posts on X were accusing it of AI, and on Saturday producer Aton Soumache told Variety, "We used an automated interpolation system for a very small part of the process, but the film itself is traditional 2D animation." Neither filmmaker named the software (Cartoon Brew), and we found no statement from Dior. Very had disclosed a "hybrid CGI and AI-assisted production process" in its November 2 release; its ad scored 5.9 stars on System1 (per MSQ) and drew no backlash we could find.

**Comment window:** open through the rest of the Christmas launch season. Very disclosed AI assistance on day one; Dior's launch materials were "explicit in describing the film as hand-drawn" (Cartoon Brew), and the interpolation answer came three days later. Put a line in the press kit saying what was drawn, shot, generated or automated, and read a representative panel before the replies.

### A working paper says "ChatGPT's ads don't pay," and OpenAI swaps bid caps for targets on November 9

On Monday, November 2, Christian Schulze of Frankfurt School opened a LinkedIn post with "ChatGPT's ads don't pay, and fixing them could cost ChatGPT its users." His working paper with Maximilian Kaiser (October, under review) uses Grips Intelligence data on 1,898 online shops, with the ad-free EU as the control; Kaiser advises Grips, as the paper discloses. In US traffic, "a paid ChatGPT visit generates $0.89 in revenue per session against $2.45 for a paid Google Search visit, a 64% shortfall." To return as much per dollar as a search click, a ChatGPT click would have to cost $0.26; "the largest publicly known account" paid $1.72, and OpenAI recommends starting bids of $3 to $5. Of 87 US advertisers buying both channels, 73 recorded no paid ChatGPT sale, and one advertiser accounts for 66% of paid ChatGPT purchases. Organic ChatGPT referrals fell an estimated 6 to 8% with ads on, a result that isn't statistically settled (p = .103).

That night OpenAI's Adam Fry told a press dinner that ChatGPT's "volume of search-like queries is now roughly one-fifth the volume of queries of Google" (Big Technology), counting every prompt that triggers a web search, and, per The Verge, that "most people do not seem to mind ads in the product." Ahrefs' tracker puts ChatGPT at 0.36% of referral traffic (via SEJ). By Friday, OpenAI had emailed advertisers (via SER) that new campaigns get Target CPA and Target CPC from November 9 in place of Max Bid and Max CPC, which retire January 1, 2027. Its help page says, "While Max CPC caps individual bids, Target CPC lets our system bid above or below your target in individual auctions". We found no OpenAI reply to the paper.

**Comment window:** open now, with existing caps retiring January 1. Set the target from your own revenue per paid ChatGPT session against your search revenue per click (the paper's parity method), keep one Max CPC campaign as the comparison, and judge it on incrementality; as Olivia Kory commented on Schulze's post, "Last-click revenue isn't incremental revenue..."

*Editor's note, added at publication on November 9: OpenAI published a VistaPrint case study reporting "5.9x return on ad spend" and "~90% lower cost per attributed order" for one US conversion-optimized campaign (July 8–August 4), based on VistaPrint-reported, platform-attributed sales. [OpenAI](https://openai.com/ads/customers/vistaprint/)*

### Google has indexed more than 6 million of Facebook's AI-written "Answers" pages

On Wednesday, November 4, Saul Westbrook of Peripheral Creative wrote that "Meta had quietly indexed millions of AI-generated Facebook pages under the url facebook.com/fb-answers/, and they were appearing frequently on Google," which he first noticed in June. By his Semrush estimates, the directory drew 40,590,906 visits in October, up from 12,167,857 in August. He also found that "LLMs are blocked from crawling these pages," and bediscoverable reads Facebook's robots.txt as admitting Googlebot and Bingbot and blocking GPTBot, ClaudeBot and PerplexityBot.

On Thursday, Glenn Gabe posted on X that "there are 6M+ urls indexed there" and that the directory ranks "across surfaces (10-blue links, AIOs, and AI Mode)," calling it "another example of massive 'Scaled content abuse'" (embedded by SER; we didn't locate the posts). His figure counts indexed pages, and the traffic figures are Semrush estimates, not Meta's data. Scaled content abuse is one of Google's spam policies, but Google hasn't said whether these pages break it, and neither company has commented.

**Comment window:** open until Google or Meta responds. Search facebook.com/fb-answers/ for your brand and log what the pages say, since they can surface in AI Overviews and AI Mode.

## This Week in Marketing AI

### Midterm political ads set a record $11.87 billion, and most AI-made ads ran without a disclaimer

AdImpact's final tally, as The Current reported on Wednesday, November 4, was $11.87 billion, above 2024's $11.2 billion presidential-year total, with connected TV at about 22% ($2.6 billion); we didn't find AdImpact's own release. Adweek reported CTV ahead of cable for the first time in a midterm. On Magnite's call that evening, CEO Michael Barrett put political contribution ex-TAC at "over $40 million for 2026 versus the $15 million previously projected," which "is more than double the last presidential election and nearly 4x the last midterm cycle," and the release puts it "mostly in CTV."

Wesleyan Media Project's October 29 count, as USA Today reported it, found 289 AI-generated political ads with more than $187 million behind them, and only 27% carried disclaimers through mid-October. CNBC counts about 30 states with laws on AI in political ads and no federal law. YouTube's rule, as CNBC quotes it, requires disclosure "when an election ad contains synthetic or digitally altered people or events," and CNBC's examples include AI videos posted organically on YouTube and X, outside the ad rules, one "with a three-second disclosure flashing in the middle."

CNBC also counted 41 data-center ballot measures in 10 states. After the election, AI and semiconductor stocks "sold off sharply" on Thursday, November 5, as investors "reassessed the data center build-out" (CNBC); in a Wednesday note, Barclays' Dominique Toublan wrote that "AI pushback is bipartisan but local, making the buildout pricier rather than stalling it". On Thursday, OpenAI's "National Community Compact" said, "We'll pay the full cost of the electricity we use and the infrastructure needed to serve us," and Reuters reported, citing potential investors, that the AI backlash could threaten the timing of Anthropic's IPO.

### Meta paused its new business terms, and a São Paulo court barred it from using Brazilian journalism without authorization

We've followed the terms since our October 19 lead. On Thursday, November 5, a Meta Business Help Center notice, as quoted by Nieman Lab, said the updates to its Commercial Terms, Product Catalog Terms, Self-Serve Ad Terms and Ad Creative Generative AI Terms "are now paused and will not take effect on November 20, 2026," that Meta "will provide a further update when we have more to share," and that "In the meantime, our existing terms remain in effect." A spokesperson said Meta is "pausing the changes as we continue to engage with stakeholders and take our time to get this right." The notice names no stakeholders and no new date, and Meta took down its preview of the new terms (Nieman Lab). The November 20 deadline in our October 26 angle no longer applies.

On Tuesday, November 3, Judge Guilherme Madeira Dezem of São Paulo's 44th Civil Court granted the Brazilian media associations ANJ, ABERT and ANER an injunction barring Meta from using journalistic content without authorization, with a fine of R$1 million a day capped at R$100 million. The order is preliminary and was issued without hearing Meta. In AFP's translation, the judge wrote, "Imposing on media outlets the free and universal surrender of their copyrighted works as a toll for remaining connected to the channels ... constitutes a manifest abuse of rights". Norway's interim order (October 28) and the CMA's investigation (October 30) still stand.

### AI Mode added home-services booking, and Performance Max may hand brand queries back to Search

Google's Robby Stein announced on Wednesday, November 4, that AI Mode now has direct links for researching and booking home services through Angi, Thumbtack, Housecall Pro and Jobber, with ServiceTitan coming soon, in the US only. Jobber's help page says its customers pay no lead fees and must be on paid US subscriptions, and Housecall Pro's Roland Ligtenberg wrote, "One request, one Pro, no lead fee, no bidding war." Those are the partners' terms for their customers; Google's terms with the partners weren't disclosed. The links are organic partner integrations, not ads; homepros reports that contractors needn't be partners to appear, and Google hasn't said how providers are chosen or ranked.

Search Engine Roundtable reported Wednesday that an "AI SEO" query showed four sponsored results under an AI Mode answer, first seen in a screenshot Sachin Patel shared on November 2. Adriaan Dekker wrote on LinkedIn (quoted by SER), "This looks like a test." Google hasn't commented, so treat it as one sighting of a possible test.

Google's help page on how Performance Max interacts with other campaigns now says, as SER reproduces it, that "Performance Max campaigns may also filter out navigational or brand-specific search queries to prioritize serving them through dedicated Search campaigns." It tells advertisers to give brand Search campaigns "sufficient budget and high-quality, eligible ads" and to consider "adding Search Themes to the Performance Max campaign." PPC News Feed (credited to Hana Kobzová) spotted the paragraph Monday and SER reported it Tuesday. It wasn't in the archived page, carries no date, and doesn't define a brand query or say what happens in accounts without a brand Search campaign.

### Shopify said AI-attributed orders grew 3.3x, HubSpot said AI is changing how customers find it, and Snap's ad revenue grew 4%

### AppLovin's $2.18 billion quarter topped five ad tech rivals combined

On Wednesday, November 4, AppLovin reported record revenue of $2.18 billion (up 55%) and net income of $1.44 billion (up 72%, per CFO Matt Stumpf on the call), and it guided Q4 revenue to $2.365–2.395 billion. On Friday, Digiday's Ronan Shields tallied that AppLovin's quarter exceeded the combined revenue of Unity, Taboola, Teads, Criteo and Magnite, about $1.94 billion. Unity grew 32% to $622 million in its first profitable quarter since its IPO, Taboola's revenue fell 6.9%, and Teads fell 26% to $235.1 million, citing "continued weakness in open-internet advertising," with no guidance.

Criteo's revenue fell 11% to $416 million, with retail media down 23% on "a $20 million headwind from previously communicated scope changes with two specific Retail Media clients." Its release says Criteo became "OpenAI's first advertising technology partner in March 2026" and is "working to bring sponsored products into AI-powered conversational search with THG Ingenuity." Magnite's contribution ex-TAC rose 20% to $200.6 million (revenue rose 14%), with CTV up 39% and now 53% of the total. Asked about pressure on open-web referral and search traffic, Barrett said, "The numbers would be much stronger if it were more of a search generated traffic world as opposed to answer engines." The Trade Desk reports Monday, November 9, after the close.

### The New York Times and three Tribune papers sued Brave over its ad replacement and AI search API

On Monday, November 2, The New York Times (No. 26-cv-9549) and the Chicago Tribune, New York Daily News and Orlando Sentinel (No. 26-cv-9554) filed separate complaints against Brave in the Southern District of New York, both through Rothwell Figg. The Tribune papers' complaint says, "The Brave Browser also includes built-in ad-blocking features that remove paid advertisements from the Publishers' websites and show users Brave's own ads instead," and alleges that "Brave is the supplier, dealer, and distributor of stolen copyrighted content for its own users and many of the most prominent GenAI companies, including Anthropic, Perplexity, Mistral, xAI, and Cohere." It says Brave "reported 10 active ad campaigns underway in New York State" as of October 30. Ars Technica reports that the Times wants Brave to shut down its paywall blocker and end its deals with AI firms. These are allegations, and we didn't find a response from Brave.

## Common Threads

### The week's arguments turned on which unit gets counted

Carma counted social posts (77% negative) and System1 a representative panel (6% "felt real contempt"); Fry counted prompts that trigger a web search, and Ahrefs counts referral traffic. Clarity counts agent sessions, Semrush estimates visits, and Shopify's 3.3x counts orders it attributes to AI from a "still small" base, so label the unit before setting two figures side by side.

### Google will label AI-made ad assets itself, and brands and campaigns split on disclosure

Google says the AI labels it applies itself in six places "cannot be overwritten," while only 27% of AI-made political ads carried disclaimers through mid-October. Very disclosed AI-assisted production up front and drew no backlash we found, while Dior's "all hand-drawn" needed a producer's explanation three days later.

### Intermediaries moved closer to the purchase

AI Mode books home services through four partners, Amazon's text agent carries Sponsored Products in its replies, and Shopify saw its first shoppers delegating purchases to personal agents. Reddit is testing ads in its own search, Criteo is "working to bring sponsored products into AI-powered conversational search," and the Tribune papers allege that Brave shows "Brave's own ads" in place of publishers'.

### AI's power costs and its ad revenue were in the news the same week

Voters weighed 41 data-center measures, AI stocks sold off on Thursday, and OpenAI pledged to "pay the full cost of the electricity we use." On revenue, Schulze's post says OpenAI's projections assume "about $40 in ad revenue per free user by 2030" against "about $1" today, and Barrett said answer engines are weighing on Magnite's numbers.
