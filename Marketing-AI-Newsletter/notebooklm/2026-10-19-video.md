### ChatGPT's traffic drop was a missing tag, and the tags were back by Friday

On Tuesday evening, October 13, Pacific time, Cedric Huesler, who works on Adobe's brand-visibility products, posted Adobe telemetry on LinkedIn under the line "ChatGPT didn't stop sending visitors. It stopped telling you it sent them". Since Thursday, October 8, the day GPT-6 reached Free and Go users, inline links in ChatGPT answers had stopped carrying `utm_source=chatgpt.com`, and because they open with `noopener,noreferrer`, they passed no referrer either, so GA4 and Adobe Analytics filed the visits under Direct. He counted "ChatGPT: −31k visits a day. Direct: +26k a day on the same pages." from "4 days of data, sampled". On Wednesday, Graphite CEO Ethan Smith posted a fall of about 45% in ChatGPT-attributed GA4 sessions, 22–57% per site, and noted, "Sources panel and shopping links still carry the UTM. Inline links don't."

Huesler's follow-up on Thursday said OpenAI had confirmed "a known bug" to him on Wednesday morning, that the fix began rolling out at about 2am PT Thursday, and that by 9am tagged ChatGPT clicks were "back to roughly 95–100% of the same hour a week ago". Barry Schwartz updated Search Engine Roundtable's story the same day ("UTMs are returning to the ChatGPT links - I guess it was a bug"). Profound, which samples ChatGPT answers, dates the recovery later: 5–12% of answers carried a tagged link for about 27 hours from 19:00 UTC Wednesday, and about 99% did again from 06:30 UTC Friday. It adds that the fix "does not automatically recover source information that was missing from earlier visits." OpenAI has made no public statement, and its release notes don't mention the bug. Adobe, Graphite and Profound all sell AI-visibility products or services, so these are vendor figures.

**Comment window:** open through October reporting, since the dip sits inside every Q4 deck. Mark October 8–16 in the dashboards, report AI referrals as a floor, and check the tagged share before calling a trend in either direction.

### Meta's October 30 terms let it crawl business users' websites; a Friday rewrite cut "train" from the US text

Meta notified business users in early October that new Commercial Terms take effect on Friday, October 30, and on Thursday, October 15, Nieman Lab reported what the crawling clause says. Business users authorize Meta to "use automated software (such as web scrapers, bots, robots, spiders, crawlers, and user agents) and create test credentials" to crawl the domains and pages "associated with your products or services," and the next sentence read, "Meta may use any content from such domains, web pages, or URLs to provide, develop, improve, research, train, and maintain the integrity of" Meta's products. There's no opt-out; the notice says, "If you do not agree to the updated terms, please stop all access or use of our Products for business or commercial purposes." Cornell's James Grimmelmann told Nieman Lab the test credentials could technically let Meta "sign up as a fake user" to get past registration walls, and Meta declined to say whether they would be used to get past paywalls.

Jason Kint of Digital Content Next started a five-post X thread on Thursday evening, US time, and wrote on Bluesky, "So every biz. Read that again." On Friday, Meta's vice president of communications, Andy Stone, replied on X: "This is false. There's no change to how Meta uses web crawlers." The UK's News Media Association called the terms a "shameless landgrab attempt" the same day. That afternoon Meta removed "train" from the US-English text and limited the uses to "advertising and business products." On Saturday it said "it wasn't clear in the initial consolidation of terms that robots.txt preferences are respected." The crawl grant, the test credentials and the "associated with" scope stayed, and the clause still doesn't mention robots.txt. Matt Topic of Loevy & Loevy told Nieman Lab, "I think 'develop' is broad enough to include 'train.'" The UK-English previews still said "train" on Saturday (elsop) and the French ones on Sunday morning (MACCUS); Meta hasn't said which language edition governs whom.

Crawl-for-training wasn't new for advertisers. Nieman Lab describes the current terms as allowing crawling only under the Ad Creative Generative AI Terms, but SEJ's Matt Southern points out that the Self-Serve Ad Terms, dated August 22, 2025, already let Meta crawl an advertiser's domains after an ad order, for uses including "research and training." From October 30 the grant attaches to any business use, including a business that only manages a Facebook Page and never buys ads, and it reaches any site "associated with" the business's products. SEJ reads Meta's crawler documentation as listing Meta-ExternalAgent for training and indexing, Meta-WebIndexer for Meta AI search, and two fetchers, Meta-ExternalFetcher and FacebookExternalHit, that may bypass robots.txt. No regulator has acted; the European Publishers Council said it would petition the European Commission and EU telecoms regulators.

*Editor's note, added at publication on October 20: SEJ still found "train" in the UK-English preview of the Self-Serve Ad Terms on Monday, October 19; on Tuesday, Relevant Audience was served the revised wording, with no "train," on the UK-English previews of both the Commercial Terms and the Self-Serve Ad Terms. The crawl grant and the test credentials remain. Relevant Audience · SEJ, October 19*

### Google's Universal Cart went live in the US; Meta adds in-app product browsing to existing ads on October 28

On Thursday, October 15, Google said, "Today, Universal Cart is rolling out in the U.S. across Google Search and in the Gemini app." The cart holds products from multiple retailers and sends price-drop and back-in-stock alerts. Shoppers can check out with Google Pay inside the cart at many stores or transfer the items to the merchant's own cart. Google names "Nike, Sephora, Target, Ulta Beauty, Walmart, Wayfair, and many Shopify merchants," and says merchants "will remain the seller of record." YouTube and Gmail, which Google's I/O post in May said would come later, aren't part of the launch, per SEJ.

SEJ's Matt Southern, reading the merchant guide, notes, "This first release is one-way and covers cart creation and transfer only." The UCP Analytics dashboard tracks Buy-button clicks through purchases and cancellations, and the Merchant API (in alpha) adds cart-transfer metrics, but "None of these pages list a metric for products saved to a cart." For Shopify stores, where direct checkout in AI Mode is on by default and Google Analytics and custom pixels don't fire (covered here September 28), SEJ "could not determine if in-cart purchases are handled with the same checkout process as purchases completed through Direct Checkout."

Meta's notice to advertisers, circulating from about October 14, reads, "Starting October 28, 2026, we'll add product browsing to your existing ads." When people tap an ad, they "may see an experience on Facebook or Instagram with product info and personalized suggestions" before reaching the advertiser's site. Meta cites "3% more conversions" from its own experiment, run August 6–21 across about 117,700 advertisers. The notice says, "Don't want this? Opt out before October 28, 2026 below," and a per-ad toggle in Advantage+ Creative and Destination settings works after that date. Relevant Audience found no Meta help-center, changelog or newsroom page on the change, and the notice doesn't say whether new ads will default on. MediaPost's Laurie Sullivan notes that the feature puts an extra click between the ad and the advertiser's website.

### Google Ads added channel priorities and caps; Display Network logic in Search campaigns ends in February 2027

On Wednesday, October 14, Google announced, "Say goodbye to all-or-nothing channel targeting." Advertisers mark each channel High or Low priority, and "campaigns will fully fund high-priority channels (within their target when using Target CPA or Target ROAS) before allocating the remaining budget to lower-priority channels." Low-priority channels can take a daily percentage cap, applied "On a best-effort basis." Performance Max and Demand Gen treat Search, YouTube, Maps, Gmail and Discover as one setting, "Google channels." The help page says the controls arrive "progressively" from October 15, that budget allocation isn't supported in Demand Gen, that the settings can't be applied from a manager account, and that "Support for the Google Ads API and Google Ads Editor is coming soon." Ginny Marvin, Google's ads product liaison, posted the same day on LinkedIn, "Supported via Google Ads API at launch. Editor support coming soon." The two haven't been reconciled publicly.

Starting in February 2027, per the help page, Search campaigns with Display Expansion "will automatically migrate to channel prioritization and budget allocation," "the current spend allocation setup will go away," and advertisers "may see a shift in channel spending between Google Search, Search partners, and the Google Display Network." The Display Network will also stop serving on Search campaigns that bid on Target Impression Share. Google's post tells advertisers who use the Display Network to set it to "Low priority" with a 50% budget cap by February 2027 "to maintain your existing configuration." On *Ads Decoded* Ep. 7, product manager Tal Akabas said "None of your defaults will be changed with this launch," described today's setup as "a backend mechanism that is effectively doing channel prioritization in the backend," and said "the automated backend logic will be going away". He put the switchover in the "February, March timeframe next year" and added, "We're trying to get these controls out ahead of the holidays so you have them."

### DataDome says Muse shops without identifying itself; Amex wants intent recorded before agents buy

DataDome, which sells bot and agent management, published its Q3 AI Traffic Report on Thursday, October 15, from traffic on its own network. AI-agent requests grew 41.5% quarter on quarter, to nearly 25 billion from 17.7 billion, and Meta generated 12.4 billion of them (49.7%). Agentic-browser requests rose 4.7 times in September, to 58.2 million, and Meta's Muse, fully available only from September 22, accounted for 33% of the month's volume in nine days. "Muse does not identify itself as an AI agent," the report says. In a traffic sample whose size it doesn't give, 99.3% of Muse's requests came from cloud and CDN infrastructure, and 8.9% touched forms, login, payment, account-creation or cart endpoints. "Policies that trust residential or ISP-based traffic won't catch agents like this," said Jerome Segura, DataDome's VP of threat research. Amazon raised the same identification complaint when it blocked Muse on September 20.

American Express published an Intent Intelligence Framework on Tuesday, October 13, for recording a customer's purchase intent before an agent transacts. A Create Intent API captures the instructions and limits (merchant, maximum budget, product preferences), the customer authenticates and signs off, and an Intent ID and Proof of Intent Token then obtain a tokenized credential the agent uses at checkout in place of the card number. Hannah Bozian, VP of agentic partnerships and strategy, gave TechCrunch the example of a customer who asks for white Nike sneakers in size 9 and an agent that tries to buy Adidas, a purchase Amex could stop. Digital Commerce 360 reports no live integrations yet.

Deloitte's holiday survey, published Thursday, polled 4,000 US consumers August 26–September 10. On a base of 3,700 of them, "39% say gen AI is their primary tool for at least one holiday shopping task, but only 16% would trust it to complete a purchase with their permission," and by Deloitte's calculations, shoppers who use all three of search, social media and gen AI plan to spend $2,893, against $1,175 for those who use none.

### ChatGPT's fan-out searches favor brand sites, Ahrefs found, and its Reddit citations fell 88%

On Monday, October 12, Ahrefs, which sells SEO and AI-visibility tools, published a study of 66.6 million free, logged-out GPT-5.6 Luna responses from August and September and their 15 million fan-out queries. Of those fan-outs, 6.8% used the `site:` operator, and 75.9% of the most common `site:` searches pointed at brand sites, against 11.9% for retailers and 1.9% for Reddit and Facebook combined. "Official" appeared in 13.1% of English fan-outs in September, up from 10.1% in August, and "price" rose from 6.04% to 8.88%. In a separate test of 20,000 US prompts, 84.4% of factual questions cited at least one official source. Ahrefs' citation data shows that "ChatGPT's Reddit citations dropped 88% in six months," from about 1 in 5 answers in March to 1 in 43 by late September, and it concludes that "ChatGPT seems to be moving from sites that summarize information to the sources that produce it." The data predate GPT-6 reaching free users; on Wednesday, David Konitzny posted that "GPT-6 is becoming more critical of claims made by listicles and comparison pages."

Semrush, "an Adobe company" that owns Search Engine Land, released its first UK AI Visibility Index on Tuesday, covering January–July with fixed monthly sets of 2.5 million UK and 25 million US prompts across ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Mode, plus Google AI Overviews results. Fewer than half of the top 100 mentioned brands also appear among the top 100 cited domains. Tesco and Asda stayed among the top 100 mentioned brands on all four platforms every month, "Yet in July, Tesco ranked as ChatGPT's 25th most-cited UK domain, while Asda ranked 606th" (on Gemini, 29th and 60th).

Also on Tuesday, Bing Webmaster Tools added Topics Portfolio (beta) to its AI Performance report. It groups the grounding queries where a site appears into topic clusters, and a Top Opportunity Queries list shows strong queries where other sites appear and yours doesn't, with citation volumes as ranges. Bing asks users to "Treat the numbers as directional signals for planning, not as a precise ledger."
