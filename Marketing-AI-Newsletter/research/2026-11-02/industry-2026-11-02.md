# Industry track — week of October 26–November 1, 2026

## One robots.txt line for AI training: Google, Microsoft, OpenAI and Anthropic back it, and none honors it yet

**Timing note:** in window. Anthropic's K. Kelley updated the IETF individual draft on Friday Oct 30 and posted it to the IETF ai-control list at 15:39 UTC that day. Google's Search Central post (John Mueller), the Bing blog (Krishna Madhavan) and OpenAI's post ran the same day, as did SEJ, SEL and The Verge (which credits SER as earlier; SER's post URL not recorded). Every implementation date is after the window: Bing's temporary grounding preference Dec 15, 2026; Microsoft honors the new preferences from Feb 1, 2027; Google "in 2027"; OpenAI "in the coming months"; Anthropic has "already started" with no date.

- **The draft (IETF Datatracker; "draft-kelley-aipref-training-preference-signal"; last updated 2026-10-30; expires May 3, 2027):** an individual Internet-Draft, which the Datatracker notes is "not endorsed by the IETF and has no formal standing in the IETF standards process", with "Intended status: Standards Track". The token is `__usage_ai-training`, used as a robots.txt user-agent group. "However, wildcard groups (i.e., User-Agent: *) MUST NOT be interpreted as matching the policy token." "If no group contains the __usage_ai-training policy token (i.e., there is no User-Agent: __usage_ai-training line in the file), then no preference has been expressed." "Consumers MUST match policy tokens exactly, apart from case, as for any product token. (Note that some deployed parsers match more flexibly; the __ prefix makes accidental matches unlikely.)" Its example group: `User-Agent: __usage_ai-training` / `Disallow: /` / `Allow: /press-kit/`. Origins: [IETF Datatracker](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-kelley-aipref-training-preference-signal/)
- **Anthropic (Kelley's message to the ai-control list, Fri Oct 30, 15:39 UTC; The Verge calls him "a technical lead for standards at Anthropic"):** "I'm also excited to share that Anthropic has already started implementing support for this draft.  This complements our existing crawler controls rather than replacing them." "It'll take us some time to add support across our services and work through the inevitable complications and edge cases that I'm sure we'll discover." "As a result, a robots.txt file that doesn’t use this mechanism, as is true of every one published today, expresses no preference at all." On crawling: "(though the draft does ask crawlers not to fetch what they can’t use)". And: "We hope that others will join us and make this a single signal that is respected across the Internet." SEJ: "As of Oct. 30, there was no posting in Anthropic’s newsroom regarding the signal." Origins: [IETF mail archive](https://mailarchive.ietf.org/arch/msg/ai-control/mddjatOxSR0h6GmLRBWlc839c7w/) (the double space after "draft." is in the original) · [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-microsoft-openai-shared-ai-training-opt-out/595387/)
- **Google (Search Central blog, Fri Oct 30; John Mueller):** "When a website uses this policy token to opt out of training in its robots.txt file (and Google-Extended is not explicitly specified), Google plans to interpret that as if the publisher had disallowed Google-Extended." "Websites that specifically allow Google-Extended are eligible to appear in responses and receive clicks via grounding in Gemini Apps." "We aim to support this new control along with other AI providers in 2027". SEJ: "this control also applies to grounding within Gemini Apps". Google's crawler documentation says "early 2027" (seen via SEJ and PPC Land; docs URL not recorded). Origins: [Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/10/ai-training-control) · [SEL](https://searchengineland.com/google-also-gives-publishers-more-control-over-how-ai-uses-their-content-499971)
- **Microsoft (Bing blog, Fri Oct 30; Krishna Madhavan):** two separate preferences. AI Training can be set in robots.txt or at page level; AI Grounding only at page level (Allow, Disallow or DisallowExtended). "If you select `DisallowExtended` or `Disallow` for grounding, you will see reduced brand visibility in AI experiences." "Neither choice guarantees inclusion, traffic, or payment." Via SEL: "To maintain your chosen preferences, complete the steps above by March 1, 2027; after that date, NoArchive and NoCache will no longer be read as a preference for training or grounding," The dates: Dec 15, 2026 (a temporary grounding preference in Bing Webmaster Tools for sites using NoArchive or NoCache); Feb 1, 2027 (Microsoft starts honoring the preferences); Mar 1, 2027 (NoArchive and NoCache stop counting); Sept 30, 2027 (temporary preferences expire). Origins: [Bing blog](https://blogs.bing.com/search/2026/10/your-content-your-choice-clearer-preferences-for-training-and-grounding/) · [SEL](https://searchengineland.com/microsoft-bing-gives-publishers-more-control-over-how-ai-uses-their-content-499967)
- **OpenAI (Fri Oct 30):** "Today, we’re announcing that OpenAI will honor a common signal in robots.txt, the file that lets websites express their preferences about the use of their content for foundational generative AI training." "This voluntary effort makes it easier for publishers and website operators to communicate the same preference to multiple participating providers at once." "Previously, website operators would identify individual crawlers and maintain separate rules for each and every provider." "We aim to implement the signal in the coming months, and will share another update with details about our implementation when we do so. This new signal will not replace the controls we already provide through robots.txt." "We encourage other AI providers to honor the signal." SEJ: "Neither OpenAI’s blog post nor its crawler documentation (checked Oct. 30) states what would happen when a website has both a shared line rule and GPTBot rule that conflict with each other." crawlerpeek found that OpenAI's crawler page didn't mention the token on Oct 31 or Nov 1. Origins: [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/helping-websites-express-ai-training-preferences) · [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-microsoft-openai-shared-ai-training-opt-out/595387/) · [crawlerpeek](https://crawlerpeek.com/usage-ai-training-robots-txt)
- **Meta (via The Verge, Fri Oct 30): no commitment.** Andy Stone: “We believe creating a common signal that conveys such preferences to multiple providers at once is an important step,” and “And we look forward to working with other AI companies on the standards and their implementation across the industry.” The Verge: Stone "doesn’t say whether the company will honor it." Origins: [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1023763/openai-google-microsoft-ai-scraping-standard-opt-out)
- **Coverage and early checks:** The Verge (Fri Oct 30) · sxz.io found the token in none of the 61 robots.txt files it checked · PPC Land's write-up (discovery only). Origins: [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1023763/openai-google-microsoft-ai-scraping-standard-opt-out) · [sxz.io](https://sxz.io/openai-robots-txt-training-signal-individual-draft-partial-group-prefers-training/)
  - Conflict to hold: The Verge says the four companies "will restrict their AI crawlers from accessing a site if they encounter the shared token" and that Anthropic "has already adopted the signal". Both go further than the primaries: the token states a usage preference, and Kelley wrote that Anthropic "has already started implementing support". Quote the draft and Kelley.

**Claims to hold precisely:** no provider honors the token yet. Anthropic "has already started implementing support" with no date; OpenAI aims to implement "in the coming months"; Google says "in 2027" in its post and "early 2027" in its crawler docs; Microsoft starts Feb 1, 2027. Meta called a common signal "an important step" but hasn't said it will honor it. It's an individual Internet-Draft with no IETF standing, not a standard, and Kelley wrote it, so don't call it "Anthropic's standard". Wildcard groups don't match it, and a file without the token expresses no preference ("no preference has been expressed" in the draft; "expresses no preference at all" in Kelley's message). It states a usage preference; it isn't a crawl directive, though "the draft does ask crawlers not to fetch what they can’t use". On Google it becomes a Google-Extended disallow unless Google-Extended is named, and Google-Extended covers training and grounding in Gemini Apps together. Search's own AI features have a separate control in Search Console, the one Cyrus Shepard used (conversation item 2), so don't conflate the two. Microsoft splits training from grounding; Google doesn't. sxz.io's 61-file check is a small sample.

- **Pennsylvania's Artificial Intelligence Deceptive Advertising Act (Act No. 99 of 2026; signed Thu Oct 22; Kelley Drye's analysis Wed Oct 28):** straddle. The governor signed it in the previous window, which didn't cover it; it takes effect after 60 days, on Dec 21. The bill text: "This act shall be known and may be cited as the Artificial Intelligence Deceptive Advertising Act." "(a) Disclosure.--An advertiser may not knowingly, recklessly or willfully create and disseminate or cause to be created and disseminated an advertisement that uses synthetic content in a manner that creates a false consumer perception about the nature, origin or authenticity of the goods or services being advertised, without clear and conspicuous disclosure. A disclosure under this section must state that the advertisement contains content that was modified or generated using artificial intelligence." The disclosure must be "(1) Displayed in the first instance whenever the synthetic content is presented to the consumer. (2) Presented in the same medium as the synthetic content. (3) To the extent technically feasible, permanent or extraordinarily difficult to remove from the synthetic content." "Synthetic content" is "Information, including an image, video, audio clip or text, that has been significantly modified or generated by artificial intelligence." Exceptions cover content "evidently fictional based on the content or context of the depiction", "parody, satire, commentary or criticism", or "limited to text". Penalty: "A civil fine that does not exceed $3,000." The Attorney General or a district attorney enforces it. Prime sponsor: Sen. Nick Pisciottano (D). Votes: Senate 48-2 (June 10, when PN 1808 was amended on third consideration), House 163-40 (Oct 7). Kelley Drye: "The law takes effect December 21, 2026, so companies have less than two months to prepare." "There is no private right of action." "Based on a strict reading of the law, a disclosure shouldn’t be required if the use of AI doesn’t create a false consumer perception about the nature, origin, or authenticity of what is being sold." "One thing is for certain: we’re likely to see more of these laws." Pisciottano (via City & State PA, Oct 23): the law “sets a commonsense transparency standard so Pennsylvanians can make informed decisions about what they are buying, while still allowing businesses to take advantage of this technology.” Origins: [Pennsylvania General Assembly, SB 806](https://www.palegis.us/legislation/bills/2025/sb806) · [Bill text, PN 1808](https://www.palegis.us/legislation/bills/text/HTM/2025/0/SB0806/PN1808) · [Act 99 page](https://www.palegis.us/statutes/unconsolidated/law-information?ActNum=0099.&SessInd=0&SessYr=2026) · [Kelley Drye, Oct 28](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/pennsylvania-enacts-an-ai-ad-disclosure-law-what-companies-need-to-know-before-december-21) · [City & State PA](https://www.cityandstatepa.com/policy/2026/10/7-bills-signed-law-gov-josh-shapiro-october/416799/)
