## 1. "AI Mode does not drive traffic": Reddit blamed Google for falling referrals, lost about 30% in a day, and the feed asked whose traffic is next

**Timing note:** in window. Alphabet reported after the close on Wednesday Oct 28, and Philipp Schindler's "lower monetization" line came on that call (news lead). Steve Huffman's u/spez post "About that login screen" went up on Thursday Oct 29, hours before Reddit's call (Shacknews' write-up is stamped 2:46 PM; Dexerto's 20:38). Reddit reported after the close the same day; The Verge's write-up of the call ran at 10:19 PM UTC. Shares fell about 18% after hours (Reuters) and closed down about 30% on Friday Oct 30 (Decrypt). The X posts ran Friday Oct 30 (BuccoCapital Bloke 14:24 UTC, The Kobeissi Letter 15:31 UTC, Cyrus Shepard and Kevin Indig the same day). Google's spokesperson line to The Verge ran Friday at 9:56 PM UTC. Reddit's plan to close its public API and RSS (Sept 30) was covered in the 2026-10-05 edition; only the Oct 31 cutoff is new (platforms track).

**What happened:** On Wednesday, Alphabet reported Search & other revenue of $63.35B, up 12%, and Schindler said on the call that FX was "the single largest driver of Search deceleration relative to Q2" and that "the strong growth in AI Mode queries had an impact because of the lower monetization we were seeing in AI Mode" (SEL: "Google didn’t disclose how much less revenue AI Mode queries generate"). On Thursday, Huffman posted that Reddit will "evolve Reddit to being a primarily logged-in experience" (as quoted by Philstar Tech), because "it’s better for user experience, better for our ads business, is more in line with safety regulations, and more protective of Reddit data" (Dexerto), and because "the vast majority of unauthorized access to Reddit (basically the scraping and stealing of Reddit’s data) happens through our logged-out experiences" (Shacknews). Per Dexerto's and Philstar Tech's accounts, he wants the front page and posts reached from search or shared links to stay open. The 10-Q says tests start in "one or more international markets" this quarter, followed by "some form of logged-in restrictions globally, including in the United States." On the call that evening, Huffman's prepared remarks said October search referrals were down "by more than 1 million referrals on average per week" and that total DAUq "could fall in Q4 by a low double-digit percentage" if the decline continued. In Q&A he said "the AI Mode does not drive traffic, not for us and not to anybody as far as I know", and named a second factor: machine-translated content "being ranked lower". Q3 revenue still rose 53% to $895M (ads $847M, +54%), but US DAUq fell 2% to 50.7M, the first US decline since the IPO (Reuters), and US logged-out DAUq fell from 30.1M to 27.3M on the quarter (shareholder letter). COO Jen Wong said "over 80% of our monetization sits on the shoulders of logged-in and app users" and that Reddit is "pretty skeptical of GEO". The 10-Q adds that "substantially all" licensing contract value comes from two partners whose agreements "are approaching the end of their respective current terms". An analyst on the call named OpenAI and Google, and Huffman said "we're in active discussions." On Friday the stock fell about 30%, and X generalized it. BuccoCapital Bloke's thread (about 850,000 views on a mirror) called it "a knife fight for the remaining and dwindling volume of human traffic on the internet", The Kobeissi Letter said the call "will be referenced for years to come", and Cyrus Shepard summed up the day with "Reddit stock is down because of AI answers - Google stock is down because of AI answers - Your web traffic is down because of AI answers". Kevin Indig pushed back: "What if it really is fewer people searching?" We found no Google reply to Huffman. In a separate Verge story on Friday about whether AI Overviews appear less often, a spokesperson said "We haven’t reduced the triggering of AI Overviews".

## 2. Cyrus Shepard opted a site out of Google's AI answers, and its regular organic clicks fell too

**Timing note:** in window. Shepard's LinkedIn post decodes to 2026-10-28 22:40 UTC (Wednesday afternoon US time), and his first X version is dated Oct 28. SEL wrote it up Thursday Oct 29 (spelling him "Shepherd"). SER followed Friday Oct 30 at 7:51 am with his answers to Barry Schwartz's questions, and a second X post on Oct 30 added "up to a 20% drop in clicks". The test dates aren't disclosed. Google's Search generative AI opt-out control has been available worldwide since Aug 31 (per seofrancisco), so the test ran after that. Google's, Microsoft's and OpenAI's AI-training opt-out posts came Friday Oct 30 (news story 4).

**What happened:** Shepard used Google's Search generative AI opt-out controls to keep one site out of AI Overviews and AI Mode, then switched it back. That's SER's description; SEL says he didn't disclose the method. AI impressions went "immediately to 0". By subtracting known AI impressions from overall site clicks and impressions, he estimates AI impressions had a "~0.9% CTR" and made up "about 8% of total impressions". And "regular organic clicks ALSO DROPPED when AI features were disabled", which he calls a "Search SERP Halo Effect". Sitewide average position rose during the test, which he puts down to "a couple of new posts + brand search", but the URLs with the most AI visibility dropped and then rose when AI visibility came back. AI Overviews still showed on the results pages during the test; only his site was missing from them. Schwartz notes that this means the test doesn't compare results pages with and without AI Overviews. SEL: "He didn’t disclose the site’s traffic volume, test dates, size of the organic click decline, or opt-out method." On Friday, Shepard's X post put the effect at "up to a 20% drop in clicks". In SER's comments, Greg Sterling said Near Media's research finds AI Overviews create "a kind of brand lift or awareness". It inverts last week's argument (2026-10-26 item 1). Those studies measured AI as a referral channel and found it small; Shepard measured what leaving AI answers cost the rest of the page.

**Comment window:** open while sites decide how to use the shared training token (Google "in 2027", Microsoft from Feb 1, 2027). The take: Shepard turned off Search's own AI control and lost clicks. The new robots.txt token is a different switch, and on Google it's wider than its name. Google will read the token as a Google-Extended disallow when Google-Extended isn't named. Google says sites that allow Google-Extended "are eligible to appear in responses and receive clicks via grounding in Gemini Apps", and SEJ notes that Google-Extended covers training and grounding together. So a site that adds the token only to stop training also gives up grounding in Gemini Apps, unless it names Google-Extended, and naming it allows both. Microsoft splits training and grounding into separate preferences; Google doesn't. Decide training and Gemini visibility as one choice on Google and two on Bing, and test on one section before going sitewide (Shepard: "Always test your own data.").
