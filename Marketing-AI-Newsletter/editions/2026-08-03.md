# The Monday Signal — Week of August 3, 2026

> **Four earnings reports landed in 48 hours, and one pattern ran through all of them: every dollar of ad growth traveled through an automated buying product.** Meta grew ads 27% while its free cash flow fell 91%; Amazon's ads accelerated to 26% with sold-out sports inventory; Microsoft's search ads decelerated while its agent registry added 40 million entries in two months. Meanwhile the week's research showed AI slop grading as premium inventory, bots holding 57.5% of web traffic, and the EU's AI-labeling law switching on Sunday, right on schedule.

## The Conversation

### Zuckerberg says the AI bets are paying off; the market sold the stock anyway

Meta's July 29 call gave both sides their ammunition. Zuckerberg: "On a dollar basis, our ads business is reporting faster year-over-year revenue growth than any other company's reported ad business — so these AI investments are paying off." He's right about the growth; ads did $59.4 billion, up 27%. The other column: EPS missed by about 14%, operating margin fell to 31% after eight quarters in the 40s, capex hit $31.1 billion, and free cash flow collapsed to $784 million from $8.55 billion a year earlier. Shares dropped roughly 8–10% after hours, and MediaPost framed the investor question as "diversification or distraction."

The detail connecting this to our last edition: Adweek reported Meta ran its optimism-themed brand campaign (the Bowie one) precisely because it entered earnings week needing to justify the 2026 AI spend. The ad was investor relations wearing a brand-campaign costume.

**Comment window:** open all week; every AI-spending debate will quote this quarter. The lazy take picks a column. The better one explains why both are the same strategy: ad pricing power (+12% per ad) is what funds the capex.

### Musk says X stopped demoting links a year ago

In a public exchange with Paul Graham on July 30, Elon Musk said X stopped demoting posts containing links "more than a year ago." Marketers built an entire posting style around the assumed penalty: links in the first reply, screenshots instead of URLs, "link in bio" theater. If the claim holds, that workaround era ran a year longer than it needed to.

It's a platform owner's statement, not a measurement. Nobody outside X can verify when or whether the demotion ended, and reach anecdotes point both ways.

**Comment window:** open now, and cheap to make substantive: run links-in-post against your old pattern for two weeks and publish the numbers. First marketers with actual data own this conversation.

**Source:** [PPC Land weekly (July 30 item)](https://ppc.land/bots-overtake-humans/) · July 30, 2026

### 77 companies sign a letter against Anthropic

Nvidia, Google, Meta, and OpenAI were among 77 signatories on a July 28 letter accusing Anthropic of lobbying for an open-weights ban. Anthropic counters that its proposals cover chip exports and pre-release testing, not open weights. Two weeks ago the labs fought through ad campaigns; now it's coalition letters.

For a marketing team the relevance is positioning, not policy: the AI vendors your clients build on are publicly sorting themselves into camps (open vs. closed, safety vs. access), and "which lab's worldview does your stack encode" is turning into a real client question.

**Comment window:** a few days on the letter itself; durable as a "vendor politics are now brand politics" observation.

## This Week in Marketing AI

### Earnings sweep: the auction is now a conversation between models

Four reports in 48 hours. Meta (July 29): ads $59.36 billion, up 27%, on impressions up 14% and price per ad up 12%. US/Canada prices rose 20% on just 9% more impressions, so mature markets are monetizing through price. Advantage+ passed a $75 billion annual run-rate (company-stated; it's not a reported line item), and CFO Susan Li called the new Generative Recommender "a paradigm shift in how our ads system works": LLMs reason about ad content and user preferences together, with the GEM ranking model credited for 8.3% more clicks and 15.7% more conversions on Facebook. All Meta-measured.

Amazon (July 30): ads $19.8 billion, up 26%, the fastest rate it has disclosed. Its most interesting number is conversational: shoppers who click a sponsored prompt in Alexa+ convert 48% more often and spend 21% more (seller-reported), and 350 million customers used the shopping assistant over the trailing year. Microsoft (July 29): search ads grew just 10% and got guided to mid-single digits, while Agent 365 registered about 40 million agents in two months; Microsoft told the market which business it's in. Reddit (July 30): ads up 64% to $762 million, and the stock still fell 12.5% because US daily uniques grew 6% against a 100 million target. CEO Steve Huffman said "AI Overviews has yet to make a similar level of positive impact" and valued direct users at "multiples more" than search-referred ones.

The shared pattern: Advantage+, Ads Agent, AI Max, Reddit Max. Every growth dollar moved through an automated buyer.

### AI slop grades as premium inventory, and buyers pay extra for it

The Trustworthy Accountability Group, the ANA, and Fiducia published the first rigorous sizing of AI-generated junk in programmatic on July 28, and the finding inverts the verification stack. Slop accounts for 1.3–2.4% of open-web programmatic spend, and it beats clean supply on the signals buyers actually use: invalid-traffic rate of 0.05% (vs 0.32%), viewability of 77.2% (vs 74.9%). It graded as premium more than 70% of the time and cleared at a higher price: a TrueCPM of $7.08 against $6.15 for clean inventory.

The mechanism is mundane. Template sites render fast, serve measurable slots, and avoid bot traffic because their revenue depends on passing verification. They perform well against tests designed to catch fraud; nobody built a test for "did a human write this." The structural marker: 30% of slop domains are templated, versus 1.2% of clean supply. The 88% overlap with made-for-advertising inventory means existing MFA suppression catches most of it; the remaining 12% escapes every current framework. And the strongest commercial finding cuts the other way: known publishers showed effectively zero slop, which is the best sales argument human-produced media has had in a while.

**Source:** [PPC Land on the TAG/ANA analysis, via weekly synthesis](https://ppc.land/bots-overtake-humans/) · July 28, 2026

### Bots are 57.5% of the web, and 2.6% of AI crawls serve an actual person

IAB Australia published crawler guidance on July 28 built on a stat that reorganizes publisher thinking: automated requests reached 57.5% of web traffic by June (Cloudflare data), with most crawler activity now coming from AI systems rather than search engines. The composition matters more than the headline. Roughly 52% of AI crawler requests feed model training; only about 2.6% are real-time fetches triggered by a human asking a question. Almost none of it sends anyone back.

The guidance sorts every crawler into five categories with four verdicts (allow, conditional, license, block). Training crawlers get block-or-license; live agents get conditional access. It also flags that robots.txt is unreliable for user-triggered fetchers: Google's and OpenAI's generally ignore it, Anthropic's honors it. Two dates give this teeth: Cloudflare default-blocks training and agent crawlers on ad-bearing pages for new domains from September 15, and its July policy shift moved the model from charging AI firms per crawl to paying publishers per answer. Meanwhile Time went furthest: Digiday reported July 30 it now serves ads to AI agents, converting pages to agent-readable markdown via TollBit, with an open question a publishing exec put plainly: can a bot be influenced by an ad?

**Source:** [PPC Land weekly synthesis](https://ppc.land/bots-overtake-humans/) · July 28–30, 2026

### Article 50 went live Sunday, and Meta signed the code it refused last year

The EU AI Act's transparency obligations became applicable August 2: disclosure when people interact with AI systems, machine-readable marking of synthetic content, penalties up to 3% of global turnover. Meta signed the EU code of practice on AI content marking on July 28, a reversal of its 2025 refusal delivered by policy VP Markus Reinisch five days before penalties attach. Signing changes Meta's evidentiary position more than its engineering; the labeling plumbing was already going into the buying tools (Google's AI labels are now operational across Ads, Merchant Center, and Ads Editor, with the DV360 attestation field live since July 25).

The unresolved question survived the deadline: which party in the chain (platform, agency, advertiser) carries the visible labeling duty on any given ad. Brazil added the enforcement rationale the same week: a ban on paid deepfake ads in the 72 hours before elections, after its data authority documented a fraud ring that moved roughly R$20 million on deepfakes of Gisele Bündchen. An unlabeled synthetic spot now carries liability in Brussels, New York, and Brasília under three different definitions.

**Source:** [PPC Land weekly synthesis](https://ppc.land/bots-overtake-humans/) · July 28–August 2, 2026

### The click-to-agent ad arrives, and ChatGPT shows up in Google's auction insights

Two sightings this week sketch where paid AI surfaces go next. Search Engine Roundtable spotted a new ChatGPT Ads campaign type called Agent on July 31: clicking the ad starts a conversation with the advertiser's Business Agent instead of opening a website. Meta disclosed on its earnings call that over 1 million businesses already use its Business Agent weekly (the Brazilian rental chain Movida reported 44% more daily WhatsApp bookings with 85% of conversations resolved by the agent; a company case). An ad whose destination is another AI now exists at two companies at once, and it rethinks the click itself; the landing page becomes optional.

The second sighting is smaller and sharper. Consultant Jyll Saskin Gales found ChatGPT sitting inside a client's Google Ads auction insights, present since May on exact-match keywords in niche financial software. That's the first practitioner receipt that ChatGPT ads and Google ads compete in overlapping auctions for the same buyer. Check your own auction insights; you may already be bidding against a chatbot.

**Source:** [Paid Media Collective Issue 224](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/paid-media-news-week-31-yoann-ferrand-zujec) · [PPC Land weekly](https://ppc.land/bots-overtake-humans/) · July 28–31, 2026

### OpenAI cut GPT-5.6 prices up to 80%, repricing agent work again

OpenAI dropped GPT-5.6 Luna API token prices 80% and Terra's 20% on July 29; intelligence that was frontier-class a year ago now costs about six cents on the dollar per task and runs roughly nine times faster. A Fast mode for GPT-5.6 Sol offers 2.5x standard speed at twice the price. Two weeks ago the same move came from Google (Gemini Flash cuts, July 21). Agent workloads run in loops and are priced per token, so every automation your team shelved on cost grounds in the spring deserves a re-run of the math. This is also the quiet engine behind the week's agent-infrastructure news: the MCP protocol rewrite, TikTok's Agentic Hub, Amazon's Ads Agent expansion. Cheap loops make agents viable; viable agents need plumbing.

## Common Threads

### Every ad dollar routes through an automated buyer now

Advantage+ at $75 billion run-rate, Amazon's Ads Agent in 11 new countries, AI Max absorbing Google's legacy formats on a published calendar, Reddit Max growing 150% sequentially. The auction has become a negotiation between models, and the human's job is moving to inputs (feeds, creative, constraints) and verification.

### The quality stack graded fabrication as premium

Slop inventory beat clean supply on invalid traffic, viewability, and price. Metrics designed as fraud checks became adversarial signals; "high viewability on a templated long-tail domain" is now a warning, not a comfort. Meanwhile known publishers showed effectively zero slop; verified human context quietly became the scarce asset.

### The web's majority isn't human, and the toll booths are going up

57.5% automated traffic; 2.6% of AI crawls serving a person; Cloudflare flipping to pay-per-answer with default blocking from September 15; Time selling ads to agents. Publishers spent two years asking whether to block crawlers. This week the question became what to charge them.

### Disclosure arrived on schedule

Article 50 live, Meta's holdout ended, Google's label tooling operational, Brazil's 72-hour deepfake ban. The compliance layer we tracked in edition one is no longer pending; it's the operating environment.

### "Meta grew ads 27% and its free cash flow fell 91%. Same strategy, both true."

The AI-spending debate will run on this quarter for weeks, and most takes will pick one column. The grounded position: price per ad up 12% (20% in the US) is what pays for $31 billion of quarterly capex, and Advantage+ at a $75 billion run-rate is the product that pricing power runs through. Meta's earnings math only works if the AI ad stack keeps outperforming; that's the bet, stated in numbers.

Window: open while the earnings cycle runs.

### "Your verification stack thinks AI slop is premium inventory."

TrueCPM of $7.08 for fabricated supply against $6.15 for clean; premium grades 70% of the time; 0.05% invalid traffic because slop sites need to pass your checks to get paid. The actionable version: MFA suppression already catches 88% of it, so ask your verification vendor specifically about the other 12%, and note that known publishers showed effectively zero. Media buyers and publisher-side sellers can both post this one.

Window: a week or two, while the study circulates.

### "2.6% of AI crawls serve a human. Plan your content for the other 97%."

The machine audience stopped being a metaphor: bots are 57.5% of traffic, half of AI crawling feeds training, and Time now sells ads to agents. The interesting question for content teams isn't block-or-allow anymore; it's which crawler categories you license, what your llms.txt actually gets you (97% of them received zero AI requests), and whether your pages are legible to the agents that do fetch live.

Window: durable; September 15 (Cloudflare default blocking) gives it a news peg for weeks.

### "The click is becoming a conversation."

OpenAI is testing ads that open a Business Agent chat instead of a website; a million businesses run Meta's agent weekly; Alexa+ prompt-clickers convert 48% more (Amazon's own number). If the destination is an agent, the landing page, the form, and the funnel all get renegotiated. Early POV territory for anyone advising on conversion paths.

Window: fresh this week; the format is days old.

### "X stopped punishing links a year ago. Test it before you take Musk's word."

The claim came from the owner in a public exchange, which makes it a claim worth testing rather than adopting. Two weeks of links-in-post vs. your current pattern, publish the reach and click numbers, and you own the practical version of a conversation everyone else is having rhetorically.

Window: this week for the take; the test results are content in two more.
