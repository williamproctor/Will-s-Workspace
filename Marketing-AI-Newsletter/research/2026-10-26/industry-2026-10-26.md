# Industry track — week of October 19–25, 2026

## Meta's terms: pushed to Nov 20, text unchanged, AI clause intact

**Timing note:** straddle. Meta notified businesses of its revised Commercial Terms and Self-Serve Ad Terms in early October; the crawl-clause fight and the Friday "train" rewrite ran Oct 15–18 (the 2026-10-19 lead). In window: the AI Features liability clause reported Monday Oct 19 (CIO.com and Computerworld); the CMA's Will Hayter telling a parliamentary committee on Tuesday Oct 20 that he would meet Meta on Wednesday Oct 21 (reported in the Oct 23 coverage); the News/Media Alliance and WAN-IFRA letters on Thursday Oct 22; the delay on Friday Oct 23.

- **The delay (Meta Business Help Center, Fri Oct 23):** "From November 20, 2026, updated versions of the Meta Commercial Terms and the Self-Serve Ad Terms will take effect." The Product Catalog Terms fold into the Commercial Terms, and the Ad Creative Generative AI Terms into the Self-Serve Ad Terms. Origins: [Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2481113349080785)
- **Who broke it (Fri Oct 23):** Reuters (via Investing.com, stamped 09:37 AM) quoted a spokesperson moving the date "to 20 November to allow more time for us to explain to our partners and stakeholders what’s changing and what’s not," and The Verge credits Fast Company (Sarah Fielding) as first. Record both. Origins: [Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/meta-to-delay-new-commercial-terms-criticised-by-publishers-advertisers-4969745) · [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91628981/meta-quietly-delays-controversial-update-that-critics-say-will-allow-website-scraping) · [The Verge (Emma Roth, 2:47 PM UTC; spokesperson Daniel Roberts)](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1017450/meta-business-terms-delayed-ai-scraping)
- **What didn't change (Nieman Lab, Hanaa' Tameez, Oct 23, 2:03 p.m.):** Meta's spokesperson: "We are extending the effective date of our updated Commercial Terms and Self-Serve Ad Terms to November 20 to allow more time for us to explain to our partners and stakeholders what’s changing and what’s not — including how Meta uses web crawlers and the fact that content owners will still be able to use robots.txt to signal preferences," Nieman Lab: "As of October 23, Meta has made no other changes to the terms aside from the implementation date. No versions of the terms include language about respecting robots.txt preferences." Matt Topic: "Publishers don’t need more PR statements or explanations. They need Meta to keep its hands off their copyrighted material and respect publishers’ terms of service." James Grimmelmann: "I don’t think that repeating the same non-explanations for another three weeks will make much difference," and "But maybe they’ll use the time to come up with better explanations." Höppner (as named by Nieman Lab; confirm first name and affiliation there): "Meta’s conduct constitutes a prima facie infringement of European Union competition law, and a textbook case for imposing interim measures." Origins: [Nieman Lab, Oct 23](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/10/meta-delays-rollout-of-terms-that-would-allow-broad-scraping-of-news-sites-legal-experts-are-still-skeptical/)
- **Regulators (statements carried by Nieman Lab, Oct 23):** the CMA: "The CMA has been in urgent discussions with Meta this week on this important issue," and "Meta has now paused implementation until 20 November. We will continue to scrutinize these terms and engage with Meta and others as we consider our next steps." The European Commission: "The Commission welcomes Meta’s announcement that it has postponed the entry into force of its new commercial terms," and "The Commission is committed to ensuring that Meta’s practices comply in full with all applicable obligations under EU law, including Article 102 TFEU and the Digital Markets Act." Neither has opened a case. Origins: [Nieman Lab, Oct 23](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/10/meta-delays-rollout-of-terms-that-would-allow-broad-scraping-of-news-sites-legal-experts-are-still-skeptical/)
- **Publishers' letters (Thu Oct 22):** the News/Media Alliance wrote to Zuckerberg (Digiday: it represents more than 2,000 publishers; Danielle Coffey signed). Per PPC Land's account of the letter, it asked Meta to answer by Oct 26 on four conditions, quoted Grimmelmann's "still broad enough to drive a truck through", and put publishers' loss at nearly $2B a year, drawing on a 2024 Columbia paper's $1.9B estimate. WAN-IFRA's letter (Oct 22, with an addendum Oct 23) was signed by Heimgartner, Lanzaco and Ørskov and makes five asks; Ørskov on the delay: "This postponement is a welcome step, but it does not address our concerns."
