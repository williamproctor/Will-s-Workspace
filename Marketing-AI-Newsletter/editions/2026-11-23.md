## The Conversation

### Think with Google said content, not AI, made brands invisible in Search, and site owners pointed at AI Overviews

On Thursday, November 19, Google's Think with Google account posted on X, "AI didn't make your brand invisible in Search. Your content did." Search Engine Journal reports that the post pairs "Stop the 'Top 10' listicle" with "start leading with first-hand expertise," and another of its three moves for CMOs begins "Stop trusting proxy scores." In the linked October 14 article, Brendon Kraham, VP of Search & Commerce Global Ads Solutions, has a running store skip a generic "Top 10 Things to Consider" to "create a deep-dive video analyzing exactly why a specific customer's running shoes collapsed," and tells marketers to plan on "real business goals like leads or sales, not unproven proxy metrics." It's a marketing post recirculating an ads-side article, not a ranking change.

Lily Ray posted, "Wow did Google really just tell CMOs to stop with the top 10 listicles?" and Eli Schwartz rewrote the moves "without being sanitized by the PR team," with the proxy-score line reading "AEO VISIBILITY IS NOT REAL." (both via Search Engine Journal, which notes that "Neither the article nor the guide mentions self-promotional 'best' lists"). In replies collected by Search Engine Land, Daniel Foley Carter wrote that "AI overviews have been a nightmare for SEOs and digital marketers / site owners who took the time to create content to attract target customers/visitors," and the recipe site Inspired Taste wrote, "Google is lying with full Frankenstein recipes AIOs by using our brand name on mangled versions of our recipes." AEO Sherpa, whose author's agency sells AI-visibility work, cited Seeably's October study of 6,682 local businesses, in which lists and articles drew 52.7% of AI-answer citations.

**Comment window:** open now; the post links an evergreen CMO guide, so it'll keep circulating through Cyber Week. Kraham's running-store example answers one customer's problem, the material that sits in support tickets, returns notes and sales calls, so it comes from the people who talk to customers and can be measured his way, by "leads or sales."

### Malte Landwehr said two domains that publicized scaled AI content lost rankings as the core update hit from November 13

On Monday, November 16, Malte Landwehr, CPO and CMO of Peec AI, which sells AI-search visibility tracking, posted on X, "Two domains that publicly spoke about the scaled rollout of AI content in the past lost Google rankings in the November 2026 Core Update." He didn't name the domains. Glenn Gabe posted that morning that the two were "dropping pretty heavily so far and were scaling heavily via AI-generated content" (via Search Engine Roundtable), and on Tuesday he dated the update from Search Console data on LinkedIn: "I believe the update landed on Friday the 13th (Jason would be proud)... I'm seeing a lot of movement on 11/13 and beyond for sites impacted." SISTRIX agrees that "Changes are visible in UK and US data from the 13th November" and finds "no particular sector that has been affected more than others."

Google hasn't tied the update to AI content; SER quotes its launch line calling this "a regular update designed to better surface relevant, satisfying content for searchers from all types of sites." On Thursday, Lily Ray wrote in an essay that "content made for search engines at scale, with no human reviewing it, adding to it, or standing behind it, eventually becomes a liability," and on Friday Gabe expected the update to "complete by early next week" (via SER).

**Comment window:** open through the rollout's end (about November 24) and into Cyber Week. Group your pages by publish quarter and compare each cohort's Search Console clicks from November 13 with the two weeks before. If the newest cohort fell while older pages held, review how those pages were produced and checked; if everything moved together, SISTRIX's "no particular sector" reading fits better. Decide nothing before Google posts completion.

### ChatGPT answers started carrying Black Friday coupon codes with Copy buttons, and coupon sites may lose the click

On Thursday, November 19, Metehan Yeşilyurt posted on LinkedIn, "ChatGPT just turned Black Friday coupons into a widget." His hosting-coupon prompts returned a card for each brand with an end date, the code with a Copy button and a "Get deal" button, sourced through "a small 'Hostinger +5' pill at the top." He wrote, "This is bad news for coupon and affiliate sites. Their model is the click... If the code is already on the card with a Copy button, why would anyone click?" On his Substack he added, "In the Black Friday prompts I track, these deal cards went from about 3% to 16% of ChatGPT answers that show products, in just four weeks."

Glenn Gabe saw the cards only in ChatGPT's free version and posted that this "basically cuts out the coupon code websites completely," while "For retailers, it's providing codes directly from them, so that won't hurt them really." At SER, Barry Schwartz noted that "Google has been doing this in their ads for a decade now," and Google's Black Friday post on Monday said Universal Cart "shows you merchant promo codes and offers." Yeşilyurt isn't sure "if this is a test or a rollout" or where "Get deal" sends you, and we found no statement from OpenAI. Under his post, Kristof George noted that "several affiliate networks can credit a sale to whoever owns the coupon code even with no click or cookie."

**Comment window:** open through Cyber Monday (November 30), after which Yeşilyurt "will check again." A shopper who copies a code from a card and types your URL shows up in GA4 as direct or organic, so count redemptions: give the code on your official promo page (his advice for brands) a name used nowhere else and compare it with your affiliate and email codes, and if you're an affiliate, ask your network whether a code redeemed with no click still pays.

## This Week in Marketing AI

### Muse's shopping skill now says "Do not search Amazon," and Jassy wants "a fair exchange of value" from outside agents

Meta's Muse launched in Australia on Tuesday, November 17, with connectors from partners such as Kogan, MECCA and ShopBack "rolling out progressively." "With the festive season coming up, the timing couldn't be better," said Will Easton, Meta's managing director for Australia and New Zealand. On Wednesday, Loblaw became Muse's first Canadian retail connector ("Starting today, shoppers can plan meals, build their basket and check out with participating Loblaw banners, all in Muse"); Meta says Muse "checks with you before sensitive actions such as sending an email or making a purchase."

GeekWire reported Tuesday that Muse's shopping skill now tells it to "leave out Amazon unless the user asks for it" and "Do not search Amazon," a change Juozas Kaziukėnas spotted on Friday, November 13, and summed up as "Amazon blocked Muse, so now Muse blocked Amazon" (his account). At a Semafor event on Tuesday, Andy Jassy said that for outside agents, "it has to be a good customer experience, which it hasn't been to date, and it has to be a fair exchange of value" (via Business Insider); the same day, Amazon.com.au joined YouTube Shopping's new Australian affiliate program. Klarna introduced an assistant that "handles shopping, returns, bookings, and everyday admin" over WhatsApp and iMessage, "Currently live with a small group of early users," and on Wednesday Google's Demand Gen ads added "checkout links or Universal Commerce Protocol-enabled checkout."

On Thursday, Salesforce forecast that Cyber Week (November 24–30) "will drive a record $357 billion in global online sales, and $75.8 billion of that figure will be influenced by AI and agents," and that "Twenty percent of holiday shopping traffic will be nonhuman." On November 12 it said AI "is projected to drive" the same $75.8 billion; the new release makes the figure global but doesn't define "influenced" or say whether the nonhuman share counts bots and scrapers or only agents. Salesforce sells commerce software.

### Microsoft launched ads in Bing's AI Overviews with no separate setup and rebuilt its bidding models

On Thursday, November 19, Microsoft Advertising said "Ads in AI Overviews are now available in all markets except China," and "There's no separate campaign set-up required" for eligible campaigns, which include "Performance Max and AI Max." Last week we reported SER's sighting of unannounced ads at the top of Bing's AI Mode; this launch covers AI Overviews. Microsoft's case is that "Search is changing from a list of links to a faster path to an answer" and that "Microsoft data shows a 21% increase in purchases after a Copilot interaction," a figure its footnote takes from "global Edge users, December 21–31, 2024." The post says "AI content transparency controls are now generally available," and Search Engine Land reported Friday that AI-generated ad images now carry "Made with AI" labels; PPC Newsfeed reads Microsoft's help page as applying watermarks only in the EU and New York State. Microsoft's new Discover feed "shows 35% fewer ads per page."

A day earlier, Microsoft announced "its biggest upgrade to bidding models in a decade," explaining, "We rebuilt that prediction model with new user signals and a better way of valuing each conversion." Bids now adjust "nearly twice as quickly," and in August tests advertisers saw "10% more conversion value per click at a similar ROAS." The 8% lift it reports for moving from tCPA to tROAS (August–September) and the 18% for moving from eCPC to Max Conversions with a CPA target (June–July) compare strategy switches, so they don't isolate the new model. Microsoft says, "There's more to come in 2027."

### Anthropic priced its IPO at $83, closed its first day at $97.17, and described no ad product in its prospectus

Anthropic priced 980 million shares at $83 on Wednesday, November 18, above the $70–$78 range it set for 825 million shares when its roadshow opened on November 10, and they began trading on Nasdaq as ANT on Thursday. Its pricing filing puts the offering at $81.34 billion before the underwriters' option on 147 million more shares, alongside a concurrent $26.0 billion private placement. The stock opened at $111.50 and closed at $97.17, up 17%, a "fully diluted market cap of roughly $1.8 trillion" by CNBC's measure, and "More than 846 million shares changed hands."

The prospectus says, "Enterprise customers embed Claude in business-critical workflows, driving stronger retention and higher monetization than consumer AI." Anthropic plans to "invest in brand and marketing," and its 2025 marketing and advertising expense rose $170 million on "higher media and public relations spend to support product launches." Its consumer plans are Claude Pro, Max and Ultra, and as we read the prospectus, it describes no advertising product, while OpenAI sells ads in ChatGPT. We covered the S-1 in our November 2 edition.

APAC grew 10.3%, while the Americas fell 6.5%, EMEA 7.1% and Japan 2.4%, and organic growth over nine months was down 1.0%. ## Common Threads

### The checkout moved into the answer

Muse checks out Loblaw baskets inside the chat, Demand Gen ads can end in "Universal Commerce Protocol-enabled checkout," and ChatGPT's deal cards carry the code and a "Get deal" button. Bing sells ads inside AI Overviews, AI Mode's hotel booking put Expedia's "Continue on Google" over the hotel's own site, and Salesforce projects that "Twenty percent of holiday shopping traffic will be nonhuman."

### Access turned into terms

Jassy set "a fair exchange of value" as a condition for opening Amazon to outside agents, and Muse now says "Do not search Amazon." Meta expects talks on its paused terms in "early 2027," OpenAI rewrote its Business Hub terms after Digiday's questions, and from December 15 LinkedIn rejects EU-targeted campaigns without a political-intent field.

### The content arguments turned on human review

Landwehr's two domains had spoken publicly about scaling AI content, and Ray drew her line at content "with no human reviewing it, adding to it, or standing behind it." Think with Google asked CMOs for "first-hand expertise," and Coca-Cola, whose US ad is live action, says human storytellers "remain at the center of the creative process."

### Impressions and outcomes came apart

Clark's site left AI Overviews and AI Mode and kept its clicks roughly level, and Kraham told marketers to plan on "leads or sales, not unproven proxy metrics."
