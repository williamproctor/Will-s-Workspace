## Who pays to read the web: a dismissal, a pilot's payouts, a closed API, an agent paywall

- **Chegg v. Google and Penske Media v. Google dismissed (Wed Sept 30):** Judge Amit P. Mehta (D.D.C.) granted Google's motions to dismiss in one Memorandum Opinion covering both cases (No. 25-cv-00543 and No. 25-cv-03192): "A final, appealable order accompanies this Memorandum Opinion. Dated: September 30, 2026." The reciprocal-dealing theory needed an agreement trading content for traffic: "Plaintiffs have pleaded only that they have an "expectation" that Google will send them search traffic if they make their content available for free." Then, after a record citation: "But an expectation is not an agreement. It is simply how a general search engine works." On price: "But Plaintiffs do not claim that this term arose from a meeting of the minds with Google, as opposed to their voluntarily acceding to cost-free crawling. The latter circumstance is the more plausible." The theory "would mean that Google contracts with every website that it indexes for Google Search. Google therefore would have agreements with billions of web content publishers." The claims "fail to get out of the starting gate." On Penske's tying claim: "the more plausible inference is that Google Search and AI Overviews have the same use case: providing answers to users' search queries." The court declined supplemental jurisdiction over the unjust-enrichment counts. And: "The court does not treat Plaintiffs' alleged harms lightly. Nor is it unsympathetic to the situation publishers now find themselves in, and the knock-on consequences to journalists, educators, and other online creators whose content Google takes and repurposes without compensation." It points elsewhere, quoting a 2009 decision: "that is an issue for Congress [or regulators] to consider." Penske's complaint, per Press Gazette: organic affiliate revenue fell "more than a third from its peak to the end of 2024." Not a copyright case; neither company sued for infringement (The Ruling Desk, Oct 2). Origins: [Memorandum Opinion (CourtListener)](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.277735/gov.uscourts.dcd.277735.27.0.pdf) · [Chegg order](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.277735/gov.uscourts.dcd.277735.28.0.pdf) · [Chegg docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/69668109/chegg-inc-v-google-llc/) · [govinfo copy](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-dcd-1_25-cv-00543/pdf/USCOURTS-dcd-1_25-cv-00543-0.pdf) · [Press Gazette, Oct 1](https://pressgazette.co.uk/news/penske-ai-overviews-lawsuit-dismissed-because-no-formal-bargain-struck-with-google/) (Charlotte Tobitt) · [Reuters, Oct 1](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/google-wins-dismissal-chegg-penske-media-lawsuits-over-ai-overviews-2026-10-01/) · [Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/reading/judge-publishers-voluntarily-acceded-to-cost-free-crawling-and-cant-sue-google-over-ai-related-revenue-losses/)
