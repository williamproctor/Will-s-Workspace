The guardrails conversation happening now will shape what AV teams can safely deploy in 6-12 months.

### Thread 4: The Shift from "Bigger Models" to "Smarter Inference"
- **Summary:** Multiple sources pointed to a paradigm shift: the AI race is no longer about who builds the biggest model, but who runs inference most efficiently. NVIDIA's Dynamo inference engine, AMD's self-improving GPU kernels, Sebastian Raschka's "reasoning-first" thesis, and the "deep thinking tokens" discussed on Last Week in AI all point the same direction — smaller, smarter models that reason through problems rather than memorize solutions.
- **AV Impact:** Emerging — This shift means AI-powered AV tools will get faster and cheaper over time. Local/edge deployment becomes viable for real-time tasks like live captioning, audio enhancement, and on-set video analysis. The "20-30% speedup from self-written GPU kernels" (Eye on AI) is a preview of what's coming.
- **Sources:**
  - Podcast: Latent Space — "NVIDIA Dynamo" and Speed of Light framework (Mar 10)
  - Podcast: Eye on AI — Sharon Zhou on self-improving GPU kernels (Feb 27)
  - Podcast: TWIML AI — Sebastian Raschka on reasoning-first architectures (Feb 26)

## Novel Ideas

### Idea 1: ElevenLabs Flows as a "Node-Based AV Production OS"
- **Source:** X: @ElevenLabsio — Flows launch (https://x.com/ElevenLabsio, Mar 11)
- **What's new:** ElevenLabs has quietly become more than a voice AI company. Flows is a node-based canvas that connects 35+ visual models with TTS, voice cloning, lip-sync, music generation, and sound effects. This is the first serious attempt at a unified AI production interface — think Unreal Engine's blueprint system but for AI media production.
- **AV Implication:** This could be the first tool that AV teams evaluate as a full production environment rather than a point solution. If Flows delivers on its promise, it replaces the "use 6 different AI tools and manually stitch them together" workflow.

### Idea 2: The "Speed of Light" Framework for AV AI Benchmarking
- **Source:** Podcast: Latent Space — "NVIDIA Dynamo & Brev" (Mar 10, swyx paraphrasing Dynamo team)
- **What's new:** NVIDIA's "Speed of Light" concept measures how close your AI workload is to the theoretical maximum throughput of your hardware. Most teams are running at 20-30% efficiency. This isn't a new concept in compute, but applying it specifically to AI inference workloads is — and it gives AV teams a concrete way to benchmark whether their AI pipelines are actually well-optimized.
- **AV Implication:** AV teams running AI models locally (for rendering, voice processing, real-time captioning) should be asking: "How close to Speed of Light are we?" If the answer is 20-30%, there's significant performance headroom without buying more hardware.

### Idea 3: Rick Beato's "Democratization vs. Devaluation" Framework for Creative AI
- **Source:** Podcast: Lex Fridman #492 — Rick Beato (Mar 1)
- **What's new:** Beato framed the creative AI tension not as "AI vs. humans" but as "democratization vs. devaluation." AI stem separation genuinely improved his workflow. But AI music generation tools like Suno and Udio could devalue the entire profession. The distinction: AI tools that enhance human creativity (stem separation) are net positive; AI tools that replace human creativity (full song generation) risk devaluing the craft.
- **AV Implication:** This framework applies directly to video too. AI tools that help AV pros work faster (auto-captioning, color grading assist, noise reduction) enhance value. AI tools that generate complete videos from text prompts could devalue the profession. AV teams should adopt the first category aggressively and monitor the second category carefully.
