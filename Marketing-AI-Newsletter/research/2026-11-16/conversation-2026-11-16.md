## 1. "A sort of Search Console for ChatGPT" or "a business profile with a report attached": OpenAI's Business Hub went live with mention and click counts, and the first sites to check it found 1 click against 1,020 sessions

**Timing note:** in window, with a straddle. A screenshot of a "Claim your business in ChatGPT" page surfaced Fri Nov 6 (Juozas Kaziukėnas, per Gehani; SER the same day, in its Nov 9 recap), before the window. Tarun Gehani's post on the leak decodes to Mon Nov 9 13:01 UTC. OpenAI launched "Businesses in ChatGPT" in beta on Tue Nov 10 (OpenAI; Reuters; SER at 1:45 pm; SEL). Lily Ray's post decodes to Nov 10 19:02 UTC, Kevin Indig's to 19:19 UTC and Claudia de Coux's to 21:06 UTC; The State of Brand ran its Ramp data piece the same day. Profound's blog post is dated Wed Nov 11, NoGood's comparison Thu Nov 12 and Previsible's Fri Nov 13. SEJ published its own Hub numbers in its Sat Nov 14 SEO Pulse.

**What happened:** OpenAI opened a profile-and-reporting product for businesses: "We're introducing Businesses in ChatGPT in beta, giving businesses a new way to connect with the 1.2 billion people who use ChatGPT each week." A verified business sees Insights for the past 28 days: "the number of mentions and website clicks a business receives" and "discovery topics—common themes across conversations where their website is cited". The help page sets the unit: "For an online business, a mention counts when ChatGPT references the business's website. The brand name alone doesn't count..." Conversations stay private ("We don't share users' conversations or Business Agent chats with businesses"), metrics appear only above "certain thresholds", and a blank metric "doesn't necessarily mean zero activity." Eligible US ecommerce and online service businesses can also build a Business Agent (Shopify merchants through Shopify Catalog), and "Businesses can also sponsor their Business Agent and use it as the destination for ChatGPT Ads". The feed's first reaction was about the click column. Barry Schwartz (SEL): "It is amazing OpenAI is giving us click data, a metric Google and Microsoft refuse to offer in its AI reporting tools." Ray: "And it includes CLICK DATA." Indig: "we're finally getting 1st party data from ChatGPT!", "a sort of Search Console for ChatGPT".
- **Then the first numbers.** NoGood's Mostafa ElBermawy: "ChatGPT's Business Hub says it sent us 1 click. Google Analytics counted 1,020 sessions." Its Hub showed 65 mentions, 1 profile view and 1 click; GA4 showed 1,020 ChatGPT sessions; its logs showed 3,021 ChatGPT-User fetches, so "those numbers differ by 46x to 1,000x." His conclusion: "That's why I think of this product as a business profile and not a search console." Previsible's Jordan Koene found "1,834 mentions and 12 website clicks" against 32 GA4 sessions: "I guess we can confirm without any doubt that clicks are not coming from ChatGPT." SEJ's Vahan Petrosyan reported 353,356 mentions and 2,663 website clicks for SEJ from Oct 14 to Nov 10, and SEJ warned that "A recorded click isn't a confirmed visit".
- **The unit argument.** Indig: "the brand name alone appearing in an answer is not reported." In his comments, Tim Lane: "the more famous you are, the more Business Hub might undercount you." Profound (a GrowthX competitor) says brand names appear without a site reference "nearly 50% of the time" in its data, and called the Hub "the first ground truth data an Answer Engine has given brands". The State of Brand, on Ramp data showing Profound used by about half of AEO tool buyers and Peec by about one in five: "a tracking dashboard by itself just got harder to sell." Takeshi Young: "Feel bad for all the prompt tracking companies out there though!" Gehani, before launch: "a mention count on its own is a vanity metric. Paired with prompt tracking, it becomes a check on the tools."

**Conflicts to hold:**
- Hub "website clicks" and GA4 "sessions" are different measures (Previsible says so itself). NoGood's two windows are offset by a day (Hub Oct 14–Nov 10, GA4 Oct 15–Nov 11). Print the ratios as "clicks counted by OpenAI against sessions counted by GA4", not as one system being wrong.
- The mention rule counts website references only. Profound's "nearly 50%" is a vendor's own data, and Profound sells the tracking the Hub competes with; NoGood's crawler and citation data come from Goodie AI, ElBermawy's company (disclosed). Report both neutrally.
- PPC Land's ~3.5% click-per-mention against "roughly 35%" in OpenAI's demo comes only from PPC Land; don't print it without the demo itself.
- "1.2 billion people who use ChatGPT each week" is OpenAI's figure. "A sort of Search Console" is Indig's framing; OpenAI doesn't use the phrase.
- Business Agents are beta and limited to eligible US ecommerce and online service businesses; claiming is open more widely ("Any eligible business located where ChatGPT is available", per SEJ's reading of the announcement). Jess Joyce was put on a waiting list.
- A Profound Community user's undated complaint ("clicks that don't match ChatGPT referred clicks in my web analytics") has no date; leave it out.

**Comment window:** open now; the 28-day Insights window means the first full like-for-like month lands in early December. The take not made in the posts we found: the Hub's two units are both OpenAI's, a "mention" is a website reference and a "click" isn't a visit, so the useful number is the ratio between the Hub and your own analytics, not either count. Pull Hub clicks and ChatGPT-referred GA4 sessions for the same 28 days and log the ratio monthly as the instrument's calibration. Lane's point cuts the other way for big brands: if ChatGPT names you without citing your site, the Hub won't count it, so check whether your own pages are the ones being cited. And note who else is in the product: the same page that reports your mentions sells a sponsored Business Agent as an ad destination.
