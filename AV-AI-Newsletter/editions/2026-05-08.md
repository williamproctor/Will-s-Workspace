## This Week in AI

### Cannes 2026: AGC Boards "Critterz" While the Festival Bans AI From Competition

Cannes opens Tuesday, May 12 with the AI question split exactly between the festival and its market. Three concrete data points landed this week.

**AGC Studios boarded "Critterz" for worldwide sales** on May 5, ahead of the Marché du Film. The animated family feature is the first mainstream commercial film to use generative AI throughout its production pipeline, with the package going to buyers at the Marché next week. The numbers:

- **Budget: ~$30M**, against an estimated $80–100M for a traditional animated feature of comparable scope.
- **Production timeline: ~9 months**, against the ~3-year cycle typical for animated features.
- **Direction**: Nik Kleverov, co-founder of AI production studio Native Foreign.
- **Producer**: Chad Nelson, OpenAI creative strategist, alongside Vertigo Films' Allan Niblo and James Richardson.
- **Screenplay**: James Lamont and Jon Foster (*Paddington in Peru*) plus Tom Butterworth (*Birthday Girl*).
- **Voice cast: entirely human.** AI is used in the production pipeline (image-generation models, GPT-5 for structure work) rather than for performance.

The film is a feature-length adaptation of a 2023 viral short of the same name, which was one of the first commercial projects to use OpenAI's creative tooling. The story follows an anxious woodland creature who teams up with a band of outcasts to find her lost brother.

**Cannes itself banned AI from Palme d'Or competition.** The festival's stated position: "AI imitates very well, but it will never feel deep emotions." Films using generative AI in their core creative output are not eligible for the main competition — though tooling for restoration, color, sound, and post is permitted under the existing rules.

**The Marché du Film expanded its AI for Talent Summit** to two mornings (May 15–16), up from a single day in 2025. The summit is invitation-only, targeting C-level executives, investors, senior technology professionals, and decision-makers from major studios, tech companies, and startups. The three themes this year: integrating AI into production workflows, ethical and responsible use of AI, and education and access. Last year's summit drew roughly 200 attendees; this year's expanded format suggests demand outpaced supply.

**Why the split is the story.** Cannes is positioning itself as both gatekeeper and growth platform — protecting the artistic legitimacy of the Palme d'Or competition while letting the market embrace whatever the buyers will pay for. The two positions are coexisting, deliberately, at the same address. For AV teams watching how the industry sorts the boundary between "AI as production infrastructure" (allowed) and "AI as creative authorship" (rejected), Cannes 2026 is the first major festival to draw the line publicly.

The next data point is the buyer reception. AGC plans to track "Critterz" sales velocity against comparable traditional animated packages of the past two years. If the buyer math works, the $30M ÷ 9-month production model becomes the precedent that the next 20 AI-assisted features will be measured against.

### OpenAI Ships GPT-5.5 Instant — A Significant Quality Jump in the ChatGPT Default

OpenAI released **GPT-5.5 Instant** on May 5, replacing GPT-5.3 Instant as the default model for every ChatGPT user — including the free tier. This is not a minor refresh. It's the model the majority of ChatGPT's user base will hit by default, and the quality deltas are substantial.

The headline numbers:

- **52.5% reduction in hallucinated claims** on high-stakes prompts in medicine, law, and finance.
- **37.3% reduction in inaccurate claims** on user-flagged factual errors.
- **AIME 2025: 81.2** (vs. 65.4 for GPT-5.3 Instant), a major math-reasoning step.
- **1M token context window**, 128K max output tokens.
- **Better web-search use, better image analysis, better personalization** based on prior conversation context.

**API pricing (gpt-5.5):** $5.00 per million input tokens, $30.00 per million output tokens, $0.50 cached input. Prompts above 272K input tokens are charged at 2× input and 1.5× output for the full session. GPT-5.3 Instant remains available to paid API users for three months.

**For AV teams**, the practical implication is the floor moved. Anyone using ChatGPT for shot-list scaffolding, rough script polish, transcription cleanup, metadata pass review, or release-note drafting is now getting the upgraded model whether they paid for it or not. The medicine/law/finance hallucination reduction is the headline benchmark, but the underlying model improvements carry through to creative-prompt accuracy too — especially when answering technical questions about codecs, color spaces, audio formats, and platform specs where prior versions were prone to confident-sounding errors.

### Grok 4.3 Lands With Strong Agentic Benchmarks at Roughly Half the Price of GPT-5.5

xAI publicly announced **Grok 4.3** on May 5–6, after a consumer beta on April 17 and quiet API availability since April 30. The release is interesting less for absolute capability — Grok 4.3 sits behind GPT-5.5, Claude Opus 4.7, and Gemini 3.1 Pro on the broad intelligence index — and more for what the model is positioned to do well, plus the pricing.

The benchmark profile:

- **Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index: 53** — above Claude Sonnet 4.6, behind GPT-5.5 (60), Claude Opus 4.7 (57), and Gemini 3.1 Pro (57).
- **#1 on Vals Case Law (v2): 79.3%** — the model now leads the legal benchmark.
- **#1 on Vals Corporate Finance (v2): 68.5%**.
- **Tied #1 on τ²-Bench Telecom: 98%** — the agentic customer-support evaluation.
- **+321 Elo points on GDPval-AA** real-world agentic tasks (1,179 → 1,500).
- **81% on IFBench** instruction-following.
- 1M-token context, video input, function calling, structured outputs, web search, code execution, MCP connections.

**API pricing**: $1.25 per million input tokens, $2.50 per million output. That's roughly 37.5% lower input and 58.3% lower output costs versus Grok 4.20, and approximately one-quarter to one-half the comparable GPT-5.5 / Claude Opus 4.7 token cost depending on direction. Cached input is roughly $0.31 per million.

**The positioning is the story.** Grok 4.3 is being marketed not as the smartest model on the market but as the one to use when you need agentic tool calling at scale and have to manage cost. The Vals Case Law and Corporate Finance leads suggest a deliberate domain bet — fewer benchmarks, deeper performance on the ones that map to high-volume enterprise use cases. For AV teams whose AI use is dominated by background batch tasks (metadata generation, transcription cleanup, rough draft work) rather than frontier creative work, the price-per-token math gets attention.

### Topaz Ships Hyperion 2 + a Native Adobe Premiere Panel

Topaz Labs' Next-Gen release on April 28 was last week's headline. The follow-up shipped May 7 with two pieces that matter more for working AV teams than the original launch did: **Hyperion 2**, a new SDR-to-HDR video upscaler, and **Topaz Labs for Premiere**, a UXP panel that drops Topaz cloud processing into the Premiere editing interface.

**Hyperion 2** uses inverse tone mapping (ITM) to convert standard dynamic range footage to HDR, generating additional color depth and expanding the dynamic range from SDR's 6–10 stops to HDR's 12–17.6 stops. The model converts BT.1886/Rec. 709 SDR sources to BT.2100/PQ HDR output, intelligently processing each region of the frame to increase color depth, contrast, and peak brightness. The pitch is that archival SDR libraries — broadcast tape, older streaming masters, dailies from pre-HDR camera systems — get a one-pass route to HDR delivery without manual frame-by-frame grading.

**Topaz Labs for Premiere** is the more immediate workflow change. The new UXP panel, available at `topazlabs.com/premiere-panel`, lets editors send media directly from a Premiere project timeline to Topaz's cloud processing for upscaling, denoising, and enhancement — and pulls the result back into the same project bin without an export-import roundtrip. The pricing model uses the same Topaz subscription tiers; cloud-processing minutes count against the standard credit pool.

CEO Eric Yang framed the Next-Gen + Hyperion 2 + Premiere panel sequence as "our largest fundamental improvement in model quality in years" — Topaz's first model architecture overhaul since 2018. That's the long-term context. The short-term context is that the Premiere integration moves Topaz from "the tool you switch to when you need a rescue pass" into "a tab inside your existing edit session."

### The Likeness Economy Moves: Twinnin Raises $3M, YouTube Detection Goes Live for Hollywood

Two stories landed this week that, taken together, define the shape of the AI-actor likeness economy in 2026: one selling licensed digital actor likenesses to studios and brands, the other helping studios and talent take down unlicensed deepfakes. They're being run by different companies for different audiences, but they're answering the same question — *who owns an actor's face when it can be re-rendered at scale, and what does that ownership cost?*

**Twinnin opened a $3M seed round** at a $25M post-money valuation. The platform lets actors post a digital likeness, which studios and brands can then license for use in films, TV, and advertising. Pricing: **$14.99/year for actors** to maintain a twin, **$499–$1,200/month for studios/brands** by tier. Backers include Google and NVIDIA. The platform reports **2,000 signed actors with weekly subscriber doubling** since the April launch, and $3M already pledged from an unnamed lead investor and angels including JP Morgan C-suite executives. A Series A is anticipated in 12–14 months if KPIs hold.

The opposition is loud. **UK actors' union Equity does not endorse the platform**, citing concerns about consent enforcement and pre-launch incidents — including reports that one agency promoted Twinnin to parents of minors before the public launch. SAG-AFTRA's position remains under review.

**YouTube expanded its AI-powered likeness detection tool** to the entire entertainment industry in late April, with continued rollouts this week. Built in partnership with **CAA, UTA, WME, and Untitled Management**, the tool works similarly to Content ID — it scans YouTube for AI-generated deepfakes of enrolled participants' faces and lets them request takedowns. Enrollment is open to celebrities, actors, musicians, creators, and athletes whether or not they have a YouTube channel; verification requires an ID and a selfie video. The tool is free.

The earlier rollout sequence is worth noting: creators in Fall 2025, politicians and journalists in March 2026, entertainment industry figures in April 2026, with continued expansion this month. Removal requests are evaluated against YouTube's privacy policy — protected uses (parody, satire, news) may not be removed.

**Why these two stories travel together.** Twinnin is the supply-side: the marketplace where actors can opt their faces into a system that monetizes synthetic use. YouTube's detection is the enforcement layer for what they did *not* opt into. Both are the legible end of the same question, and the gap between them — the unanswered middle — is where the next round of policy, contract language, and platform design is going to land. Equity's refusal to endorse Twinnin is the early warning that the supply side is running ahead of the union framework.

### ElevenLabs Ships Expressive Mode + Eleven v3 Conversational

ElevenLabs released **Expressive Mode for ElevenAgents** the week of May 4, anchored on a new TTS model called **Eleven v3 Conversational**. The release sits squarely on the agentic-AI thread that's been running through every edition this spring — voice agents are getting good enough that the limiting factor is no longer the speech synthesis quality but the *conversational presence* of the agent.

What's new:

- **Eleven v3 Conversational** — an emotionally intelligent TTS that maintains conversational context and produces speech reflecting intent, emotion, and emphasis. It listens to what the agent is saying and adjusts mid-utterance, rather than reading each line in isolation.
- **New turn-taking system** — uses real-time signals to determine when the agent should speak, pause, or wait, reducing the awkward overlap-or-step-on-each-other failure mode that has been the calling card of voice agents to date.
- **70+ language support** with native expressive control across all of them.

The May 4 and May 7 SDK updates rolled in adjacent infrastructure: conversation tags for organizing agent sessions, enhanced conversation list filters, expanded LLM model options (Claude Opus 4.7, GPT-5.4, GPT-5.5, Qwen3 added as agent backends), and increased MCP response timeouts.

**For AV teams**, the immediate use cases are the obvious ones — voice cloning for ADR pickup work, multilingual dubbing, voiceover drafts for in-progress edits — but the more interesting downstream is the agent integration. An ElevenAgent with Expressive Mode plus an Eleven v3 Conversational backend running on Claude Opus 4.7 with MCP tool access is the cleanest "talk to your post-production assistant" prototype currently shippable. Whether anyone in AV builds it that way this quarter is a separate question; the building blocks are now all in one place.

### Revopoint POP 4 Brings Native Gaussian Splatting to a Consumer 3D Scanner

Revopoint launched a **Kickstarter for the POP 4** the first week of May — a handheld 3D scanner with native Gaussian Splatting export built in. The pairing matters because it closes the loop on last week's lead story (the Insta360 + Splatica Project Eternal pipeline). One device captures a 360 video walk-through and converts it to a navigable splat in the cloud; the other captures a fixed-volume object or space at sub-millimeter precision and exports the splat directly from the device.

POP 4 specs worth flagging:

- **0.03mm single-frame accuracy** — the highest accuracy class for any consumer 3D scanner currently shipping.
- **Multi-mode capture**: full-field HD scanning, blue laser scanning, and infrared structured-light, with up to **105 fps in multi-line laser mode**.
- **Direct `.PLY` and `.SPLAT` export** — drops directly into Unreal, Unity, Blender, and game-engine viewers.
- **Color RGB capture** integrated into the splat output, rather than as a separate texture pass.

**The category context is the story.** Within a single week (Insta360 + Splatica) and the next (Revopoint POP 4), consumer hardware has moved from "Gaussian splatting is a research demo" to "you can buy two different devices that produce splats out of the box." Add to that the Khronos `KHR_gaussian_splatting` glTF extension reaching release-candidate status in February (full ratification expected this quarter) and NVIDIA's vkSplatting demo at GTC in March, and the standardization layer is shipping at the same moment as the consumer-capture layer.

For AVS teams running virtual production, previs, or location-scouting workflows, the implication is concrete: scene capture is no longer a specialty rig question. It's a procurement question.

## Common Threads

### The Cannes Split Is a Template

Cannes 2026 is the first major festival to draw a clean public line between AI as production infrastructure (allowed in the market, allowed in non-competition films) and AI as creative authorship (banned from competition). The clarity is unusual — most festivals to date have left their position vague. Whether other festivals — Venice, Toronto, Sundance — adopt or reject the same boundary in their fall and winter announcements is now the next industry-watching question. The argument the festival is making is also the argument every individual AV professional is being asked to make on their own work: where does the production tool stop and the authorship begin?

### Foundation Models Are Competing on Pricing-and-Speciality, Not Headline Capability

GPT-5.5 Instant ships as a quality upgrade to the default that everyone hits. Grok 4.3 ships as cheaper and agentic-first with a deliberate domain bet on legal and corporate finance. DeepSeek V4 ships as 86% cheaper than GPT-5.5 with comparable benchmarks. HappyHorse 1.0 ships as the #1 video model open-source. The pattern across the spring releases: nobody is winning the headline-capability race outright, and increasingly nobody is trying to. The competition has bifurcated — frontier capability for the highest-margin work, and aggressive specialty pricing for the long tail of background tasks. The practical consequence for AV teams is that the right model for any given workflow is increasingly *not* the model at the top of the leaderboard.

### Capture Is Migrating to Consumer Hardware Faster Than Anyone Predicted

Two consecutive weeks of consumer Gaussian-splatting capture launches — Insta360 + Splatica last week, Revopoint POP 4 this week — confirm the volumetric-capture migration that NAB 2026 hinted at six weeks ago. The standardization layer (`KHR_gaussian_splatting` glTF extension, NVIDIA's vkSplatting tooling) is shipping at the same time as the consumer-capture layer. The combined effect is that scene capture is becoming a commodity input format, the way 4K video capture became one between roughly 2014 and 2018. The downstream implication for production workflows is that the next two years of NLE updates, virtual-production stage software, and game-engine integrations will be focused on splat *consumption* and *editing*, not capture.

### The Likeness Economy Is Being Built in Public

Twinnin's $3M seed and YouTube's likeness-detection tool are the supply side and the enforcement side of the same problem launching three weeks apart. Both are operating ahead of the union framework. Equity's non-endorsement and SAG-AFTRA's pending position make the gap explicit. The question for AV teams isn't whether your organization will need a position on this — it's how quickly the position will need to be written down once a contract requires one.

## Novel Ideas Worth Watching

### "Critterz" as a Cost-Math Precedent

If "Critterz" sells well at the Marché next week, the $30M-and-9-months production model becomes the precedent every AI-assisted feature for the next 24 months gets benchmarked against. The interesting follow-on is whether mid-budget animated features (the $40–80M range that has been squeezed by streaming consolidation) is the actual unlock — not low-budget indie animation, where the cost math was already manageable, and not tentpole animation, where the creative-control question is more fraught.

### Specialty-Pricing Foundation Models Reaching the AV Stack

Grok 4.3's deliberate cost positioning is interesting less because of the price itself and more because it's the first frontier-tier model explicitly engineered to be a *background-task* model rather than a flagship. The thesis — "specialty-pricing for the long tail of agentic batch work" — is the same thesis that ElevenLabs and Topaz have been operating on quietly for two years. The next watch-item is whether Anthropic and OpenAI ship analogous discount-tier offerings (a Claude Haiku 4.7 or a GPT-5.5 Mini priced for batch work) in the next two quarters.

### Voice Agents With Backend Choice

The Eleven v3 Conversational + ElevenAgents + Claude Opus 4.7 / GPT-5.5 backend stack is the first cleanly-shippable "talk-to-your-assistant" prototype that doesn't require committing to a single provider's full stack. Voice from one vendor, intelligence from another, tool access via MCP from a third. This composability has been the agentic-AI promise for two years; the May 4 SDK update is the first time it's actually shipped without manual integration work.

### Splat-Native Editing as an Open Workflow Question

Now that consumer scanners and 360 cameras both produce Gaussian splats out of the box, the missing layer is the editing one. Photoshop's role in the still-image pipeline took roughly five years to crystallize. The splat-native equivalent doesn't exist yet — and the company that ships it (whether Adobe, Blackmagic, Niantic, an existing 3D tool like Blender, or a new entrant) is the one that defines volumetric-media production for the rest of the decade.

## Tip of the Week

### Build a Personal Context File This Weekend

The single highest-leverage move any AV professional can make right now to adopt AI tools — independent of which tools they use — is to write a one-page personal context file once, and reuse it everywhere.

**The problem it solves.** Every time you start a new ChatGPT session, open a new Claude project, or fire up a new agent, you re-explain who you are, what your team does, what your current projects are, who your stakeholders are, and how you communicate. That re-explanation is the friction. It's also the reason most people's AI output reads generic — they're starting from zero on every prompt, so the model is starting from the global average on every response.

**What goes in the file.** Five short sections, one page total:

1. **Identity** — your name, role, team, communication style, and 3–5 hard rules the AI should never break (e.g., *"never write in marketing voice," "always cite sources for technical claims," "default to concise; expand only on request"*).
2. **Context** — AVS team structure, current major projects, key stakeholders, recurring deliverables. Update when something changes; don't try to keep it complete.
3. **Tools you use** — Premiere, Resolve, Pro Tools, Frame.io, whichever color/sound stack — so AI knows which platform-specific advice is relevant when you ask a workflow question.
4. **Voice samples** — two or three short examples (a paragraph each) of how you write or speak. The model uses these to match your style without you having to describe it.
5. **What you typically ask AI to help with** — drafting, summarizing, troubleshooting, ideation. This biases the model toward your usual mode without you having to prompt it explicitly each time.

**Where to drop it.**

- **ChatGPT**: Settings → Personalization → Custom Instructions. Two text fields, paste the file split across them.
- **Claude**: Create a Project, paste the file as the project's "Custom Instructions." Every chat in that project inherits it.
- **Cursor / Codex / agentic coding tools**: name it `AGENTS.md` and put it at your repo root.
- **Anywhere else**: keep the markdown file in your notes app and paste the relevant section into the system prompt or first message.

**Why it compounds.** Every interaction you have with AI for the next year benefits from the file. You write it once. The cost is one weekend; the value is a lower-friction, higher-quality output across every tool you currently use and every tool you adopt next.

This is the user-side first move on the "Agent OS" framework we covered last week. The seven-layer architecture starts here. Do this part now; the rest of the layers (skills, memory, connections, automations) only matter if the foundation is in place.
