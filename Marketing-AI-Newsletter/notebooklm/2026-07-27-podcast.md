## Act One — Lead story

The most important story this week is **Alphabet Q2: Ads Inside AI Search Are Now a Disclosed Revenue Line**. Give it the most discussion time.

Alphabet reported second-quarter 2026 results on July 22: revenue of $119.8 billion, up 24%, with Google advertising up 14% to $81.6 billion. Search advertising grew 17% to $63.3 billion. The Google Network line — AdSense, AdMob, Ad Manager, the open-web share — fell 1% to $7.3 billion, its latest quarter of contraction while Search added $9.1 billion year over year.

The marketing-relevant shift is where that Search growth is coming from. AI Mode has passed one billion monthly active users since expanding globally in October 2025, and Google is now monetizing the conversational surface directly. Chief business officer Philipp Schindler walked investors through three formats: text ads with contextual sitelinks generated from the conversation, Direct Offers (the January pilot with Chewy, Gap, and L'Oréal, with IHG Hotels & Resorts coming next to surface deals during trip planning), and Highlighted Answers — "our latest experience placing clearly marked sponsored links inside list responses." Those formats debuted at Google Marketing Live on May 20 and appeared on an earnings call as monetization drivers nine weeks later.

AI Max, the campaign type that matches ads to conversational queries, is out of beta with more than 500,000 advertisers. Google says advertisers using AI Max or Performance Max see an average 15% more conversions or conversion value at similar ROAS — a company-reported figure worth holding loosely: independent tests published in November 2025 found AI Max produced a *higher* cost per conversion than broad, phrase, and exact match inside identical campaigns, and that discrepancy has not been publicly resolved. Dynamic Search Ads retire in September with automatic upgrades into AI Max, making the AI format default infrastructure rather than an opt-in.

One more data point belongs next to this story. An SE Ranking analysis covered by Search Engine Journal in July found AI Mode returned a text ad on 29% of tested commercial keywords — and for those keywords, the advertiser's domain appeared among the sources the AI answer actually cited only 11% of the time (the exact URL, 1.95%). The keyword set was preselected to trigger ads and SE Ranking sells AI-visibility tracking, so treat the percentages as directional. The direction, though, is clear: paid placement in AI search, organic citations, and classic rankings are behaving like three separate surfaces. Buying one does not buy the others.

## Act Two — Supporting stories

### August 2 Is a Hard Deadline: AI-Content Labeling Gets Real Fines

On July 20, the European Commission published its implementation guidelines for Article 50 of the EU AI Act (Communication C(2026) 5054 final) alongside a finalized Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content. Article 50 — the transparency obligation covering synthetic media — becomes applicable on **August 2, 2026**, with fines up to €15 million or 3% of total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. Google announced on July 24 that it is signing the Code.

Two details matter most for marketers. First, **the artistic carve-out does not cover advertising**: Commission guidance explicitly lists an AI-manipulated video using synthetic humans to sell a product as content that does not qualify as an artistic work. A synthetic spokesperson in an ad needs the full label. Second, assistive editing is exempt — retouching, color work, cropping, and edits that do not substantially alter the content's meaning stay outside the regime.

The EU deadline is one layer of a stack. New York's synthetic performer disclosure law has been in force since June 9 ($1,000 first violation, $5,000 after; audio ads exempt). India's intermediary rules have applied since February 20, using loss of platform safe-harbour rather than fines. California's SB 942/AB 853 were deliberately synced to the EU's August 2 date. A brand running one AI-assisted campaign across those markets now answers overlapping, non-identical disclosure tests in the same quarter.

Platform plumbing arrived in pieces all month: Google shipped an AI label setting across five ads products on July 9, added synthetic-content attestation structures to the Google Ads API on June 24 and to the Display & Video 360 API on July 23, and on July 22 revised its own ads policy so AI-disclosure labels would not violate the existing prohibition on text overlays in Search image assets. Every regime and every platform tool converges on the same allocation: the disclosure duty — and the liability — sits with the advertiser, not the platform. Google's own documentation states that using its label setting does not guarantee compliance with any regulation.

### ChatGPT Ads Grows Into a Performance Channel — With a Dark Auction Underneath

OpenAI rolled out a batch of ChatGPT Ads capabilities over the week that reads like a checklist of what performance marketers were waiting for: conversion-optimized (oCPC) campaigns via a new Conversions objective, geographic exclusions, AppsFlyer and Adjust integrations for app measurement, Automatic Advanced Matching using hashed customer data, an asynchronous bulk Ads API, and product feed cards showing prices and star ratings. Daily budgets also change to seven-day rolling averages with automatic intraday pacing, effective July 27. The platform has come a long way from its February pilot and its original $200,000 minimum commitments; the self-serve Ads Manager has had no minimum spend since May.

The demand side is arriving faster than the reporting side. Adthena now tracks 7,378 advertisers running in ChatGPT, with tracked placements growing 97-fold in a single quarter — a small base, but the point is that a surface that carried effectively no advertising a year ago now supports a competitive-intelligence index. Booking.com leads all three tracked markets, and the US accounts for 60.1% of observed advertisers.

What advertisers cannot see is the auction. ChatGPT's native Ads Manager reports only an advertiser's own CPC, impressions, and CTR — no impression share, no auction insights, no competitive view. UK advertisers have watched costs swing between 39% and 278% with no visible explanation; Adthena and dentsu launched a product this week to reconstruct that missing layer externally (three dentsu clients sit among the top nine UK ChatGPT advertisers, for disclosure). The unit of competition is also different: advertisers contest loose, conversational prompt categories rather than enumerable keywords. This auction sits roughly where paid search sat two decades ago — except spend is arriving at modern scale before the reporting exists.

### Similarweb: The Assistant Market Fragments, and Citations Don't Equal Traffic

Similarweb published its 2026 Generative AI Landscape report this week. Generative AI platforms drew 9.5 billion average monthly web visits over the trailing year, up 70%, with 655 million monthly unique visitors. The share story is the headline: ChatGPT held above 80% of tracked standalone gen-AI web visits through late 2024 and sits just above 50% as of May 2026, with Gemini, Claude, Perplexity, and DeepSeek absorbing the difference — June snapshots of the same panel put Gemini around 27% and Claude around 9%. Meanwhile 95% of ChatGPT's measured users also used Google properties — overlap, not exclusivity.

For anyone doing AI-visibility work, four findings stand out:

- **AI Overviews appeared in 43% of US searches** by Similarweb's measure — but Adthena's June pulse recorded 18% for the US. Two commercial trackers disagree by more than 2x and neither has published a reconciliation. There is no industry-standard measurement of AI Overview frequency.
- **ChatGPT's citation rate is climbing fast:** roughly 1.3% of answers carried web citations in June 2025; 6.8% by May 2026. Travel (22.6%) and retail (13.5%) lead. Reviews and user-generated content supply 28.9% of top US citations, news and publishers 26.0%.
- **Citations and referrals point at different pages.** About 65% of cited URLs sit two or three folders deep in a site, while 58.8% of AI referral traffic lands on homepages — a split that widened after a May 7 ChatGPT search update. The pages that earn the citation are usually not the pages that receive the click.
- **The traffic that does arrive converts.** ChatGPT referral traffic converted at 7.1% in Similarweb's clickstream panel — second only to paid search at 7.8%, ahead of organic, social, email, and display.

Advertising penetration reached 26% of US desktop ChatGPT chats in June (14% in May), with click-through at 0.50% and compressing as inventory scales. Which still means roughly three-quarters of even the most ad-saturated assistant surface carries no ads at all: most of what an assistant says about a brand remains earned — a function of what the model retrieves and cites, not what anyone bought. Similarweb's own disclaimer applies throughout: panel-based estimates, not audited measurements.

### WARC: AI Tripled the Output, Not the Judgment — and the Constraint Is the Brief

WARC's "The New Creative Advantage," produced with TikTok and LIONS Advisory (published July 14, picked up by trade press July 22), surveyed 400 marketers across the US, UK, Australia, and Brazil who are directly involved in creative production decisions. Ninety percent said AI is now part of the creative toolkit. **88% report higher creative volume since adoption; 45% report a significant quality improvement.** Meanwhile 87% rate their own organization's use of AI as effective — a self-assessment worth discounting, since respondents are grading output they commissioned and approved.

The study's sharpest finding is where it locates the problem: the brief. **67% of marketers use demographic data as the primary input when briefing a generative model, while 59% agree demographic segmentation no longer works** — the same people, running an input they've concluded is broken, at machine speed. Only 17% consistently incorporate community or audience insight beyond demographics. The named obstacles read as symptoms of that input problem: over-reliance on generic visual styles (40%), unpredictable quality (36%), output lacking originality (32%). TikTok's Andy Yang framed it in the foreword: the gap in AI-assisted creativity is "not a technology gap, it is an intelligence gap." (Disclosure: TikTok commissioned research whose conclusion — marketers need richer cultural signal — aligns with what TikTok sells. The finding stands on its data; the alignment belongs on the record.)

The mechanism is worth internalizing. Before generative tools, a thin demographic brief got diluted by the layers between brief and asset — strategists, creatives, argument. Compressed AI production removes those compensating layers, so the brief travels almost directly to the finished asset. The input quality now matters more than it ever did, at exactly the moment most teams left it unchanged.

## Closing segment two — Tip of the week

Give the practical closing tip enough room to be useful, with its concrete steps intact.

### Run a Monthly Three-Assistant Brand Audit

The visibility market fragmented (ChatGPT ~52%, Gemini ~27%, Claude ~9% of gen-AI web visits), and only 36 of 1,200+ tracked brands show up consistently across platforms. You cannot manage what you've never looked at. Thirty minutes a month covers it:

1. **Write five buying-intent prompts** a real prospect would ask in your category — not brand searches ("best X for Y-stage B2B companies," "how should a team like ours handle Z").
2. **Run all five in ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude** (fresh sessions, no memory). Fifteen answers total.
3. **Log three things per answer:** Is your brand mentioned? What is said about it (and is it accurate)? Which sources are cited?
4. **Build the citation list.** The domains that keep appearing are the retrieval layer for your category — those are targets for content placement, digital PR, reviews, and data the models can cite.
5. **Repeat monthly and diff.** Presence shifts month to month (source sets change, models update); the trend line is the insight. Fifteen minutes of prompting, fifteen of logging.

Why it works: citation sources are concentrated and discoverable (reviews/UGC 28.9% and news 26.0% of ChatGPT's top citations), assistant answers demonstrably steer visits (recommended brands drew 2–4x the follow-on traffic in Similarweb's brand-pair data), and a running log turns "how do we show up in AI?" from a vibe into a dataset you can act on — and show clients.
