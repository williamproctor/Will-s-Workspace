## This Week in AI

### Google Brings Veo 3.1 Into Flow With Audio Across More Workflows

Google updated Flow with **Veo 3.1**, adding richer audio, stronger prompt adherence, more realistic texture rendering, and broader audio support across existing video workflows.

The practical change is that audio is no longer limited to a narrow subset of generation modes. Google says Flow now brings audio to:

- **Ingredients to Video**
- **Frames to Video**
- **Extend**

That matters because these are the modes that move beyond a simple text prompt. Ingredients to Video works from reference materials. Frames to Video gives the model a starting or ending frame. Extend continues an existing clip. Adding audio to those modes makes the generated output more like a production object: image references, timing, movement, and sound can travel together.

Google also introduced more precise editing controls inside Flow. The new **Insert** capability adds an element to an existing scene while preserving the surrounding clip, and Google says object removal is coming. The pattern is familiar now: generated video is becoming less of a one-shot output and more of an editable sequence.

Veo 3.1 is also available beyond the consumer Flow interface. Google says the model and new capabilities are available through the **Gemini API** and **Vertex AI**, with Flow, Google Vids, the Gemini app, and API surfaces all part of the rollout. For AV teams, the important point is not only higher fidelity. It is that the same model family is showing up in both a creative interface and developer/enterprise workflows.

The caveat is still worth keeping in view. Google describes some of the audio features as experimental and actively improving. That is the state of the category: promising enough to matter, but still requiring review, editing, and separate quality control.

### Claude Sonnet 5 Makes Agentic Work Cheaper and More Available

Anthropic released **Claude Sonnet 5** on June 30, positioning it as the most agentic Sonnet model so far.

The headline is not that Sonnet 5 replaces the largest models. It is that it brings more of that capability into the lower-cost Sonnet tier. Anthropic says Sonnet 5 can make plans, use browsers and terminals, and run autonomously at a level that recently required larger and more expensive models.

The availability story is also important:

- Sonnet 5 is available across Claude plans.
- It is available in Claude Code.
- Developers can call it through the Claude API with the model ID `claude-sonnet-5`.
- Introductory API pricing is **$2 per million input tokens** and **$10 per million output tokens** through August 31, 2026.
- Standard pricing after that is **$3 input / $15 output per million tokens**.

On Anthropic's reported benchmarks, Sonnet 5 reaches **63.2% on SWE-bench Pro** and **80.4% on Terminal-Bench 2.1**. Those are software and command-line evaluations, not AV benchmarks, but they map to a kind of work that increasingly surrounds AV production: file organization, scripts, metadata cleanup, web publishing, SharePoint packaging, QA checklists, batch processing, and small internal tools.

The model story this week is therefore practical rather than dramatic. GPT-5.6 Sol remains the more aggressive frontier signal, but it is still in limited preview. Sonnet 5 is generally available. That makes it more relevant for actual workflow experimentation in the near term.

**Source:** [Anthropic — Introducing Claude Sonnet 5](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-sonnet-5) · [Anthropic — Claude Sonnet 5](https://www.anthropic.com/claude/sonnet?prog_id=5) · June 30, 2026

### Runway Expands Enterprise Media Deployment With Bertelsmann

Runway announced a creative partnership with **Bertelsmann** on July 1. Under the agreement, Runway's AI models will be integrated across Bertelsmann businesses including RTL Group, BMG, and Bertelsmann Marketing Services.

This is not a model release, but it is still a useful signal. The AI video market is moving in two directions at once:

- Consumer-facing tools keep competing on output quality, speed, audio, and control.
- Enterprise media companies are building operational agreements around the tools they intend to use at scale.

Runway says the Bertelsmann relationship began in 2024 through the Bertelsmann AI Hub and is now expanding into broader workflow development. The announcement points to use across creative workflows rather than a single narrow use case.

For AV production, the interesting part is the shape of deployment. Large media organizations are not only testing prompt-to-video clips. They are looking at where generative tools sit across ideation, visual development, music marketing materials, review cycles, and repeatable production workflows. That is a different kind of adoption than an individual user trying a model in a browser.

It also connects to a broader pattern from the last few weeks. Adobe is putting creative agents into established apps. Google is putting Veo into Flow and API workflows. Runway is deepening media partnerships. The category is becoming less about which single model makes the prettiest clip and more about which systems fit into the production chain.

**Source:** [Runway — Runway Announces Creative Partnership with Bertelsmann](https://runwayml.com/news/runway-announces-creative-partnership-with-bertelsmann) · July 1, 2026

### Adobe's Topaz Deal Puts Enhancement Back in the Center of AI Video

Late last week, Adobe announced a definitive agreement to acquire **Topaz Labs**, the company behind widely used AI image and video enhancement tools.

This story is slightly outside the clean July 3 window, but it is relevant because it fills in a different side of AI production. Generative video gets most of the attention, but finishing often depends on enhancement: sharpening, denoising, restoring, upscaling, stabilizing, and improving material that already exists.

Adobe says Topaz technology will be integrated into Firefly, Firefly Services, and Creative Cloud apps, while Topaz products will continue as standalone offerings. Adobe also highlighted Topaz's **Neurostream** technology, which is designed to run large video and image models locally on consumer hardware.

That local-processing angle is worth watching. If more enhancement can happen on-device instead of only in the cloud, the user experience changes: faster previews, lower latency, and more practical use on large files. It also fits the hybrid reality of AV work, where teams may combine camera footage, screen captures, generated elements, archival material, and rendered graphics in the same final piece.

## Common Threads

### AI Video Is Becoming Editable by Default

Veo 3.1 in Flow, Runway's enterprise partnerships, Seedance 2.5's region-level editing, and LTX's retake/extend-style workflows all point in the same direction. The next stage of AI video is not only better generation. It is better control after the first generation.

### Audio Is Moving Into the Video Model Itself

Last week, the pattern showed up in video-to-music and Seedance's audio-video architecture. This week, Google expanded audio support across Flow's reference and extension modes, while LTX continues to emphasize synchronized audio-video generation. The separation between "make the clip" and "add sound later" is starting to narrow.

### Enterprise Adoption Is About Workflow Fit

Runway with Bertelsmann, Adobe with Topaz, and Google through Vertex AI all show the same enterprise pattern. The tools need to fit into existing review, production, enhancement, and deployment paths. Output quality matters, but workflow reliability increasingly matters just as much.

---

## Novel Ideas Worth Watching

### Audio-Aware Reference Workflows

Reference-driven generation used to mean images, frames, and style boards. Now audio is becoming another reference layer. A voice track, music cue, or timing bed can shape motion and structure, not only sit underneath the final video.

### Local Enhancement as Production Infrastructure

Topaz's Neurostream technology points to a possible split between cloud generation and local finishing. If enhancement models can run well on local hardware, teams may use cloud systems for heavier generation while using local tools for sharpening, denoising, restoration, or upscaling.

### Model Choice by Production Task

The video market is no longer cleanly ranked from best to worst. One model may lead on audio, another on reference inputs, another on editing control, another on cost, and another on enterprise deployment. The more practical question is becoming: which model is strongest for this specific production task?

## Tip of the Week

### Back to Basics, Week 6: Use Checklists as Guardrails

Last week, the Back to Basics series covered **Ask for a Delta**: after an AI revision, ask the model to list exactly what changed.

This week builds on that with a simple habit: **use checklists as guardrails**.

A checklist is useful because it gives the model a concrete review standard. Instead of saying "make this better," the checklist defines what "better" means for the task.

Use this pattern:

```text
Before you revise, use this checklist:

- Preserve all names, dates, and timecodes.
- Keep technical terms unchanged unless they are clearly wrong.
- Remove repeated ideas.
- Make the wording clearer and shorter.
- Do not add new claims.
- Flag anything uncertain instead of guessing.

After the revision, show:
- Revised version
- Checklist results
- Items that need human review
```

For AV work, checklists are useful with edit notes, equipment summaries, captions, SharePoint copy, scripts, documentation, asset lists, and post-production QA. They help the model separate the writing task from the review task.

The small habit: before asking AI to revise or summarize something, give it a short checklist of what must stay true.
