### August 2: AI-Content Labels Become Enforceable in the EU, With 3%-of-Turnover Fines

The European Commission published its Article 50 guidelines and finalized the Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content on July 20; the EU AI Act's transparency obligations become applicable **August 2**, with fines up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide turnover. Google announced July 24 it's signing the Code — while its own documentation keeps liability with the advertiser.

Two details matter most. The artistic carve-out **does not cover advertising**: a synthetic spokesperson selling a product needs the full label. Assistive editing (retouching, color, cropping) stays exempt. And the EU is one layer of a stack: New York's synthetic-performer disclosure has been live since June 9 ($1,000–$5,000 per violation), India's rules since February, and California's SB 942/AB 853 are synced to the same August 2 date. Google spent the month shipping the plumbing — an AI label setting across five ads products, attestation fields in the Google Ads and DV360 APIs — all of it recording declarations that no system validates and that remain the advertiser's legal problem.
